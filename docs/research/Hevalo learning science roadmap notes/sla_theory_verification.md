- **Verdict rules:**
  - **confirmed**: an independent retrieval matches the claim.
  - **overstated**: the core finding may hold, but a number, label or design inference goes beyond the evidence, or an independent retrieval gives smaller numbers.
  - **refuted**: independent evidence contradicts the claim.
  - **unverified**: no independent corroboration could be found this session. Per the task rules, I gave no benefit of the doubt, even where the claim matches my background knowledge.
- **Many "unverified" verdicts reflect the exhausted search budget, not a suspected error.** Re-run searches for rows 3, 7, 8 and 9 when the budget allows.

## Omitted counter-evidence and caveats

1. **The effect-size labels are inflated by the notes' own yardstick.** Most of the cited "medium" effects are small by the Plonsky & Oswald SLA benchmarks the notes adopt: ER d = 0.46–0.57, captions g = 0.56, chatbots g ≈ 0.6, spacing g = 0.58 immediate. The two cases where a sibling reported bias correction or a CI show how fragile the estimates are: HVPT perception g dropped from 1.02 to 0.71 after publication-bias adjustment (speech_audio_tech.md), and the ASR CI runs from 0.31 to 1.08. Field trials in education with standardised outcomes produce far smaller effects than lab meta-analyses (Kraft 2020, *Educational Researcher*; background, unverified). Hevalo should expect real-world effects well below any number in these notes.
2. **Lab results may not carry over to an app.** Most cited meta-analyses pool short, lab-based studies with researcher-made tests on the exact items taught. Li 2010 itself found lab and short treatments gave larger effects than classroom and longer ones. Design decisions should be validated with Hevalo's own delayed, AI-free and XP-free retention tests (see ai_llm_tutoring.md on Bastani et al. 2025, where unguarded AI raised practice scores but lowered exam scores by 17%).
3. **Spacing and retrieval may not transfer to young children in real lessons.** Goossens et al. found no benefit from spaced or retrieval practice in grades 2–6 classroom vocabulary lessons (children.md). Kids mode should rely on recycling words across stories, songs and games rather than adult-style flashcard SRS.
4. **Interactive add-ons hurt children's story learning.** Takacs, Swart & Bus 2015 (43 studies) found narration, animation and music helped, but hotspots, games and dictionaries distracted, most of all for less-advantaged children (children.md). This limits recs 5–6 (tap-to-gloss and multiple-choice glosses everywhere) in kids mode.
5. **Reading-while-listening only helps when the audio sets the pace.** A meta-analysis of 30 studies (N = 1,945) found RWL beat reading alone by only g = 0.18 overall. The gain appeared when audio paced the reading (g = 0.41), not when reading was self-paced (g = 0.06) (speech_audio_tech.md, *Language Teaching*). Rec 3 should specify **audio-paced, karaoke-style highlighting**, not "audio available".
6. **Translation has direct supporting evidence that the notes missed.** Laufer & Girsai 2008 (*Applied Linguistics*; background, unverified) found that contrastive analysis plus translation tasks beat meaning-focused and non-contrastive form-focused instruction for vocabulary and collocations. The notes say they found "no rigorous evidence" on translation exercises. That is not the same as evidence against them, and rec 29's planned fading of translation has no direct empirical support either way. Keep contrastive L1–Kurdish items for collocations and light-verb constructions.
7. **The case for prompts over recasts is contested.**
   - Goo & Mackey 2013 (*SSLA*) critique the prompts-vs-recasts comparisons (background).
   - Ammar & Spada 2006 found the prompt advantage mainly for lower-proficiency learners (background).
   - Prompts presuppose partial knowledge. For brand-new forms, giving the correct form, with an explanation, is necessary.
   - Elaborated feedback beats showing only the correct response in computer-based learning generally (Van der Kleij et al. 2015; background).
8. **Explicit instruction also improves free production.** Spada & Tomita 2010 reported explicit-instruction benefits on free-production measures (background), which weakens the notes' claim that explicit-knowledge tests inflate the explicit advantage. My unverified recollection of Shintani, Li & Ellis 2013 is that production-based instruction was equal or better for *productive* knowledge at delay. If so, rec 13's "comprehension before production" should be "comprehension *and* production".
9. **The coverage measure needs work before it means much.**
   - The 95% and 98% figures are English, word-family based and for unassisted reading.
   - The lexical-unit debate (word family vs lemma or flemma; Brown et al. 2022; background) matters for beginners.
   - Kurdish inflection and clitics make a lemmatiser a prerequisite for the rec 2 coverage meter.
   - Glossed reading tolerates lower coverage.
   - Interleaving new words hurts: blocking beat interleaving for word materials, g = −0.39 (Brunmair & Richter 2019, memory_retention.md). Mixing confusable forms helps, so "games as review delivery" (rec 10) should interleave confusables, not new items.
10. **Older learners have a general rate advantage.** Muñoz's program and the ACTFL brief show older learners progress faster per hour of instruction. Total hours of exposure matter more than starting age (children.md). The "ER works better for adults" moderator is probably part of this general pattern, not a reason to withhold reading from children.
11. **Learner-to-learner interaction is not the interaction in the meta-analyses.** Mackey & Goo's effects come mostly from interaction with native-speaker or researcher interlocutors who give feedback. Peer chat without feedback training gives fewer and less accurate corrections; Sato & Lyster 2012 showed peers need to be trained to give CF (social_collaborative.md, prior knowledge). Computer-mediated text interaction is roughly as effective as face-to-face (Ziegler 2016, *SSLA*; background), which supports DM-based tasks *if* they contain feedback.
12. **App-efficacy studies share the same weaknesses.**
    - **Survivorship:** they test completers only.
    - **Self-selection:** time-on-app correlations are confounded by motivation.
    - **Vendor authorship:** Jiang 2021, Smith 2024 and Kittredge 2024 are vendor-authored.
    - **Equivalence:** none of the cited studies tests equivalence.
    - **Children:** with elementary pupils, Duolingo and classroom Spanish showed no difference (Rachels & Rockinson-Szapkiw 2018; background), which is equally underpowered.
13. **Kurdish speech and language technology is a precondition for recs 20 and 21, not a detail.**
    - **Speech recognition:** speech_audio_tech.md found no hosted Kurmancî speech-to-text and Soranî only in preview.
    - **Text-to-speech:** no Google or Azure TTS for either variety.
    - **Large language models:** quality for Kurdish is unverified.
    - **Chatbot evidence:** the meta-analyses are mostly short, English-language and university samples, and 1–7-day interventions inflate effects (ai_llm_tutoring.md).

- I used three other kinds of evidence instead:
  1. **Independent retrievals by sibling researchers in this folder.** A sibling line counts as corroboration only if that researcher found it in their own search. Lines tagged `[SIB]` or "via motivation_gamification.md" are copies of these notes and do not count. Most overlapping sibling lines turned out to be copies. Those retrievals were also from search snippets, not full papers.
  2. **Internal consistency:** whether the findings the notes cite actually support the Implications built on them.
  3. **My own background knowledge** (training data up to about mid-2026), labelled **[background, unverified]** every time. It is never used to mark a claim "confirmed". No URLs were invented for it; background items are given only as bibliographic pointers.
- **I did not open any URL in the Sources column.** Each URL is labelled either *(notes)*, meaning the original notes cited it, or *(children.md, independent)*, meaning a sibling found it in its own search.
- **Verdict rules:**
  - **confirmed:** an independent retrieval matches the claim as it is used.
  - **overstated:** the core finding may hold, but a number, label, scope or design inference goes beyond the evidence. This can be shown from the notes' own text or by statistical reasoning, without a new retrieval.
  - **refuted:** independent evidence contradicts the claim.
  - **unverified:** nothing independent corroborated it this session. Per the task rules, these get no benefit of the doubt, even where they match my background knowledge.
- **Many "unverified" verdicts reflect the exhausted search budget, not a suspected error.** The priority re-checks are listed at the end.

## Omitted counter-evidence

All items are [background, unverified] unless marked otherwise, and should be re-checked before being cited in the final report.

1. **Reminders can get in the way of habit formation.** In a smartphone pill-taking study, Stawarz, Cox & Blandford (CHI 2015) found that reminders improved adherence while they were in use but did not build automaticity. Contextual cues tied to an existing routine did. Rec 15's daily reminder could therefore make practice depend on the reminder. Prefer cue-based if-then plans (rec 4) and fade reminders out over time.
2. **A missed day does not break a habit** (Lally et al. 2010). This directly supports forgiving streaks and the weekly-goal mode, and raises the question of whether a *daily* streak is needed at all.
3. **Virtual points have not been shown to undermine intrinsic motivation.** In Mekler et al. 2017 (*Computers in Human Behavior*), points, levels and leaderboards increased the *quantity* of output in a tagging task without lowering intrinsic motivation, but they did not improve *quality* either. That matches Cerasoli's quantity/quality split and argues against treating XP as equivalent to Deci's "tangible rewards".
4. **Specific game elements can raise perceived competence** (Sailer et al. 2017). Badges, leaderboards and performance graphs raised competence need satisfaction. Avatars, a story and teammates raised relatedness. This contradicts the blanket "gamification does little for competence" (row 2).
5. **The overjustification debate is still open.** Cameron & Pierce (1994), Eisenberger & Cameron (1996) and Cameron, Banko & Pierce (2001) argue that undermining is confined to expected tangible rewards for merely *doing* a task, and that rewards for meeting a performance standard can *raise* interest. Both camps agree on one practical rule: rewarding mastery milestones (recs 6 and 7) is safe, and paying for participation (posting, logging in, opening the app) is risky.
6. **Megastudy and vendor "wins" are inflated by selection.** The best of 54 Milkman arms, and the few wins Duolingo reports from 600+ streak experiments, are biased upward and will shrink on replication. Milkman's own abstract (as I recall it) reports that only about 8% of interventions had measurable effects after the programme ended. With Hevalo's user base, lifts below about 1% (such as +0.38% DAU) cannot be detected at all.
7. **Engagement is not learning.** Every Duolingo number in the notes (streaks, leagues, notifications, Streak Revival) is an engagement metric. The notes' Gaps say so, but the Implications quote these numbers as if they were outcomes. Hevalo should treat them as hypotheses and test them against learning metrics (rec 21) [internal-consistency point, not background].
8. **The average gamification effect hides negative results.** Sailer & Homner's motivational and behavioural effects were unstable in rigorous studies. Kurnaz & Koçtürk's I² ≈ 89 means effects of both signs sit inside the average; the notes themselves cite negative results (Hanus & Fox; the 2024 *Learning and Individual Differences* leaderboard study). The L2 mobile-game g = 1.25 and the "45-study d = 0.72" figures should not be used; the notes already flag them as low confidence.
9. **Implementation-intention effects shrink in the field and in the newer meta-analysis** (row 6). A one-screen if-then prompt should be expected to produce small absolute gains.
10. **There is almost no evidence from children for the core mechanics.** Both these notes and children.md found no experimental evidence on streaks, leagues or notifications in children's learning. The kids' recommendations rest on regulation, on a moderator Deci reported only as "tended to" be worse for children, and on harms seen with Snapchat streaks. Snapchat streaks are peer messaging streaks, a socially pressured setting that differs from a solo learning streak.
11. **Age thresholds are inconsistent across recommendations.** Rec 17 uses under-16. The DSA guidelines and the ICO Code use under-18, COPPA uses under-13, and the GDPR Art. 8 consent age is 13–16 by country. Apply the strictest threshold for each mechanic and market.
12. **League viability at small scale should be a design constraint, not just a gap.** Matched weekly leagues need dozens of concurrently active users per tier. With a small Kurdish-learner population, family or class team leagues, or comparisons against a learner's own personal best, may be the only workable versions.

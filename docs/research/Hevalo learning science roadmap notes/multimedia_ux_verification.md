## Omitted counter-evidence

1. **Multimodal-gloss meta-analyses.** Two dedicated meta-analyses came back in the same search the notes relied on (log L2525, title only): Ramezanali et al. on multimodal glossing, and a 2024 multimedia-gloss meta-analysis.
   - [background, unverified] The first suggests text-plus-picture glosses can beat text alone.
   - "Gloss modality doesn't matter" should be "not shown to matter in one meta-regression".
2. **Captions can become a crutch.** [background, unverified] Learners with captions on often read instead of listening, and the gains seen while captions are on need not carry over to listening without them.
   - Rec 4 ("fade text support with level") should be a **required** progression with uncaptioned listening checks, not an optional setting.
   - The absolute caption gain is 3–4.5 percentage points of target words (row 1).
3. **Sleep.**
   - The Schimke 2024 null result was omitted.
   - The meta-analysis covers adults only (row 8).
4. **Small sets shorten spacing.** Nakata & Webb (2016) found that larger sets help because they lengthen within-session spacing. That cuts against "3–5 new items × 5–7 retrievals" unless reviews are interleaved between retrievals (row 7).
5. **Young children vs pop-up glosses.** Takacs found dictionaries and hotspots distract young children, especially disadvantaged ones. Rec 2's "guess-first glosses everywhere" needs an age gate (row 5).
6. **Manipulative design in Hevalo's own features.** Radesky's coding treats accumulating daily rewards, stickers and trophies as "lures", and characters urging continued play as "parasocial pressure" (row 13).
   - The notes use emotional-design evidence to justify a friendly mascot.
   - The same mascot must never plead, guilt or count down, especially in the kids' track.
7. **Notification dependence.** The motivation agent's search result (log L3316) reports **"sharp declines in consistency once reminders cease"** and user fatigue from overly frequent alerts.
   - The source is a ResearchGate paper, "Push Notifications and Habit Formation: Behavioral Impact on Daily Language Practice Consistency"; it is snippet-only and its quality is unknown.
   - Reminders may prop behaviour up rather than build a habit. Test a reminder-fade schedule and measure what happens when reminders stop.
8. **Keyword mnemonics.** [background, unverified] Learner-generated keywords often work worse than well-chosen supplied ones: they take time, and novices pick poor keywords.
   - This cuts against rec 12's "learner-written" hooks.
   - Community upvotes may reward funny hooks over effective ones.
   - A curated default hook per UI language, with user hooks as an option, is safer.
9. **Mayer's lab effect sizes.** [background, unverified] A newer overview of reviews (Noetel et al. 2022, *Review of Educational Research*, a meta-meta-analysis of multimedia design principles) is a better source than Mayer's own lab medians. It reports positive but variable effects of mixed evidence quality. Use it for any external claim about signaling, segmenting or redundancy.
10. **Seductive details vs emotional design.** [background, unverified] Both bodies of evidence come mainly from short adult STEM lessons. Neither tests children's vocabulary drills. Treat "no motion during items" (rec 9) as a hypothesis to A/B-test, not a rule.
11. **Interleaving.** Brunmair & Richter's "word materials" finding concerns category induction, not L2 word–meaning pairs (memory_retention_verification.md row 3). "Interleave confusable letters" is extrapolation, though plausible.
12. **Aspiration varies by dialect.** The speech agent's search (log L1277) says the Kurmanji aspiration contrast is **absent or fading in some dialects** (e.g., Khorasan). Rec 23's "listen and choose" aspiration drills must not mark those speakers wrong. Sibling checks raised this too.
13. **Audio-first for low-literacy adults.** [background, unverified] Adult literacy instruction generally needs explicit decoding (phonics) practice. The audio-first path (rec 41) should be an on-ramp to the literacy course, not a substitute that delays script learning.
14. **Legal floor vs best practice.** In the EU the enforceable floor is WCAG 2.1 via EN 301 549, not 2.2 (rows 11, 18). DSA Art. 28 probably does not bind a micro or small Hevalo (row 10). Recs 21 and 30 are good practice; label them that way rather than as "compliance".

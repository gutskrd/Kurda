# Fact-check: market_landscape.md (2025–2026 app landscape, kids' apps, Kurdish resources, monetization)

**How this check was done. Read this first.**
- **I could not run any new web searches.** My first WebSearch call returned "this turn's web search budget is used up (limit: 200 WebSearch calls per turn, shared by every agent in it)". WebFetch is blocked by the egress proxy. I did not try to get around either limit.
- I used four other kinds of evidence instead:
  1. **The saved raw search log** (`searches_all.txt` in the session scratchpad: 248 logged WebSearch calls by every research and verify agent in this run). Almost every [SIB] item in market_landscape.md was copied from one of these searches, so I read the original search output behind each claim. That let me check two things: whether the notes transcribe the search output faithfully, and whether the output names a **primary source** (an SEC filing, a journal abstract, a regulator page) or only a blog or aggregator. The market_landscape agent's own four searches all failed on the budget limit, so every [MK] item has no retrieval behind it.
  2. **A local copy of the srs-benchmark README** (git object at commit bd9110f, 18 Sep 2026), cloned earlier this session by the memory_retention verifier. I read it with `git show`.
  3. **The Hevalo repository**, read directly wherever a claim or recommendation depends on what Hevalo already has.
  4. **My background knowledge**, labelled **[background, unverified]** every time. It never upgrades a claim to "confirmed", and I invented no URLs for it.
- **I did not open any URL in the Sources column.** Every URL comes from the logged search output. "(log: agent)" names the agent whose search returned it. Everything rests on search-engine summaries, not full pages, unless marked "(read locally)".
- **Verdict rules:**
  - **confirmed**: the logged output attributes the claim to a primary source, or two separate searches returned it, and the notes do not stretch it further than that.
  - **overstated**: the core fact holds, but a number, scope or design inference goes further than the evidence.
  - **refuted**: the evidence contradicts the claim.
  - **unverified**: the only support is model knowledge or a single blog or aggregator snippet. Per the task rules, these get no benefit of the doubt.
- I picked the 12 claims the "Implications for Hevalo" section depends on most. Other claims I checked are listed after the table and are not counted in the tally.

## Omitted counter-evidence

1. **Hevalo already has hearts.** The mobile lesson player gives **5 hearts per lesson**, takes one per wrong answer, and **ends the lesson as a fail at zero** (`mobile/src/lesson/player.ts`, `HeartsBar.tsx`; client-only per hevalo_current_state_backend.md). Rec 19 says "Do not adopt hearts", as if this were a future choice. It should be "remove or soften the existing per-lesson hearts on mobile", for example by never ending a lesson and showing errors as feedback only. Hevalo's hearts are not a paywall, so the notes' monetization critique does not apply to them, but the error-penalty critique does.
2. **FSRS-7 has superseded FSRS-6, and the baselines are humbling.** See row 7. FSRS-6 is worse than a constant predictor on same-day reviews, and a zero-parameter moving average beats it overall. "Best in class" should not appear in the report.
3. **The "speaking and listening gap" is not shown to be app-specific.** Listening below reading also holds for university students (Jiang 2021). Smith 2024 found significant speaking gains. About 69% of Babbel users improved on an oral interview (Loewen 2020). The SSLA 2026 study found app ≈ classroom for beginners. The case for Hevalo's speaking modes should rest on time spent speaking and on learner demand, not on a claimed app deficit.
4. **The market leader is moving value into the free tier.** The Q2 2026 letter summary says Duolingo is "improving the free learner experience to grow word of mouth". Explain My Answer (if confirmed) is the same move, and the shares fell after the Q2 2026 results. This raises the bar for any Hevalo freemium limiter more than the notes acknowledge.
5. **Further child-safety laws in the children agent's searches (not in the notes):**
   - **Texas's App Store Accountability Act** is **in effect** after a 5th Circuit stay on 28 May 2026. App stores must verify age and get parental consent for minors' downloads and purchases, and pass age-band and consent signals to developers. Utah and Louisiana have similar acts. Hevalo would receive these signals and must honour them.
   - **Oregon SB 1546** requires design features that reduce excessive or compulsive use for users known or reasonably presumed to be children. That directly covers streaks and notifications.
   - All of this is search-summary level and needs legal review.
6. **COPPA does not regulate push notifications.** The FTC dropped that proposal. The push-off default for minors comes from the DSA guidelines and ICO guidance, and may not bind a small company (DSA Art. 19 exemption, [background, unverified]). Treat it as a design-ethics choice. Where the law applies, it is also a legal requirement.
7. **Leaderboard harm is concentrated and design-dependent.** The searches behind row 4 report that low-ranked students lose motivation, that relative leaderboards and hiding bottom ranks reduce the harm, and that trait competitiveness changes who benefits. This bears directly on the "Common, optional" verdict for leagues: make leagues opt-in, relative and never shown to children.
8. **The Duolingo numbers that circulate disagree with each other.** SEO and "case study" blogs give leaderboard figures (+25%, +40%, 12%→55%) that do not match Mazal's first-hand account. Use only SEC filings, Duolingo research papers or first-hand accounts.
9. **Duolingo filling the Kurdish gap is a perishable assumption.** It built 148 courses with generative AI in under a year ([MK]). Its old route for minority languages ended with a paid $4M wind-down, not abandonment. The "community-owned platform" argument is strategic, not evidential, and AI-built courses could weaken it quickly.
10. **General AI tutors are unassessed Kurdish competitors.** Talkpal, a competing AI-tutor app, appears in the Kurdish-app search results with Kurdish-learning pages. Whether it or the general assistants (Gemini, ChatGPT voice) offer usable Kurdish conversation was not checked. "No major competitor offers this for Kurdish" (rec 10) is untested.
11. **Engagement is not learning.** Every Duolingo number used in the Implications (streaks, leagues, revival, notifications, HLR's +12%) is an engagement metric from vendor A/B tests or retrospectives. Rec 25 says to judge on learning outcomes. Recs 1, 5 and the feature table still cite engagement wins as if they were outcomes.

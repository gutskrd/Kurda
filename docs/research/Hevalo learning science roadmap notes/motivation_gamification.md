## 1. Self-determination theory and the overjustification (crowding-out) effect: what do the meta-analyses say?

### Takeaway
Expected, tangible, task-contingent rewards reliably lower later free-choice intrinsic motivation, and the effect is worse for children. Informational positive feedback raises it. Intrinsic motivation predicts the *quality* of performance, while incentives predict its *quantity*. In education, gamification mostly boosts extrinsic motivation and perceived autonomy and relatedness, and does little for perceived competence. So points, coins and badges should carry information about competence, not act as payment for doing lessons.

### Cited Findings
- **[MA] Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999** (128 experiments; older but still the reference point). Engagement-contingent, completion-contingent and performance-contingent rewards lowered free-choice intrinsic motivation by d = −0.40, −0.36 and −0.28. Engagement- and completion-contingent rewards also lowered self-reported interest (d = −0.15, −0.17), as did all tangible and all expected rewards. — [Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999 (PDF)](https://home.ubalt.edu/tmitch/642/articles%20syllabus/Deci%20Koestner%20Ryan%20meta%20IM%20psy%20bull%2099.pdf); [Semantic Scholar record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/A-meta-analytic-review-of-experiments-examining-the-Deci-Koestner/8ad9801baea65b40fbbe6fc56e34b2b7be47d0ba)
- **[MA] Same meta-analysis.** Positive (informational) feedback *raised* free-choice behaviour (d = +0.33) and self-reported interest (d = +0.31). "Tangible rewards tended to be more detrimental for children than college students, and verbal rewards tended to be less enhancing for children." — [Deci et al. 1999 (U. Washington copy)](https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf); [Leeds/Colorado copy](https://leeds-faculty.colorado.edu/dahe7472/deci%201999.pdf)
- **Contested:** Eisenberger, Pierce & Cameron published a formal rebuttal ("uses and abuses of meta-analysis"), arguing the undermining effect is narrower. The debate was not fully settled, but the SDT position remains mainstream. — [PubMed 10589298](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10589298); Deci et al.'s education-focused follow-up reply: [Deci, Koestner & Ryan 2001, RER](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543071001001)
- **[MA] Cerasoli, Nicklin & Ford 2014** (40 years, 183 samples, N = 212,468). Intrinsic motivation is a medium-to-strong predictor of performance. Incentives *directly* tied to performance went with lower intrinsic motivation, while *indirectly* salient incentives went with higher intrinsic motivation. Per the published abstract (not shown in the snippet), intrinsic motivation better predicted the *quality* of performance and incentives better predicted its *quantity*. — [Cerasoli et al. 2014 (PDF, selfdeterminationtheory.org)](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/2014_Cerasoli_Intrinsic.pdf); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Intrinsic-motivation-and-extrinsic-incentives-a-Cerasoli-Nicklin/41977de33d27ef015a5c6780964bbfa721a6341a)
- **[MA] Li, Hew & Du 2024** (ETR&D; 35 interventions, ~2,500 participants). Gamification had a small positive effect on intrinsic motivation (Hedges' g = 0.257). It raised perceived **autonomy and relatedness** but had **minimal impact on perceived competence**, and it affected extrinsic more than intrinsic motivation. — [Springer, ETR&D 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-023-10337-7)
- **[MA] Kurnaz & Koçtürk 2025** (Psychology in the Schools; K-12; 31 studies, 41 effect sizes). Pooled effect on motivation g = 0.654 (95% CI 0.442–0.866), with very high heterogeneity (I² = 88.9). The effect was larger on extrinsic motivation (g = 0.713) than intrinsic (g = 0.638). It was largest for secondary students (g = 1.015), then high school (g = 0.821), and **smallest for primary school children (g = 0.309)**. — [Wiley, Psychology in the Schools 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/pits.70056); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394050015_A_Meta-Analysis_of_Gamification's_Impact_on_Student_Motivation_in_K-12_Education)

### Inferences
- Paying Zêr or XP for *completing* or *engaging in* activities is the reward type with the most negative effect sizes in Deci et al. (engagement-contingent d = −0.40), and the effect is worse for children. That matters most for activities learners already enjoy, such as writing poems and stories or playing word games.
- What helps intrinsic motivation is feedback that tells learners they are becoming competent (d ≈ +0.3). Gamification rarely provides this (Li et al. 2024), so Hevalo's main motivational gap to close is likely *competence signalling*, not more points.
- The Deci et al. abstract reports undermining by *expected* rewards. By inference, surprise rewards ("bonus chest for your 10th story") are less risky than announced pay-per-task schemes. This is an inference from the abstract's wording, not a separately reported effect size in the snippet.

### Gaps
- No meta-analysis found that tests overjustification *inside* commercial language apps. The lab and classroom evidence is being extrapolated.
- The Cerasoli quality-versus-quantity split and the Deci child/adult moderator come from the published abstracts. I could not read the full papers to report subgroup effect sizes.

## 2. Gamification meta-analyses: learning outcomes and which elements matter

### Takeaway
Gamification gives small-to-medium average gains: cognitive g ≈ 0.49, motivational g ≈ 0.36, behavioural g ≈ 0.25 (Sailer & Homner 2020). Only the cognitive effect survives restriction to rigorous studies. Narrative ("game fiction") and *competition combined with collaboration* are the moderators that matter. Points, badges and leaderboards alone ("PBL") are the weakest version. Effects are heterogeneous, prone to novelty decay, and often inflated in small, short L2 vocabulary studies.

### Cited Findings
- **[MA] Sailer & Homner 2020** (Educational Psychology Review; studies mostly from before 2018, so "older" but still the most cited). Random-effects estimates: cognitive g = 0.49 (95% CI 0.30–0.69; k = 19, N = 1,686), motivational g = 0.36 (0.18–0.54; k = 16, N = 2,246), behavioural g = 0.25 (0.04–0.46; k = 9, N = 951). The cognitive effect held in high-rigour studies, but the motivational and behavioural effects were "less stable". — [ResearchGate PDF](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/335189630_The_Gamification_of_Learning_a_Meta-analysis); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/The-Gamification-of-Learning:-a-Meta-analysis-Sailer-Homner/be6769b967370c9852210e2fb7a34e499902f814)
- **[MA] Same paper, moderators.** Game fiction (narrative) and social interaction significantly moderated behavioural outcomes, with "inclusion of game fiction and combining competition with collaboration" particularly effective. A subsplit suggested competition plus collaboration may also help motivational outcomes. The authors recommend letting learners "work together in teams while competing with other teams". — [d-nb.info full text](https://d-nb.info/1202307655/34)
- **[Secondary interpretation, not from the paper itself]** A blog summary claims reward-and-status mechanics alone were "far less effective" than designs with challenge, meaningful goals and narrative. This is consistent with the moderators above, but the wording comes from the blog. — [cogn-iq.org blog](https://www.cogn-iq.org/blog/gamified-learning/)
- **[MA] Bai, Hew & Huang 2020** (Educational Research Review). Overall effect of gamification on academic achievement g = 0.504. Sample size and intervention duration were significant moderators. Financial rewards and subject were also examined. — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Does-gamification-improve-student-learning-outcome-Bai-Hew/4baea6e6ca74597feaa6ce691c41577a67e87b54); [EdUHK repository](https://repository.eduhk.hk/en/publications/does-gamification-improve-student-learning-outcome-evidence-from-/)
- **[MA, L2 specific] Mobile games and language learning** (CALL journal, 2025). The search summary reports large positive effects for vocabulary (g = 1.251) and stronger effects for L2 than L1 (g = 1.123). It was not clear from the snippet that these figures belong to this exact paper, so treat them as **low confidence**. — [Taylor & Francis, CALL 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09588221.2025.2528786)
- **[Low confidence]** A PMC article on "AI-driven gamification" cites a "meta-analysis of 45 studies, 2019–2024" with d = 0.72 for vocabulary and d = 0.64 for persistence. I could not identify the underlying meta-analysis, so treat it as unverified. — [PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13006607/)
- **[OBS]** In mobile-assisted EFL vocabulary learning, gamified behavioural engagement correlated with vocabulary achievement (r = 0.49) and mediated the link from ideal L2 self to achievement. — [PMC 2026 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13254086/)
- **[QE, longitudinal] Novelty effect.** Rodrigues et al. 2022 (IJETHE) found that gamification's effect declined after about 4 weeks, then trended upward again between weeks 6 and 10. They called this a "familiarization effect" following the novelty effect. — [Springer, IJETHE 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41239-021-00314-6); [ERIC EJ1325797](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1325797)
- **[QE, longitudinal] Hanus & Fox 2015** (older). Over 16 weeks with 80 students, a course gamified with badges and a leaderboard produced *less* motivation, satisfaction and empowerment over time than a non-gamified course, mediated by lower intrinsic motivation. The original abstract also reports lower final exam scores; that detail is not in the snippet. — [Academia.edu copy](https://www.academia.edu/12454798/Assessing_the_Effects_of_Gamification_in_the_Classroom_A_Longitudinal_Study_on_Intrinsic_Motivation_Social_Comparison_Satisfaction_Effort_and_Academic_Performance)
- **Conflicting meta-analytic findings on duration.** One meta-analysis reports large effects for very short interventions and *negative* effects for 1–2-year interventions. Another (and Gyedu et al. 2026 in higher education) reports larger effects for interventions longer than about 12 weeks or one semester. A secondary summary concludes the durability of gains is "unresolved and design-dependent". This rests on a blog summary plus snippet, not on reading the papers. — [cogn-iq.org blog](https://www.cogn-iq.org/blog/gamified-learning/); [Gyedu et al. 2026, SAGE Open](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/21582440261421375)
- **[MA exists, no figures retrieved]** A 2024 meta-analysis on gamification in *collaborative* learning exists (J. Computers in Education). — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40692-024-00349-4)

### Inferences
- Hevalo's games (Wordle, rhyme rounds, typing race, quiz battles) count as *game-based learning*, where the activity itself is the game. That is a different thing from *gamification*, where points are bolted onto drills. The meta-analytic moderators favour narrative and team-based social play over leaderboards.
- Primary-school children show the smallest motivational gain (g = 0.31) and the largest undermining risk from tangible rewards (section 1). For children, Hevalo should rely on intrinsically fun games and story or character narrative rather than on Zêr and XP economies.
- Expect a novelty dip at about 4–6 weeks after any new mechanic. Measure features at 8–12 weeks, not at launch.

### Gaps
- No meta-analysis isolates individual elements (points vs badges vs leaderboards vs streaks) with clean effect sizes. Most studies bundle them.
- There is no peer-reviewed evidence on gamification for heritage learners or for minority or less-resourced languages such as Kurdish.

## 3. Streaks, loss aversion and habit formation

### Takeaway
Streaks raise short-term return rates. Duolingo's own data, from more than 600 experiments, shows that *forgiving* streaks (freezes, a minimal daily requirement, repair) increase engagement. Lab research shows a *broken* streak demotivates, and more so when the user blames themselves, unless the streak can be repaired. Habits take about two months on median and vary widely (4–335 days). If-then planning (d = 0.65) and "comeback" rewards after a miss (+27% in a 61k-person megastudy) are among the best-evidenced add-ons.

### Cited Findings
- **[MA] Habit timing.** Lally et al. 2010 (older, naturalistic): median 66 days to reach peak automaticity, range 18–254. Singh et al. 2024 systematic review (20 studies, 2,601 participants): median times of 59–66 days, means of 106–154 days, and individual variation of 4–335 days. This supersedes the popular "21 days" myth. — [Singh et al. 2024 PDF (Healthcare)](https://www.newswise.com/pdf_docs/173752351874824_healthcare-12-02488.pdf); [ScienceDaily summary](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2025/01/250124151347.htm); [Lally et al. 2010, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/32898894_How_are_habits_formed_Modeling_habit_formation_in_the_real_world)
- **[MA] Implementation intentions.** Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 (94 tests, 63 reports; older but robust) found if-then plans of the form "If situation Y, then I will do X" improved goal attainment by d = 0.65. They helped with getting started, shielding an activity from interference, and disengaging from failing routes. Source is a secondary summary that quotes the paper. — [goalsandprogress.com summary](https://goalsandprogress.com/implementation-intentions-gollwitzer-how-to/); [Gollwitzer & Oettingen chapter (PDF)](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/sites/default/files/Gollwitzer_Oettingen_11_Planning_Goal_Striving.pdf)
- **[QE/RCT, consumer research] Silverman & Barasch 2023**, "On or Off Track: How (Broken) Streaks Affect Consumer Decisions" (7 studies). Showing an *intact* streak in a behaviour log increases later engagement compared with showing a *broken* one, and this holds regardless of actual past behaviour; only the representation matters. People adopt streak maintenance as a goal in itself. The effect is amplified when the break is attributed to oneself and attenuated when the streak can be "repaired". — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/On-or-Off-Track:-How-(Broken)-Streaks-Affect-Silverman-Barasch/edfc39fd411eec3313d9a70bdf87acbd88d67bfc); [CU Boulder summary](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions); [Psychology Today summary](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/ulterior-motives/202306/how-broken-streaks-sap-motivation)
  - An unverified claim that "broken long streaks suppress engagement below baseline" appears only in a search summary and a gamification-consultant blog. — [yukaichou.com](https://yukaichou.com/gamification-analysis/streak-design-gamification-motivation-burnout/)
- **[RCT, field megastudy] Milkman et al. 2021, Nature.** 61,293 gym members took part in 54 four-week interventions; 45% of interventions significantly raised weekly visits, by 9–27%. The best performer gave a small bonus (125 points, about $0.09) for **returning after a missed workout**, which raised exercise by 27%. — [Penn Today](https://penntoday.upenn.edu/news/wharton-study-best-ways-boost-workout-habits); [Gwern-hosted PDF](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/statistics/prediction/2021-milkman.pdf)
- **[VENDOR] Duolingo streak data**, from the blog, product talks and investor letters:
  - Learners who reach a 7-day streak are "3.6x more likely" to stay engaged long-term. Some secondary sources instead say "complete their course", and another says "2.4x more likely to continue the next day". The phrasing varies, so cite cautiously. — [Duolingo blog: how the streak builds habit](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit); [Duolingo blog: improving the streak](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak); [Duolingo blog: how streaks keep learners committed](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals)
  - Letting learners equip **two streak freezes** instead of one raised daily active learners by **0.38%**. Separating the streak from the full daily goal, so that one lesson keeps the streak, raised the number of learners on 7+ day streaks by **over 40%**. The "Streak Wager" raised D1, D7 and D14 retention, with **D7 up 14%**. Duolingo ran **600+ streak experiments over about 4 years**. These figures come via secondary summaries of Duolingo talks (Jackson Shuttleworth on Lenny's Podcast) and blogs, not directly from Duolingo pages. — [Recall summary of Lenny's Podcast](https://www.getrecall.ai/summary/lennys-podcast/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks-or-jackson-shuttleworth-group-pm-retention-team); [Elsewhere/Linear Capital](https://elsewhere.news/en/linearcapital/duolingobolt); [strivecloud](https://www.strivecloud.io/blog/gamification-examples-boost-user-retention-duolingo)
  - As of 31 December 2025, about **15 million DAUs had a 365+ day streak** and about **43 million DAUs had a 7+ day streak**. About **one-third of DAUs have a Friend Streak**. — [Duolingo FY2025 10-K / ARS](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828026012494/duol-20251231.htm); [Duolingo FY2025 annual report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828026025737/duolingodecember312025an.htm)
  - **Streak Revival (June 2026):** a one-time opt-in event let learners restore their longest-ever streak by completing three lessons. 15.4M learners revived a streak, including about 8M who had no active streak at the start. DAUs were 56.5M in Q1 2026 (+21% YoY) and 58.7M in Q2 2026 (+23% YoY). Duolingo says 2026 strategy prioritises "teaching better" and user growth. — [Duolingo Q2 2026 shareholder letter (SEC)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026053299/q2fy26duolingo6-30x26share.htm); [Q2 2026 letter (IR)](https://investors.duolingo.com/static-files/3c8277ee-bc94-4f5d-9b77-0db3e46f88b8); [Q1 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828026029790/q1fy26duolingo3-31x26share.htm)
- **[OBS] Harms of streaks.** A study of early adolescents links Snapchat streaks to problematic smartphone use and fear of missing out. Commentary describes peer pressure to keep streaks and "digital stress". — [ScienceDirect 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772503023000476). A parent-facing guide describes children doing "performative learning" for the streak number. This is opinion, not research. — [Screenwise guide](https://screenwiseapp.com/guides/duolingo-streaks-and-anxiety-in-kids)
- **[QUAL]** Mogavi et al. (L@S 2022) analysed 9 years of Duolingo forum posts plus 15 interviews. They describe "gamification misuse": learners fixate on streaks and XP, take shortcuts and are distracted from learning. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/abs/10.1145/3491140.3528274); [arXiv 2203.16175](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.16175)

### Inferences
- Streaks work through loss aversion and goal adoption, which makes them strong for adults and risky for children. The evidence-based design is a *forgiving* streak: a minimal requirement, freezes earned by learning, a repair window and comeback bonuses. In Duolingo's data, every loosening of the streak *increased* engagement.
- A broken streak should be framed as external and repairable ("life happens; repair it with two reviews"), never as personal failure.
- A ~2-month median habit window, with huge variance, means reminders and support should last at least 8–10 weeks, and a "day 66" milestone is better grounded than "day 21".

### Gaps
- There are no peer-reviewed RCTs on streaks *in language apps* with learning (not engagement) outcomes. Duolingo's figures are engagement metrics and vendor claims.
- I found no evidence on whether weekly streaks (e.g., 4 of 7 days) beat daily streaks for retention of learning.
- Whether Milkman-style effects persisted after the 4-week programme ended could not be confirmed from the snippets.

## 4. Leaderboards and leagues: low vs high performers, social comparison, dropout

### Takeaway
Duolingo reports that weekly leagues matched on prior-week engagement raised total learning time by 17% and tripled its most engaged users (vendor claim). Classroom and lab studies show leaderboards help high-ranked and competitive learners but stress and demotivate low-ranked ones. In one longitudinal quasi-experiment they did not increase optional practice and lowered exam scores. Leagues also invite XP gaming. Design choices (small matched cohorts, opt-out, team play) decide whether they help or harm.

### Cited Findings
- **[VENDOR, ex-CPO account] Jorge Mazal**, "How Duolingo reignited user growth" (Lenny's Newsletter, Feb 2023, covering about 2018–2022). Leaderboards were the retention team's "first breakthrough". Overall learning time rose **17%** and "highly engaged learners" (≥1 h/day, 5 days/week) **tripled**. D1 and D7 retention improved significantly. Users were matched to others with *similar engagement in the prior week*, auto opted-in, and the design was kept casual. Leaderboards, notifications and streak work together drove about 4.5x DAU growth over 4 years, with "Current User Retention Rate" (CURR) as the focus metric. — [Lenny's Newsletter (Mazal)](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth); [Bickerton Substack summary](https://jamesbickerton.substack.com/p/duolingo-leaderboards-and-why-your)
  - **Unreliable aggregator figures to avoid:** claims that leagues raised lesson completion 25%, that users in leagues do "40% more lessons", or that retention rose "from 12% to 55%" appear only on SEO and gamification-vendor blogs with no primary source. — [orizon.co](https://www.orizon.co/blog/duolingos-gamification-secrets); [openloyalty](https://www.openloyalty.io/insider/leaderboard-gamification-tactics)
- **[QE, longitudinal]** In a 2024 quasi-experiment (Learning and Individual Differences), leaderboards did not raise optional practice participation and had a negative effect on exam scores. Public rankings may signal that course efforts are undervalued. — [ScienceDirect 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1041608024001651)
- **[RCT/QE] Trait competitiveness moderates.** A Computers & Education 2024 study found high ranks helped the motivation of *low*-competitiveness learners, while low ranks had more positive effects on the performance of *high*-competitiveness learners. One leaderboard does not suit everyone. — [ScienceDirect, C&E 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360131524002100)
- **[OBS/QE]** Leaderboard rank correlated with better performance among higher performers, while lower-ranked students reported lower motivation and more stress. — [JOTSE](https://www.jotse.org/index.php/jotse/article/view/3321/1020); [MDPI Sustainability 2021: "Leaderboard positions and stress"](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/13/12/6608)
- **[MA/SR]** A 2024 systematic review of leaderboards in higher education found mixed results that depended on design and learner characteristics. Detailed figures were not retrieved. — [Li et al. 2024, JCAL](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jcal.13077?af=R)
- **[QUAL]** Duolingo users perceive league rankings as unfair. Some game XP, in one case with a paid hacking tool after an XP-policy change. — [Mogavi et al. 2022, arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.16175)
- **[MA]** Competition combined with collaboration (team vs team) was the most effective social configuration for behavioural outcomes (see section 2). — [Sailer & Homner 2020](https://d-nb.info/1202307655/34)

### Inferences
- Duolingo's leagues likely work because cohorts are matched on engagement, so most people have a realistic chance. A single global ranking would mostly show low performers that they are losing.
- Because league XP is the currency of competition, XP must be hard to farm. Otherwise leagues reward grinding easy content, which the Mogavi study documents as "misuse".
- For children and anxious or low-confidence learners (e.g., Kurds whose schooling was not in Kurdish and who may feel shame about their language), competitive public rankings carry a real risk of demotivation.

### Gaps
- There is no peer-reviewed independent study of Duolingo-style *matched weekly leagues* with learning outcomes, and no data on how leagues affect *dropout* among bottom-ranked users.
- There is no evidence specific to small user bases. With few concurrent learners, a matched league may contain inactive or bot-like opponents, which is untested.

## 5. Dark patterns, children, ethics and regulation; L2 motivation theory and heritage identity

### Takeaway
Manipulative design is widespread in children's apps; only 20% of apps used by 3–5-year-olds had none. European regulators now explicitly list *streaks*, *push notifications* and *virtual currencies* as risk features for minors. The relevant instruments are the DSA Article 28 guidelines (July 2025), the UK Children's Code, the CPC virtual-currency principles (March 2025), and the Digital Fairness Act expected in Q4 2026. On the motivation-theory side, the "ideal L2 self" strongly predicts *intended effort* (r = .61) but only weakly predicts measured achievement (r = .20, about .10 after bias correction). Identity and vision work therefore has to be paired with concrete behavioural supports. Heritage learners are motivated by family and identity.

### Cited Findings
**Children and manipulative design**
- **[OBS] Radesky et al. 2022, JAMA Network Open.** Apps used by 160 children aged 3–5 were analysed. Only 20% had no manipulative design. Features found were parasocial relationship pressure from characters (about 19–25% of apps with characters), fabricated time pressure (about 11–17%), navigation constraints (about 37–46%), and attractive lures (about 45–46%). The aims were prolonging play (65% of apps), encouraging purchases (56%) and ad viewing. Children from lower-SES families used apps with *more* manipulative design. — [JAMA Netw Open 2022](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2793493); [PMC9206186](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9206186/)
- **[REG] EU DSA Article 28 Guidelines on protection of minors** (European Commission, 14 July 2025). The guidelines use a risk-based, safety-by-design framework. They recommend disabling *by default for minors* features that contribute to excessive use, naming communication or messaging **streaks**, ephemeral content, read receipts, autoplay and **push notifications**. They also call for avoiding persuasive design and dark patterns that encourage excessive use, and for guardian tools that "promote communication and empowerment rather than control". — [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors); [Freshfields analysis](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/dsa-decoded-6-the-european-commission-finalises-guidelines-on-the-protection-of-102kv4s); [Georgetown KGI](https://kgi.georgetown.edu/research-and-commentary/europe-unveils-new-evidence-based-guidelines-to-advance-safer-platform-design-for-minors/)
- **[REG] UK ICO Age Appropriate Design Code** ("Children's Code", standard on nudge techniques). It prohibits nudges that extend children's use or weaken privacy, citing "reward loops or positive reinforcement techniques (such as likes and streaks)", and encourages nudges towards wellbeing such as taking breaks. Note: the snippets quote the 2019 draft. The final code took effect in 2021 and retains a nudge-techniques standard, but I could not verify its final wording here. — [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/ico-bans-use-of-nudge-techniques-in-new-draft-guidelines/); [Bristows briefing (PDF)](https://www.bristows.com/app/uploads/2019/06/Information-Commissioner-consults-on-newly-published-rules-to-protect-children-online.pdf)
- **[REG] EU CPC Network Key Principles on in-game virtual currencies** (March 2025; seven principles; not legally binding but signal how enforcers read EU consumer law). They require clear pricing in real money and pre-contractual information. They prohibit hiding the real cost of digital items or forcing currency purchases, require respect for the right of withdrawal, and require respect for vulnerabilities, especially children's, through parental controls and consumer-friendly defaults. Coordinated investigations of game companies followed in 2025. — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2025/03/26/eu-bodies-act-against-gaming-companies-to-protect-children-from-in-game-purchase-102k658); [Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/european-consumer-protection-network-issues-new-key-principles-on-in-game-virtual-currencies-impact-for-gaming-and-gambling-entities-in-belgium-the-eu-and-beyond/); [European Commission news, June 2025](https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/european-commission-hosts-stakeholders-talks-application-cpc-networks-key-principles-games-virtual-2025-06-03_en)
- **[REG, pending] EU Digital Fairness Act.** The Commission proposal is expected in **Q4 2026**, with negotiations into 2027 and enforcement likely not before about 2028. It is expected to target dark patterns, **addictive design (streaks, variable rewards, constant notifications)**, and opaque virtual currencies, with minors treated as a vulnerable group and possible default-off or prohibition of some features in children's games. As of the sources found, the proposal had not yet been published, so its content is still projected. — [European Parliament Legislative Train](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-protecting-our-democracy-upholding-our-values/file-digital-fairness-act); [KVDL](https://kvdl.com/en/articles/the-digital-fairness-act-in-sight); [CADE](https://cadeproject.org/updates/eu-weighs-stricter-rules-for-addictive-gaming-features/)
- **[REG/EXP] UNICEF RITEC** (with the LEGO Group). The RITEC-8 wellbeing framework was built with 787 children, mostly aged 8–12, in 18 countries. A RITEC Design Toolbox for game designers followed in November 2024 and covers emotional regulation, connection and wellbeing-by-design. — [UNICEF Innocenti RITEC](https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/projects/responsible-innovation-technology-children); [UNICEF press release](https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/press-releases/unicef-unveils-design-toolkit-digital-creators)

**L2 motivation and heritage identity**
- **[MA] Al-Hoorie 2018**, L2 Motivational Self System (32 reports, 39 samples, N = 32,078). The ideal L2 self predicted *intended effort* strongly (r = .61) and *objective achievement* weakly (r = .20, dropping to about .10 and non-significant after publication-bias adjustment). Intended effort did not significantly predict achievement (r = .116). Part of the L2-experience–effort correlation is a wording-overlap artefact. — [ERIC full text](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1202469.pdf); [SSLLT](https://pressto.amu.edu.pl/index.php/ssllt/article/view/12295)
- **[EXP/theory]** Critics argue the field should "look beyond" L2MSS. — [Cambridge, SSLA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/looking-beyond-the-l2-motivational-self-system/D315DA1A154FAE0BB3B154A15878FFDD)
- **[OBS/mixed methods] Heritage learners.** In a Chinese heritage-language study (2024), the ideal L2 self was the strongest predictor of intended effort, via positive attitudes. Identity was built around family and culture, and family language use was positively associated with heritage competence. — [PMC11554449](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11554449/). Portuguese heritage learners cite communicating with family as a core motive — [Language Awareness](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09658416.2021.2000996). A 2025 paper asks "Is heritage attachment enough?"; its findings were not retrieved — [ERIC EJ1480010](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1480010). Heritage identity also predicts Indigenous-language learning motivation in Taiwan — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/377685805_Heritage_identity_and_Indigenous_language_learning_motivation_A_case_of_Indigenous_Taiwanese_high_school_students)

### Inferences
- If Hevalo has a public community feed, DMs and many child users in the EU, it is plausibly an "online platform" under the DSA. However, Article 19 DSA exempts micro and small enterprises from most Section 3 platform obligations. This comes from my background knowledge and was not verified in this session, so legal review is needed. Whatever the legal status, the guidelines describe where enforcement and app-store expectations are heading, and the Digital Fairness Act may apply more broadly.
- Zêr, with a shop and cosmetics, falls into the virtual-currency category regulators scrutinise. This applies especially if Zêr can ever be bought with money or if randomised rewards are introduced.
- For heritage learners, identity and family give strong *effort intentions*. Al-Hoorie suggests these intentions do not turn into achievement by themselves, so Hevalo should convert them into scheduled, tracked, family-linked practice.

### Gaps
- I found no specific research on Kurdish heritage-language motivation, Kurdish diaspora learners, or learners whose own schooling was denied in Kurdish. The latter likely involves language shame, trauma or politicisation; this is an inference that needs Kurdish-specific sources.
- OECD-specific guidance on persuasive design for children was not retrieved in this session.
- There is no causal evidence that any particular "ethical" redesign keeps learning outcomes the same.

## 6. Goal setting, progress visibility, competence feedback (can-do statements) and what predicts long-term retention

### Takeaway
Monitoring progress improves goal attainment (d = 0.40), more so when the progress is recorded and shared. If-then plans add d ≈ 0.65. Formative self-assessment against can-do descriptors raises L2 self-efficacy (small studies). In app-based language learning, attrition is predicted by low perseverance (grit) and by age. Pauses are normal and do not mean dropout. Users who resume after breaks persist, so designing for return beats designing for never-miss.

### Cited Findings
- **[MA] Harkin et al. 2016**, Psychological Bulletin (138 studies, N = 19,951). Interventions increased progress monitoring (d = 1.98) and goal attainment (d = 0.40, 95% CI 0.32–0.48). Monitoring frequency mediated the effect, which was larger when progress was **reported or made public** and when it was **physically recorded**. Most studies were health-related. — [White Rose eprints](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/91437/); [Science for Work summary](https://scienceforwork.com/blog/goal-monitoring/)
- **[REG/EXP] NCSSFL-ACTFL Can-Do Statements** are self-assessment "I can…" descriptors meant to build self-regulation and self-efficacy and to set target proficiency goals. — [ACTFL](https://www.actfl.org/educator-resources/ncssfl-actfl-can-do-statements); [ERIC EJ1080253](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1080253.pdf)
- **[QE, small]** Regular formative self-assessment raised EFL learners' self-efficacy compared with controls. — [TESL Canada Journal 2013, ERIC EJ1033761](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1033761.pdf). For a broader review of L2 self-assessment validity and use, see [Cambridge, Language Teaching](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-teaching/article/selfassessment-in-second-language-learning/00D73BD54F6227D484297EEAB8F51BC0)
- **[OBS, longitudinal] Attrition on Duolingo** (Learning and Individual Differences, 2025). 601 beginners of Spanish or French were followed for 6 months. The best model of attrition kept two predictors: **L2 grit (perseverance of effort)** and **age**. — [ScienceDirect 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1041608025000809)
- **[OBS, survival analysis] SSLA 2024**, non-conventional adult L2 learners. High app acceptance went with *more frequent pauses* but also longer active use, shorter breaks before returning and fewer dropouts. Persistent learners paused more often. — [Cambridge SSLA 2024](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/acceptance-and-engagement-patterns-of-mobileassisted-language-learning-among-nonconventional-adult-l2-learners-a-survival-analysis/C1186B329F808A11DDF2C748E77FD1EE)
- **[QUAL/thesis]** Factors in language-app attrition include reduced motivation, social isolation, anxiety, lack of success, inability to self-regulate, lack of guidance, inauthentic content, poor instructional quality and technical issues. — [McGill thesis on Duolingo attrition](https://escholarship.mcgill.ca/downloads/k643b581s?locale=en); [Loewen et al. 2019 Duolingo case study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333431833_Mobile-assisted_language_learning_A_Duolingo_case_study)
- **[RCT, ML deployment] Notifications.** Yancey & Settles (KDD 2020) describe Duolingo's "sleeping, recovering" bandit for practice reminders. Template novelty wears off with repetition and recovers with rest. Against a strong baseline it gave **+0.5% DAU** and **+2% new-user retention**, using 200M notifications in its replication data. — [Duolingo Research PDF](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf); [KDD 2020](https://www.kdd.org/kdd2020/accepted-papers/view/a-sleeping-recovering-bandit-algorithm-for-optimizing-recurring-notificatio.html)
- **[RCT, micro-randomized, mHealth]** A tailored push notification raised the probability of engaging within 24 h by only about **3.9 percentage points** (modest). — [Bidargaddi et al. 2018, JMIR mHealth](https://doi.org/10.2196/10123). In an exploratory trial, daily and "intelligent" schedules got more notification views than occasional ones, with no difference between intelligent and fixed-daily schedules. — [Morrison et al. 2017, PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0169162). Habituation to repeated prompts is a documented concern in the same literature. One low-quality preprint on language practice reports sharp drops in consistency once reminders stop. — [ResearchGate preprint](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/401241990_Push_Notifications_and_Habit_Formation_Behavioral_Impact_on_Daily_Language_Practice_Consistency)

### Inferences
- Visible, recorded progress against *real abilities* is the best-evidenced, low-harm motivator in this section, and it directly fills the "competence gap" gamification leaves (section 1). Examples: "I can introduce my family in Kurmancî", "I can read Soranî script signs".
- Notifications are a small but real lever: per-message effects are a few percentage points, and Duolingo's whole optimisation added +0.5% DAU. They decay with repetition, so a few well-timed, rotated messages beat many.
- Since pauses are normal and the return is what matters, re-entry design (comeback review, a repaired streak, no shame) is likely as important as daily-habit design.

### Gaps
- No public churn study compares apps with and without specific mechanics on *learning outcomes*.
- I found no evidence on retention drivers for multi-script learners (Latin and Arabic-based scripts) or for very small language communities.
- Duolingo's internal CURR figures after 2023 are not public in detail.

## Implications for Hevalo

These are concrete design implications drawn from the evidence above. Each cites the section it rests on. The codebase was not inspected, so they are phrased as targets to check against the current implementation.

**Streaks (sections 3 and 5)**
1. **Minimal streak requirement.** One completed lesson, review session, alphabet drill or game round with real answers keeps the streak. Keep it separate from a configurable daily XP goal. Duolingo's separation gave +40% learners on 7+ day streaks (vendor).
2. **Forgiving streak by default.** Up to 2 equipped streak freezes, *earned through learning* (e.g., a freeze for every 5 review sessions) and never bought with real money. Offer a **48-hour repair window** ("do 2 extra reviews to repair"), since repairability blunts broken-streak demotivation (Silverman & Barasch). Add an optional **weekly-goal mode** (e.g., 4 of 7 days) for people who cannot practise daily.
3. **Comeback bonus instead of a loss message.** After a missed day, offer a bonus for returning (the Milkman megastudy's best intervention, +27%). Frame the break as external ("Life happens. Welcome back, heval!"). Always show **longest streak** and **total days learned** next to the current streak so a break does not wipe out a learner's sense of progress.
4. **Habit onboarding.** Ask for an if-then plan (implementation intention, d = 0.65), e.g., "After [dinner / the school run / my commute], I'll do one Hevalo lesson", and schedule the reminder at that time. Tell learners habits take about 2 months with wide variance. Use milestones at days 7, 30 and 66, not 21.
5. **Revival events.** A once-a-year, opt-in "restore your longest streak by doing 3 lessons" event could recover lapsed users; Duolingo revived 15.4M streaks this way in June 2026. Newroz (21 March) is a natural culturally anchored date.

**XP and competence (sections 1, 2 and 6)**
6. **Weight XP towards learning quality, not volume.** Give more XP for correct *delayed* recall in spaced reviews, newly mastered words, and productive tasks (typing, writing, speaking). Give **diminishing XP for replaying easy or mastered content**, and none for social actions like posting, liking or messaging. This guards against the "gamification misuse" Mogavi et al. documented.
7. **Make rewards informational.** Pair every unit with CEFR-style **can-do statements** in both Kurmancî and Soranî tracks, such as "I can greet elders politely", "I can read street signs in Soranî script", or "I can write a 4-line poem". Show a "can-do" progress map alongside the course map. Gamification barely moves perceived competence (Li et al. 2024), but positive informational feedback raises intrinsic motivation (d ≈ +0.33).
8. **Recorded, optionally shared progress.** Send a weekly recap ("12 new words, 3 can-do skills, longest streak 21") that learners can opt in to share with a family member, teacher or friend. Progress monitoring gives d = 0.40, with larger effects when progress is recorded and reported.

**Leagues and rankings (sections 2 and 4)**
9. **Small, engagement-matched weekly cohorts** (Duolingo-style, about 30 people matched on prior-week XP), not one global ranking. Use soft or no demotion at the lowest tiers, plus a visible **personal-best** comparison. Make **opting out** one tap.
10. **Add cooperative team play.** Family, class or diaspora-community teams compete as teams (competition plus collaboration was the most effective social configuration in Sailer & Homner). This also fits the Kahoot-style multiplayer modes, e.g., a "family vs family Wordle battle" or a Saturday-school class league.
11. **Rate-limit and audit league XP** (velocity checks, no XP from farmable repeats). Make league rules transparent, since perceived unfairness drives misuse.

**Zêr currency and shop (sections 1 and 5)**
12. **Keep Zêr earn-only, especially for minors.** If Zêr is ever sold, follow the CPC principles: show the real-money price, no bundle-size mismatch, a withdrawal right, and no pressure offers to children. **Never add randomised paid rewards** (loot-box chests). The Digital Fairness Act expected in Q4 2026 targets virtual currencies and variable rewards.
13. **Avoid paying Zêr for activities that are already intrinsically motivating**, such as writing stories and poems for the community feed. Expected, engagement-contingent tangible rewards had the most negative effect (d = −0.40) and are worse for children. Use *unexpected, informational* recognition instead: featured story, a "word of the week" credit, peer appreciation.
14. **Tie Zêr sinks to learning or culture**, such as unlocking bonus readings, recorded poems or dialect audio, or avatar items with Kurdish cultural meaning. Do not let Zêr buy streak protection, which turns loss aversion into spending.

**Notifications (sections 5 and 6)**
15. **At most one practice reminder a day**, at the learner's planned time. **Rotate templates** to avoid habituation (Duolingo's recovering bandit gave +0.5% DAU). After about 7 days without response, **back off** to weekly, then stop. Use quiet hours.
16. **Content should be informational and connected to other people**, not guilt-based. Examples: "Your friend Rojîn posted a poem using 3 words you know", "5 words are ready for review". No mascot sadness, "you're letting X down" or fake time pressure (Radesky's "parasocial pressure" and "time pressure" dark patterns).

**Children and minors (section 5)**
17. **Child mode by default for under-16s, or a lower age threshold depending on market.** Streak-loss messages off, push notifications off by default (DSA Art. 28 guidelines), no public global leaderboards (private or family leagues only), and no DMs with non-approved contacts. Add break nudges ("Great work, time for a break!"), which the UK Children's Code endorses as wellbeing nudges. Provide a guardian dashboard designed to "empower, not control".
18. **For young children, put fun in the games themselves** (rhyme, Wordle, audio alphabet play, story characters) rather than in XP or Zêr economies. Gamification's motivational effect is smallest in primary school (g = 0.31), and tangible rewards undermine children's motivation most.

**Identity, heritage and relatedness (section 5)**
19. **Ask "why are you learning Kurdish?" during onboarding** (talk to grandparents, raise bilingual children, read Kurdish literature, reconnect with identity, travel or work) and personalise goals, can-do targets and content to the answer. Ideal-self vision predicts effort (r = .61), but because it predicts achievement only weakly, couple it with if-then plans and progress tracking (items 4 and 8).
20. **Family-linked tasks that turn relatedness into practice.** Examples: "record a voice note in Kurmancî for a family member", parent and child learning together, and level-appropriate community-feed prompts ("write 3 lines using this week's words"). Treat learners whose schooling was not in Kurdish with shame-free framing: no public error counts, and positive "you already know more than you think" placement.

**Measurement and ethics guardrails (all sections)**
21. **Measure learning, not just engagement.** Track delayed-recall accuracy, can-do attainment and 30/90-day learner retention as primary metrics. Make DAU and streak counts secondary. Evaluate any new mechanic at **8–12 weeks**, past the novelty dip at about 4–6 weeks.
22. **Add wellbeing guardrail metrics to experiments.** Examples: late-night sessions by minors, share of XP from farmable repeats, opt-out rates from leagues, and short in-app pulse surveys on streak stress. A mechanic that raises engagement but worsens these should not ship.

# Fact-check: speech_audio_tech.md (pronunciation, listening, Kurdish ASR and TTS)

**How this check was done. Read this first.**
- **I could not run any new web searches.** My first three WebSearch calls (Ngo et al. 2024, Uchihara et al. 2025, Meta Omnilingual ASR) all returned "this turn's web search budget is used up (limit: 200 WebSearch calls per turn, shared by every agent in it)". WebFetch is blocked by the egress proxy. This session also does not allow reading the relevant GitHub repositories (Whisper, Omnilingual ASR, fairseq/MMS) directly. I did not try to get around any of these limits.
- I used four other kinds of evidence instead:
  1. **Independent retrievals by sibling agents.** A sibling note counts only when that agent found the source in its own search. A line tagged "via speech_audio_tech.md" is a copy and does not count. In practice, the independent overlaps were sla_theory.md (Ngo et al.; incidental-vocabulary meta-analysis; caption meta-analysis), children.md (child-speech ASR), and heritage_minority_kurdish.md / market_landscape.md (existence of the Kurmanji Whisper paper; a vendor CER claim).
  2. **Internal consistency.** I checked whether the notes' own snippets support the inferences and recommendations built on them. Most of the "overstated" verdicts come from this check.
  3. **The Hevalo repository**, read directly where a recommendation depends on current app behaviour.
  4. **My background knowledge**, labelled **[background, unverified]** every time. It never upgrades a claim to "confirmed", and I invented no URLs for it.
- **Source labels:** *(notes)* means the URL comes from speech_audio_tech.md, and the content behind it is that agent's search snippet. *(independent: agent)* means another agent found it in its own search; this is still a search summary, not the full paper. I did not open any URL listed here.
- **Verdict rules:**
  - **confirmed**: an independent retrieval matches the claim as the notes use it.
  - **overstated**: the core finding may hold, but a number, scope or design inference goes further than the evidence. This can be shown from the notes' own snippets.
  - **refuted**: independent evidence contradicts the claim.
  - **unverified**: nothing independent corroborated it in this session. Per the task rules, these get no benefit of the doubt.
- **Most "unverified" verdicts reflect the exhausted search budget, not a suspected error.** The re-checks to run first are listed at the end.

## Omitted counter-evidence

1. **High-variability training may not beat low-variability training.** **[background, unverified]** Brekelmans et al. (2022, *Journal of Memory and Language*) ran a large replication and found no high-variability advantage for learning non-native contrasts. Together with the null talker-number moderator in Uchihara 2025, this means the "many voices" element of HVPT is the weakest part of the case.
   - The well-supported part is **perception training with immediate feedback**.
   - The notes present HVPT as the strongest-evidence feature (Implications A). It is better described as "strong for perception, weak for production, and unclear on whether variety matters".

2. **Pronunciation effects shrink on spontaneous speech.** The notes report this (Saito & Plonsky 2019; the ReCALL 2024 systematic review found few global measures), but the recommendations then lean on controlled-task effect sizes (g = 0.69–0.86).
   - Expect much smaller gains in learners' free speech.
   - Hevalo should measure **intelligibility to native listeners**, not only in-app scores.

3. **Peer practice: an omitted moderator from Ngo et al.** Practising with a peer gave **g = 0.89 vs 0.44 alone** (independent: sla_theory.md). This is the largest moderator reported for ASR practice and fits Hevalo's social and multiplayer layer. The speech notes never mention it.

4. **ASR feedback can fail in both directions, and the notes quantify neither.** **[background, unverified]**
   - **False accepts:** sequence-to-sequence models with strong implicit language models (Whisper) tend to "auto-correct" learners towards the expected word, and they sometimes hallucinate. This hides real errors. CTC models without a language model, or phone recognizers, are safer for checking pronunciation.
   - **False rejects:** speech that is accented but intelligible gets marked wrong, which hurts motivation.
   - Before shipping any verdict, Hevalo should measure both rates on **deliberately mispronounced tokens** recorded by native speakers.

5. **Hevalo today: every recording passes.** I read this in the repository.
   - `api/src/content/speaking-scorer.ts` defines `StubPronunciationScorer`, which always returns `{ pass: true, confidence: 1 }` ("v1 scorer: every recording passes. Replaced by a real model in KUR-120").
   - `checkSpeaking` in `api/src/content/exercises.ts` uses it to grade speaking exercises "correct".
   - So the notes' Phase 1 (record and compare, no ASR) is not a starting point but a **correction**: the app currently gives a false "correct" verdict. Replace it with an honest "listen and compare" step until a validated scorer exists.

6. **Contrasts should be chosen by functional load and dialect stability, not only by what Hawar fails to mark.** **[background, unverified]**
   - Munro & Derwing (2006) found that high-functional-load errors hurt comprehensibility more.
   - Nobody in these notes has estimated how many Kurmanji word pairs the aspiration contrast distinguishes, or how stable it is among younger, urban and diaspora speakers. The notes themselves say it is disappearing in some dialects.
   - Sorani does not use aspiration contrastively, as far as I know. Kurdish linguists should rank the contrasts before the module is built.

7. **TTS can supply perception-training variety for contrasts the script does mark.** **[background, unverified]** Qian, Chukharev-Hudilainen & Levis (2018, *Language Learning & Technology*) built adaptive high-variability perception training from **text-to-speech voices** and reported gains and transfer in English.
   - For contrasts the orthography does mark, multi-voice TTS is a cheap way to add talker variety. Examples: Sorani ڕ/ر and ڵ/ل; Kurmanji i/î, u/û, e/ê.
   - The notes' blanket "TTS not for pronunciation" leaves this out.

8. **Captions: a newer meta-analysis was missed, and keyword captions are not supported.**
   - The notes rely on Montero Perez 2013 without effect sizes. The sla_theory agent independently retrieved **Kurokawa et al. 2025** (49 studies): **g = 0.56 overall, g = 1.36 for primary-school learners, and g = 1.04 for videos made for L2 learners**. This strengthens rec 17.
   - **[background, unverified]** Montero Perez, Peters & Desmet (2014) found **keyword-only captions no better than full captions**, and worse for comprehension. Rec 17's middle "keyword-only" step therefore has no support. Fade from full captions to none, or A/B-test the intermediate step.

9. **Child ASR is worse in real classrooms.** The children agent found **54% WER on classroom speech even after adaptation**. Home and app recordings are noisy too. This strengthens the "no pass/fail for children" rule.

10. **Licences and consent for voice data are more restrictive than rec 14 implies.**
    - From the children notes: a child's voice recording is personal information under COPPA, and the 2025 amendments add biometric and retention duties. The children notes rest the voice-recording point on prior knowledge.
    - **[background, unverified]** Releasing recordings under CC0 to Common Voice is effectively irreversible. Voice recordings are personal data under GDPR, and become special-category biometric data when used to identify someone.
    - Recordings should therefore go to Common Voice only through a **separate, adult-only, explicit opt-in**. Children's recordings should never be openly licensed.
    - **[background, unverified]** If the MMS CC-BY-NC licence is confirmed, MMS models are also out for any paid tier. Omnilingual's Apache 2.0 licence is the cleaner choice.

11. **Benchmark contamination and per-language results for Omnilingual.** Omnilingual was trained on large public corpora. **[background, unverified]** FLEURS and Common Voice are likely among them. Its Kurdish scores on those sets may therefore be optimistic, and the per-language appendix of arXiv 2511.09690 should be read before choosing it.

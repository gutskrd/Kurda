Evidence grades used: **[MA]** meta-analysis, **[RCT]**, **[QE]** quasi-experimental, **[OBS]** observational/survey/descriptive/corpus work, **[EXP]** expert opinion or position paper, **[NEWS]** journalism or official statement, **[VENDOR]** vendor or app-store claim. ## 1. Heritage language learners: profiles, pedagogy, identity and family motivation

### Takeaway
Heritage learners (HLLs) usually have strong listening and decent everyday speaking. Their literacy, formal register and some grammar are weak, and they vary along a proficiency continuum. Identity and connection to family are their main motivations. Pedagogy that works for them starts from what they already know (top-down, "macro" approach, deductive grammar, literacy-first). It does not march them through a beginner L2 syllabus.

### Cited Findings
- **Profile (largest survey to date) [OBS, survey, n≈1,800 college HLLs, 22 languages; Carreira & Kagan 2011; older but still the reference survey]:** the typical HLL (1) acquired the societal language in early childhood after the HL; (2) has limited HL exposure outside the home; (3) has "relatively strong aural and oral skills but limited literacy skills"; (4) has positive HL attitudes; (5) studies the HL mainly to connect with communities of speakers and to gain insight into their roots — [Carreira & Kagan survey PDF](https://hwpi.harvard.edu/files/heritagespanish/files/carreira_kagan_survey_0.pdf); [Results of the National Heritage Language Survey](https://cris.bgu.ac.il/en/publications/the-results-of-the-national-heritage-language-survey-implications/)
- **Motivation ranking [OBS, same survey]:** in order: (1) to understand themselves through their cultural and linguistic heritage, (2) to communicate with family and friends, (3) to fulfil a language requirement — [Carreira & Kagan survey PDF](https://hwpi.harvard.edu/files/heritagespanish/files/carreira_kagan_survey_0.pdf)
- **Identity is central [EXP]:** NHLRC/UCLA describes identity as "the centerpiece rather than the background of HL development". Affective issues (belonging, connection to the heritage culture) are central to HL learning — [NHLRC: Identity, the driving force behind HL learning](https://nhlrc.ucla.edu/nhlrc/article/205553)
- **Proficiency is a continuum [EXP/theory; Polinsky]:** Polinsky distinguishes *acrolectal* (near the homeland baseline), *mesolectal* and *basilectal* (most divergent) heritage speakers. Speakers with only receptive skills are sometimes called "overhearers". Sequential bilinguals tend to be somewhat more proficient in the HL than simultaneous ones — [Frontiers in Education 2020 (review)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2020.00053/pdf); [LINGUIST List review 32.496](https://linguistlist.org/issues/32/496/)
- **Literacy is the typical gap [EXP]:** "lack of literacy is also a typical feature of heritage speakers". For Russian HLLs, acquiring literacy is a main objective — [Frontiers in Education 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2020.00053/pdf)
- **Deductive grammar suits HLLs [EXP; Bermel & Kagan 2000, older]:** unlike non-heritage students, HLLs retain a high degree of internal grammar, so deduction (making implicit knowledge explicit) is a good strategy — [Frontiers in Education 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2020.00053/pdf)
- **Current HL pedagogy [EXP]:** focuses on developing communicative/functional abilities and on critical exploration of bilingual practices and identities — [Frontiers in Education 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2020.00053/pdf)
- **Apps in HL families [OBS, qualitative; 2018]:** whether families use digital tech for the HL depends on three interacting variables: (a) availability of resources and technical know-how, (b) parent and child attitudes towards the HL, (c) parent and child attitudes towards online games and apps — ['Is there an app for that?' J. Multilingual & Multicultural Development 2018](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01434632.2018.1502776)
- **Narrative media for HL children [QE, Korean HLLs, 2026; effect sizes not visible in snippet]:** "strategic media exposure at home" with narrative-driven media tailored to children's interests promoted language enjoyment, vocabulary development and autonomy. Mothers reported more clarity and confidence in supporting the HL. Children began using Korean with grandparents and tried teaching words to family members — [Language and Education 2026](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09500782.2026.2638212)
- **Grandparent–grandchild digital interaction [OBS, 2026]:** digital media (videoconferencing, digital stories, cartoons) supports intergenerational transmission. In a Polish case, digital stories and cartoons helped vocabulary acquisition and cultural transmission and increased enthusiasm — [Innovation in Language Learning and Teaching 2026](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17501229.2026.2614753)
- **Related 2025–2026 work [OBS]:** parents' perspectives on online HL learning — [Early Childhood Education Journal 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10643-026-02291-9). Zoom-mediated HL maintenance among Farsi-speaking migrants in Australia (mixed methods) — [Asian-Pacific J. of Second and Foreign Language Education 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40862-026-00441-5). The "digital shift" in parental HL strategies — [JMMD 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01434632.2025.2491607). HL maintenance as a collective, not only family, responsibility — [PMC 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12185927/)

### Inferences
- A one-size Duolingo-style "absolute beginner" path wastes heritage learners' time and can feel patronising. HLLs need placement or "test-out" by skill (listening vs reading vs writing). The bottleneck for most diaspora Kurds is likely **literacy in Hawar/Sorani script and spelling**, not basic vocabulary.
- The best-documented lever is **identity and family connection**. Features that connect the learner to family members (grandparents, parents) are likely to sustain motivation more than abstract XP.
- Deductive "noticing" lessons fit HLLs better than drill-only exposure: "you already say X; here is why it works that way" (e.g. ergative past or ezafe).

### Gaps
- No Kurdish-specific heritage-learner survey was found (proficiency profile, motivations, script literacy rates among diaspora Kurds).
- No meta-analysis of HL pedagogy effectiveness was found. The Korean 2026 quasi-experiment's effect sizes were not visible in the snippet.
- I did not find Valdés's or Polinsky & Kagan's (2007) primary texts directly; the characterisations come from secondary reviews.

## 2. Lessons from minority-language revitalization technology (Welsh, Irish, Māori, Hawaiian, Navajo, Basque)

### Takeaway
Apps can bring in very large numbers of beginners for minority languages: Welsh on Duolingo passed 2 million starters. But they depend on sustained funding and community content creators. Even Duolingo paused Welsh development (2023) and ended its volunteer contributor model (2021). The revitalization successes with proven outcomes (Māori Kōhanga Reo, Hawaiian Pūnana Leo) are immersion programmes centred on children and families, not apps. The recurring problem is that beginners do not progress to independent use.

### Cited Findings
- **Duolingo Welsh scale [NEWS/official, 2023]:** more than 2 million people had started Welsh on Duolingo, with 659,000 active learners, when Duolingo announced in October 2023 that it would pause development of the course from November. Resources were moved to high-demand courses such as Spanish, French and German — [Senedd research brief](https://business.senedd.wales/documents/s150940/Research%20brief.html?CT=2); [ITV News 2023](https://www.itv.com/news/wales/2023-10-24/language-learning-app-to-pause-updates-on-its-welsh-course-from-next-month)
- **Earlier Welsh figure [NEWS, c.2018–2019, superseded by the 2023 figure]:** the Welsh course passed 1 million total learners, with 337,000 active — [CNN 2019](https://edition.cnn.com/2019/10/04/tech/duolingo-endangered-languages-intl-hnk/index.html) (snippet-level; the exact source page within the result set is unverified)
- **Policy reaction [NEWS]:** Welsh Education and Welsh Language Minister Jeremy Miles wrote to Duolingo's CEO on 25 October 2023 asking it to reconsider. The founder of SaySomethinginWelsh, a competing audio-first Welsh course, also called for fresh Welsh content — [Senedd research brief](https://business.senedd.wales/documents/s150940/Research%20brief.html?CT=2); [Nation.Cymru](https://nation.cymru/news/saysomethinginwelsh-founder-calls-for-duolingo-to-continue-with-fresh-welsh-content/). The Welsh Government was also accused of "breaking" the course — [Nation.Cymru](https://nation.cymru/news/welsh-government-accused-of-breaking-welsh-duolingo-course/)
- **Hawaiian and Navajo on Duolingo [NEWS, 2018]:** Navajo (Diné Bizaad) and Hawaiian (ʻŌlelo Hawaiʻi) were added in October 2018 — [Latino USA](https://www.latinousa.org/2018/10/16/duolingonavajo/); [TIME: Can apps save endangered languages?](https://time.com/5417035/technology-endangered-languages/). Reported figure: Hawaiian was the chosen language of about 586,000 Duolingo users as of March 2022 — [Matador Network](https://matadornetwork.com/read/endangered-languages-learn-online/) (snippet-level; attribution within the result set is uncertain)
- **No outcome studies for these courses [gap/OBS]:** Duolingo's general efficacy claim (learners who finished 5 units of Spanish/French matched 4 university semesters on reading and listening) is about major languages, not Welsh/Hawaiian/Navajo. It is company-funded research — [Duolingo investor release](https://investors.duolingo.com/news-releases/news-release-details/leading-language-research-journal-publishes-study-showing) **[VENDOR-funded OBS]**
- **Community content model ended [official, 2021]:** Duolingo ended its Volunteer Contributor program in March 2021. More than 1,000 volunteers had built dozens of courses since 2013, and Navajo, Hawaiian, Welsh and Irish "would not exist" without them. Duolingo moved to paid content creation and set up a $4M "Language Impact" award fund for past contributors — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/ending-honoring-our-volunteer-contributor-program-2/)
- **Māori [OBS/EXP]:** Te Kōhanga Reo (immersion "language nests", started 1982) is described as having met and exceeded its goals of increasing native Māori speakers — [ResearchGate: Revitalizing the Māori language](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/293117325_Revitalizing_the_Maori_language_A_focus_on_educational_reform). A known challenge is the **low transition rate from basic learners to independent/fluent use** — [Māori language revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_language_revival) (snippet; secondary). A Māori mobilisation campaign (Karawhiua) framed learning as each person's "own language journey" — [Karawhiua, RDS Journal](https://rdsjournal.org/index.php/journal/article/view/1168)
- **Hawaiian [OBS/organisational claim]:** ʻAha Pūnana Leo preschools, inspired by Kōhanga Reo, have served about 7,000 children, most of whom go on to one of 22 Hawaiian-immersion public schools (kula kaiapuni) — [Ka Wai Ola](https://kawaiola.news/hoonaauao/aha-punana-leo-the-preschool-program-that-inspired-a-movement/). WINHEC reported that Pūnana Leo children often outperform English-speaking peers in early literacy **[EXP/organisational claim; no effect size]** — [Indigenous Language Immersion Education (UH ScholarSpace)](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/25a16be2-e488-4a45-b853-aa5819269430/content)

### Inferences
- **Platform-dependence risk:** minority-language courses on big platforms get deprioritised (Welsh, 2023). An independent, community-owned Kurdish platform fills a gap Duolingo has shown it will not reliably fill. But Hevalo needs a sustainable content pipeline: paid core authors plus moderated community contributions.
- **Volunteer-to-paid content creation** is the industry trajectory. A hybrid (community submits, editors review, contributors get credit or reward) fits Hevalo's existing community feed and Zêr currency.
- **"Beginner trap"** (Māori, Welsh): gamified apps produce many starters but few independent users. Hevalo should design explicit bridges out of the app: graded reading and listening, conversation with real people, family tasks.
- The proven revitalization model is **children plus family plus immersion**. For an app, the nearest equivalents are child-mode content, parent/grandparent co-play, and large volumes of comprehensible audio and video.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed outcome data (proficiency gains, retention) were found for Duolingo Welsh, Irish, Hawaiian or Navajo, or for Māori apps.
- No evidence was gathered for SaySomethingIn's method (beyond its founder's public statements), for Basque (e.g. HABE, Euskaltegi apps) or for Irish-specific app outcomes. These remain open.
- The current (2026) status of Duolingo Welsh, i.e. whether development resumed, was not verified.

## 3. Kurdish linguistic specifics: dialects, scripts, standardization, hard features

### Takeaway
"Kurdish" covers several varieties: Kurmanji (Northern), Sorani (Central) and Southern Kurdish, with Zazaki and Gorani disputed. They are written mainly in two scripts: Hawar Latin for Kurmanji in Turkey, Syria and Armenia, and a Sorani Perso-Arabic alphabet in Iraq and Iran. Kurmanji's hardest features for learners are split ergativity in the past tense (with variation among native speakers) and a gender-, number- and definiteness-inflecting ezafe.

### Cited Findings
- **Scripts [EXP/reference]:** Kurdish is mostly written in the Latin Bedirxan/Hawar alphabet (Celadet Alî Bedirxan, 1932, popularised by *Hawar* magazine) or the Kurdo-Arabic alphabet. Hawar is used mainly in Syria, Turkey and Armenia; Kurdo-Arabic mainly in Iraq and Iran — [Kurdish alphabets (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_alphabets); [Romanization of Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Kurdish)
- **Sorani standard [EXP/reference]:** Sorani uses a modified Perso-Arabic script of 33 letters, associated with Sa'id Kaban Sedqi. The Kurdistan Region has agreed a standard for Central Kurdish, implemented in Unicode. The Sulaymaniyah variety was the standard in the Mahabad Republic and later in Kurdish Iraq — [Kurdish alphabets (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_alphabets)
- **Kurmanji split ergativity [EXP, linguistics; Haig 2004, Thackston 2006, Gündoğdu 2011]:** Kurmanji is accusative in non-past tenses and ergative in the past. The direct case marks present-tense agents and past-tense patients, and the oblique case marks past-tense agents and present-tense patients. Kurmanji has "full ergativity in the past tense", which distinguishes it from other Kurdish varieties — [Arizona Iranian Languages resource](https://iranian-languages.arizona.edu/node/7); [Erbasi Gündoğdu NACIL handout](https://mariecolvincenter.org/commcms/nacil/pdfs/slides-handouts/Erbasi-Gundogdu_NACIL1Handout.pdf)
- **Ergativity acquisition and change in progress [OBS, dissertation, UT Austin 2009, older]:** children acquiring Kurmanji learn split-ergative relations from "highly variable non-systematic input from caretakers, likely due to language change in progress" — [The acquisition of split-ergativity in Kurmanji Kurdish](https://tdl-ir.tdl.org/handle/2152/ETD-UT-2009-12-448); see also [NWAV36 abstract (Mahalingappa)](https://www.ling.upenn.edu/NWAVE/abstracts/nwav36_mahalingappa.pdf). Regional varieties are reanalysing ergative marking, e.g. Adıyaman Kurmanji "ergative as accusative case" — [Boğaziçi archive](https://digitalarchive.library.bogazici.edu.tr/handle/123456789/15845)
- **Ezafe [EXP]:** the Kurmanji ezafe links post-nominal modifiers to the head noun and inflects for gender, number and definiteness (e.g. different forms for masculine, feminine and plural) — [Erbasi & Yücel NACIL abstract](https://ehs.stonybrook.edu/commcms/nacil/pdfs/abstracts/Erbasi-Yucel_NACIL_EZAFE%20ACROSSLANGUAGEFAMILIES-IRANIANALTAIC.pdf); [Arizona Iranian Languages](https://iranian-languages.arizona.edu/node/7)
- **Zazaki/Gorani debate [EXP, contested]:** historical linguists widely treat Zazaki as a separate Iranian language. A Zaza–Gorani grouping (Mann & Hadank 1932) is criticised as based on superficial similarities. Many Zazaki and Gorani speakers nonetheless identify as Kurds and call their speech a variety of Kurdish — [Critical examination of the Gorani/Zazaki separation hypothesis (J. of Kurdish Literature, UoK)](https://jokl.uok.ac.ir/article_63264.html?lang=en); [Haig & Öpengin, Kurdish: a critical research overview](https://www.academia.edu/9265357/Kurdish_a_critical_research_overview); [Zaza nationalism (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaza_nationalism). An empirical mutual-intelligibility study of Kurmanji and Zazaki in Elazığ exists, but I could not see its results — [Applied Linguistics Review](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/applirev-2020-0151/html?lang=en)
- **Further varieties [OBS, NLP]:** Southern Kurdish and Laki are low-resource, with corpus-building only recent — [arXiv 2304.01319](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.01319). Central Kurdish itself has several varieties (CORDI corpus) — [Language and Speech Technology for Central Kurdish Varieties, arXiv 2403.01983](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.01983). Badini/Behdînî is supported as a separate voice by at least one commercial TTS — [kurdishtts.com](https://www.kurdishtts.com/) **[VENDOR]**
- Turkey's school Kurdish electives (see section 4) are split into **Kurmancî and Zazakî** courses. In practice Zazaki is treated as a distinct teaching track — [Turkish Minute 2025](https://www.turkishminute.com/2025/06/03/kurdish-elective-course-enrollment-hits-record-high-in-turkey-as-barriers-remain/)

### Inferences
- **Ergative past tense** should get dedicated, spaced lesson units for Kurmanji: oblique agent, verb agreeing with the patient, and the ez/min alternation in "I went" vs "I saw him". Feedback should be lenient where native variation exists, so heritage speakers whose family usage differs from the standard are not marked wrong.
- **Ezafe and gender** need repeated, explicit practice with immediate feedback. Gender is invisible on most bare nouns, so the app should teach nouns together with their ezafe or demonstrative forms (chunks).
- **Dialect and script are identity issues.** Hevalo should present Kurmanji, Sorani (and possibly Zazaki/Badini later) as equal "varieties" without taking sides in the language-vs-dialect debate, and let users pick both variety and script.

### Gaps
- **Phonology not verified in this session:** I could not retrieve a source on Kurmanji aspirated vs unaspirated voiceless stops and affricates (p/p', t/t', k/k', ç/ç'), which standard Hawar orthography does not mark. This is from background knowledge only and should be checked against Thackston or Haig & Öpengin before use. The same applies to Sorani phonemes rare for European learners (ڕ trilled r, ڵ velarised l, ح, ع, غ, ڤ).
- The Cyrillic Kurdish alphabet (Soviet Armenia, historical) was not covered by the results.
- No learner-error corpus or second-language acquisition study of adult Kurdish learners (which features cause most errors) was found.
- Total speaker numbers per variety were not retrieved.

## 4. Kurdish diaspora demographics, education in Turkey/Iraq/Iran/Syria, and demand

### Takeaway
Diaspora figures are rough because only Sweden registers Kurds, but Germany is by far the largest community (most estimates exceed 1 million), followed by France, Sweden and the UK. School access to Kurdish ranges from full Kurdish-medium schooling (Kurdistan Region of Iraq) to small electives (Turkey, where demand is rising sharply), bans (Iran), and recent rollback (parts of Syria). Diaspora schooling covers only a few thousand pupils. Demand therefore far exceeds formal supply.

### Cited Findings
- **Diaspora estimates [EXP/secondary; conflicting]:**
  - Germany: 1.2–1.5 million per Wikipedia's figures — [Kurds in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurds_in_Germany); vs. the Institut kurde de Paris's older breakdown of Germany 600,000–650,000, France 100,000–120,000, Sweden 25,000–30,000 and UK 20,000–25,000, about 850,000 in Western Europe — [Institut kurde: The Kurdish Diaspora](https://www.institutkurde.org/en/kurdorama/). **The Institut kurde breakdown appears to be older and is likely superseded** (date not shown in snippet). The same snippet also cites an 800,000 figure for Germany.
  - France: about 150,000–240,000 — [Kurds in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurds_in_France)
  - Sweden: 100,000+ to 170,000 — [Kurds in Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurds_in_Sweden). About 84,000 Kurdish *speakers*, one of Sweden's largest minority languages — [Language on the Move](https://languageonthemove.com/kurdish-heritage-language-education/)
  - UK: 49,841 (basis unclear in snippet, possibly a census language count) — [Kurds (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurds)
  - Caveat: "with the exception of Sweden, no country registers the Kurds as Kurds". Estimates are derived from shares of Turkish, Iraqi, Iranian and Syrian citizens — [Institut kurde](https://www.institutkurde.org/en/kurdorama/)
  - See also [MERIP: Mapping Euro-Kurdistan (2008, older)](https://www.merip.org/2008/06/mapping-euro-kurdistan/); [The Kurdish Project diaspora map](https://thekurdishproject.org/kurdistan-map/kurdish-diaspora/)
- **Sweden mother-tongue instruction [OBS/policy]:** municipalities must offer mother tongue instruction (MTI) if at least 5 eligible pupils and a suitable teacher are available. Eligibility requires daily use with at least one caregiver. Huddinge first offered Kurdish MTI in 1975 — [Language on the Move](https://languageonthemove.com/kurdish-heritage-language-education/); Sweden's model is described as "impressive but imperfect" — [HLE Network interview 2023](https://www.hlenet.org/post/2023-interview-sweden)
- **Germany [OBS]:** Kurdish has been taught in *Herkunftssprachenunterricht* since 1993. About 3,000 pupils across seven German states are enrolled — [Language on the Move](https://languageonthemove.com/kurdish-heritage-language-education/)
- **KRG–diaspora online schooling [NEWS, 2026]:** a joint Kurdistani Diaspora Confederation and KRG Ministry of Education project has registered 30 Kurdish schools abroad. About 3,000 students learn Kurdish in various dialects through an online system, presented as Kurdish being taught "as a formal subject" in Sweden and Germany — [Kurdistan24](https://www.kurdistan24.net/en/story/893463/kurdish-now-taught-as-formal-subject-in-sweden-and-germany-under-joint-kdc-krg-initiative); [Sema TV, Feb 2026](https://sematv.net/en/2026/02/5-82)
- **Turkey electives [NEWS; numbers attributed to Ministry data]:**
  - 2024–25: 30,862 pupils chose the Kurmancî elective and 4,554 Zazakî — [Turkish Minute, June 2025](https://www.turkishminute.com/2025/06/03/kurdish-elective-course-enrollment-hits-record-high-in-turkey-as-barriers-remain/)
  - 2025–26: a record **59,372** (50,809 Kurmancî + 8,563 Zazakî), roughly a 68% rise year on year (my calculation from the two figures) — [Kurdistan24](https://www.kurdistan24.net/en/story/909729/kurdish-language-demand-surges-in-t%C3%BCrkiyes-schools); [Kurdistan24 Istanbul/Diyarbakır](https://www.kurdistan24.net/en/story/940251/kurdish-language-class-demand-surges-in-istanbul-and-diyarbakir) (the snippet merged these sources; the exact attribution of each figure is uncertain)
  - Supply constraints: only 10 of 20,000 new teaching posts went to Kurdish in 2024, down from 50 the year before. Many schools do not offer the course or steer pupils to other electives — [Turkish Minute](https://www.turkishminute.com/2025/06/03/kurdish-elective-course-enrollment-hits-record-high-in-turkey-as-barriers-remain/); [Duvar English](https://www.duvarenglish.com/students-in-diyarbakir-deprived-of-kurdish-language-courses-directed-towards-religious-classes-says-education-union-news-60178); [Nationalia](https://www.nationalia.info/new/11721/teaching-in-silence-the-struggle-for-kurdish-in-turkeys-education-system); curriculum analysis — [IMPACT-se](https://www.impact-se.org/wp-content/uploads/Two-Languages-One-Country-Turkeys-Elective-Kurdish-Curriculum.pdf); origin of the elective — [Hürriyet Daily News](https://www.hurriyetdailynews.com/turkish-pm-unveils-kurdish-courses-at-schools-23031) (the 2012 introduction date is from background knowledge, not the snippet)
- **Iran [EXP/NGO]:** Kurdish is barred as a medium of academic instruction, though Kurdish literature classes are permitted. Persian-only schooling is linked to "high levels of illiteracy" among Kurds — [Respond Crisis Translation](https://respondcrisistranslation.org/en/blog/ks-histories-and-geographies-of-kurdish-suppression); a Kurdish teacher was imprisoned for teaching Kurdish (sentence reduced, 2021) — [Language Magazine](https://languagemagazine.com/2021/04/30/iran-reduces-kurdish-teachers-sentence/)
- **Syria [NEWS]:** Kurdish education was fully banned before 2011. Kurdish-led administrations in the north then introduced bilingual Kurdish/Arabic models — [Syria Direct](https://syriadirect.org/kurdish-residents-in-syrias-north-find-linguistic-freedom-after-decades-of-state-suppression/); [EURAC](https://www.eurac.edu/en/blogs/eureka/the-kurdish-minority-linguistic-rights-and-education-in-turkey-and-syria). **Recent reversal:** the Syrian transitional government cancelled Kurdish education in Afrin schools, and the unified curriculum does not recognise Kurdish — [North Press Agency](https://npasyria.com/en/131383/)
- **Language shift [EXP]:** decades of bans confined Kurmanji and Sorani to the home and "stunted linguistic development in each new generation" — [CASLT](https://www.caslt.org/en/blog-discovering-languages-kurdish/); see also [Kurdish Archive blog 2025](https://blog.kurdish-archive.org/2025/05/KurdishEducation.html)
- **Iraq/KRG:** the Sorani standard is the official regional language — [Kurdish alphabets (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_alphabets). A thesis discusses Kurdistan's "language problem" (Sorani vs Badini standard tensions) — [DePaul thesis 2018](https://academics.depaul.edu/honors/curriculum/archives/Documents/2018%20Senior%20Theses/Walter,%20Kurdistan's%20Language%20Problem.pdf) (content not visible in snippet)

### Inferences
- **Three large learner segments with different needs:**
  1. **Diaspora heritage learners** (Germany, Sweden, France, NL, UK), mostly from Turkey and therefore Kurmanji. They speak or understand some Kurdish but read little. UI in de/sv/fr/nl/en matters.
  2. **In-region learners who were schooled in Turkish, Arabic or Persian** (Turkey, Syria, Iran). They are often fluent speakers and illiterate in Kurdish. Their need is reading and writing, standard spelling, and in Iran an alternative to banned schooling.
  3. **Children and school pupils**: Turkey's electives (~59k pupils, too few teachers), Sweden MTI, German HSU and KRG online schools. Teachers need classroom tools; there is a clear B2B/teacher angle.
- Demand in Turkey is rising fast while teacher supply is near zero, which favours a free, self-study, phone-first product.
- **Sweden** is missing from Hevalo's nine UI languages, despite being a top-3 diaspora country with formal Kurdish MTI.

### Gaps
- No reliable 2025–2026 figures for Kurds in the Netherlands, US, Belgium or Austria were obtained.
- No data on diaspora literacy rates in Kurdish, or on the share of diaspora Kurds who speak Kurmanji vs Sorani vs Zazaki.
- The KRG's current Kurdish-medium enrolment and the role of Badini in KRG schooling were not retrieved.

## 5. Existing Kurdish learning resources, apps, NLP/speech resources, media and children's content

### Takeaway
Duolingo has no Kurdish. The app field is fragmented among small players (uTalk, Dialect, Bimus, ZimanGo) plus tutoring marketplaces, and none appears to combine heritage-learner placement, both scripts, deep content, games and community. Kurdish NLP has grown fast: large Sorani corpora, parallel Sorani–Kurmanji–English data, transliteration tools, a Kurdish NLP toolkit, Whisper-based ASR and new TTS datasets. That makes speech and AI features feasible in 2026, especially for Sorani; Kurmanji is still thinner. Usable comprehensible input exists (Zarok TV cartoons in Kurmanji, Zazaki and Sorani; bilingual picture books; a new children's magazine), but it is scattered.

### Inferences
- **Competitive gap:** competitors are thin, single-mode apps. Hevalo's combination of both varieties and scripts, a large dictionary, games and community is unusual. Its missing pieces relative to research needs are heritage placement, graded input (stories and audio) and speech feedback.
- **AI/speech is now feasible:** Whisper fine-tunes and TTS datasets exist. Pronunciation and listening features can be built or licensed, with quality better for Sorani than Kurmanji. Vendor CER claims must be tested on Hevalo's own learner audio. Learner speech (accented, child voices) is much harder than benchmark speech.
- **Transliteration lets one content asset serve both scripts.** Sorani Arabic↔Latin is mature (rule-based). Kurmanji content shown in Arabic script (for Iraqi Badini or Iranian Kurmanji speakers) is also possible.
- **Content licensing opportunity:** Zarok TV, 49 Books/Hano and the bilingual picture-book publishers are natural partners for licensed comprehensible input in child mode.

### Gaps
- No independent reviews, usage numbers or outcome data for any Kurdish learning app.
- The Kurdish Wiktionary's current size and quality (Hevalo's dictionary source) was not checked. Wiktionary-derived lists usually include many inflected or auto-generated forms and need frequency filtering.
- Zarok TV's 2026 status and licensing terms are unknown.
- Whether Google Translate or Meta NLLB/MMS quality for Kurmanji and Sorani is good enough for learner-facing use was not retrieved in this session.

## Implications for Hevalo

### Takeaway
Hevalo's biggest audience, diaspora and in-region Kurds, consists mostly of *heritage speakers with weak literacy*, not true beginners. Hevalo should therefore (1) place and route by skill, (2) make literacy, spelling and script a first-class track, (3) build around identity and family, (4) supply large amounts of graded, culturally Kurdish input, especially for children, and (5) handle dialect and script variation respectfully. These points are justified by sections 1–5. Evidence strength behind them is mostly observational and expert opinion; no RCTs exist in this domain.

### Inferences (concrete design implications)
1. **Heritage onboarding and placement (sections 1, 4).** Ask "Who speaks Kurdish in your family? Which variety? Can you understand / speak / read / write it?" Then run a 3–5 minute adaptive placement with separate listening, reading and spelling probes. Route heritage learners past beginner vocabulary units into a **"Read & write what you already speak"** track. Let any unit be tested out of.
2. **Literacy track as a core product (sections 1, 3, 4).**
   - Extend the alphabet module into full **sound–spelling mapping**: Hawar letters ê/î/û, ç/ş, x/q/ḧ distinctions, and doubled-vowel conventions; for Sorani, vowel letters و/ۆ/ێ/ی, ڕ and ڵ.
   - Add **dictation exercises**: hear a word or sentence, type it, with diacritic-tolerant feedback that tells the learner which letter was wrong.
   - Add **reading-aloud with recorded native audio**.
   - This directly addresses the "strong oral, weak literacy" HLL profile.
3. **Deductive "you already say this" grammar notes (sections 1, 3).**
   - Give Kurmanji ergative past, ezafe (-ê/-a/-ên/-yê…) and gender their own spaced units. Each opens with familiar example sentences, then the explicit rule, then targeted practice.
   - Accept attested regional variants (e.g. where ergative marking is being reanalysed) with a "standard vs. common spoken" note rather than a hard "wrong".
4. **Dialect- and script-aware content model (sections 3, 5).**
   - Store each lexical item and sentence with tags for variety (Kurmancî, Soranî, later Behdînî/Badini, Zazakî as an opt-in separate track) and script.
   - Use rule-based **Arabic↔Latin transliteration** (Ahmadi's tools, KLPT) to offer a "show in other script" toggle and to bootstrap Sorani-in-Latin and Kurmanji-in-Arabic views.
   - Use the KurdishBLARK interdialect corpus to build **Kurmanji↔Sorani "cousin word" cards** for learners who know one variety.
   - Treat the language-vs-dialect question neutrally in copy.
5. **Family and identity features (sections 1, 2).**
   - **"Ask your grandparent" missions**: record a family member saying a word or proverb; save it to a private family collection, optionally sharing it to the community feed.
   - **Family/household groups** with shared streaks.
   - A **parent dashboard** for children.
   - Lessons on family terms, kinship, names, holidays (Newroz), food and region, so learning maps onto real family conversations.
   - Supported by the Carreira & Kagan motivation ranking and 2026 intergenerational digital-media studies.
6. **Child mode built on narrative media (sections 1, 2, 5).**
   - Short animated or illustrated stories with audio, read-along highlighting and tap-to-hear words, matched to children's interests (the Korean 2026 quasi-experiment).
   - Pursue licensing partnerships with Zarok TV (Kurmanji, Zazaki and Sorani cartoons), Hano/49 Books (Sweden) and bilingual picture-book publishers (Language Lizard, Sefa Verlag).
   - Simplified UI, no DMs, no public feed; parent controls.
7. **Graded reader and listening library to escape the "beginner trap" (section 2).** Turn the community stories and poems feed into **levelled content**: editor-reviewed, CEFR-tagged, with audio and glossed words linked to the dictionary. Readers can save unknown words straight into spaced-repetition review. This is the bridge from app learner to independent user that Māori and Welsh efforts identify as the weak link.
8. **Sustainable community content pipeline (section 2).** Avoid the Duolingo-Welsh dependency problem with a hybrid model:
   - Community members submit sentences, audio recordings and story translations.
   - Native-speaker editors (paid or Zêr-rewarded, with public credit badges) review them.
   - Contributions are tagged by variety and region.
   - This also grows a Kurmanji speech corpus, the weakest NLP area. Get licensing and consent right if the audio may later be used for ASR/TTS.
9. **Speech features, staged (section 5).**
   - Stage 1: native-recorded audio everywhere, plus TTS fallback only where quality has been checked by native speakers. Sorani TTS datasets are stronger than Kurmanji ones.
   - Stage 2: "record and compare" (as in uTalk).
   - Stage 3: ASR-based pronunciation checking using Whisper fine-tunes for Kurmanji and Sorani. Evaluate on real learner and child audio first, and never auto-fail heritage learners for regional accents.
   - Test vendor accuracy claims (e.g. 4.54% CER) independently before relying on them.
10. **Dictionary quality pass (section 5).**
    - Frequency-rank the ~400k Wiktionary entries using the AsoSoft (Sorani), Pewan and OSCAR (Kurmanji/Sorani) corpora.
    - Flag core vocabulary (top ~2–5k) for lesson design and "word of the day".
    - Hide or merge auto-generated inflected forms.
    - Link lemmas to audio and example sentences from the graded library.
11. **Teacher and school mode (section 4).** Turkey has about 59k elective pupils and almost no new Kurdish teachers. Sweden has MTI, Germany has HSU (~3k pupils), and the KRG–diaspora online schools teach about 3k. Offer classroom groups, assignable units, progress reports, and Kahoot-style multiplayer for live classes, aligned to the Kurmancî/Zazakî elective and the KRG curricula. This is a distribution channel as well as a learning feature.
12. **Localization priorities (section 4).** Add **Swedish (sv)** UI (top-3 diaspora country with formal Kurdish MTI), and consider **Persian (fa)** for Iranian Kurds. The Turkish (tr) and Arabic (ar) UIs are especially important for in-region learners schooled in those languages, who can transfer literacy (Latin letters from Turkish, Arabic letters from Arabic/Persian). Use contrastive tips such as "Kurdish ç/ş = Turkish ç/ş, but x ≠ Turkish h".
13. **Pronunciation contrasts (section 3, verify first).** If expert sources confirm Kurmanji aspirated/unaspirated stop and affricate contrasts that Hawar does not mark, add listening minimal-pair games (e.g. in the rhyme or Wordle families). The same applies to Sorani ڕ/ر and ڵ/ل contrasts, which are marked in the script and are good for "hear it, pick the letter" drills.
14. **Measure outcomes (all sections).** No outcome data exist for any Kurdish app, so Hevalo can generate the first:
    - in-app pre/post placement scores;
    - delayed retention checks;
    - cohort comparisons of heritage vs non-heritage learners;
    - A/B tests of features such as family missions and dictation.
    Publishing or partnering with Kurdish linguists (e.g. those behind KLPT/KurdishBLARK) adds credibility.

### Gaps
- There is no direct evidence that any of these features improves Kurdish learning outcomes. They are extrapolated from heritage-language research (mostly on Spanish, Russian and Korean in the US), revitalization case studies and NLP feasibility.
- Licensing terms for Zarok TV, Hano or picture-book publishers, and the open licences of AsoSoft, Pewan, KurdishBLARK and SoraniTTS, must be checked individually before use.

# Fact-check: children.md (how children learn languages; designing kids' language apps safely)

**How this check was done. Read this first.**
- **I could not run any new web searches.** My first three WebSearch calls returned "Web search was not performed: this turn's web search budget is used up (limit: 200 WebSearch calls per turn, shared by every agent in it)". WebFetch is blocked by the egress proxy. I did not try to get around either limit. The sibling fact-checkers in this folder were blocked the same way.
- I used four other kinds of evidence instead:
  1. **The session's saved raw search log.** This is `searches.txt` in the session scratchpad: about 5,200 lines of WebSearch summaries saved from the research agents' transcripts.
     - I read all 35 searches by the children agent and compared them line by line with the notes. **The notes transcribe their snippets faithfully.** I found no copying errors, so the problems below are about corroboration, scope and inference, not transcription.
     - Searches run by *other* agents (motivation_gamification, speech_audio_tech, memory_retention, heritage_minority_kurdish) count as **independent retrievals**. These are still search-engine summaries, not full papers.
  2. **Sibling notes in this folder.** A sibling counts as corroboration only when it is not tagged `[SIB]`, "via children.md" or "VIA SIBLING". Almost every overlapping sibling line turned out to be a copy of children.md.
  3. **Internal consistency.** I checked whether the notes' own snippets support the inferences and recommendations built on them.
  4. **My background knowledge**, labelled **[background, unverified]** every time. It never upgrades a claim to "confirmed", and I invented no URLs for it.
- **Source labels:**
  - *(notes)*: a URL children.md cites. Its content comes from the children agent's snippet.
  - *(independent: agent)*: a different agent found it in its own search this session.
  - *(title only)*: only the result title was seen.
- I did not open any URL listed here.
- **Verdict rules:**
  - **confirmed**: an independent retrieval matches the claim as the notes use it.
  - **overstated**: the core finding may hold, but a number, label, scope or design inference goes beyond the evidence. This can be shown from the notes' own snippets or from an independent retrieval.
  - **refuted**: independent evidence contradicts the claim.
  - **unverified**: nothing independent corroborated it this session. Per the task rules, these get no benefit of the doubt.
- **Most "unverified" verdicts reflect the exhausted search budget, not a suspected error.** The re-checks to run first are listed at the end.

## Omitted counter-evidence

1. **Retrieval practice and spacing do work for school-age children, including in real classrooms** (independent: memory_retention).
   - Agarwal et al. 2021 classroom review: 57% of effects were medium or large.
   - Karpicke et al. 2016: robust effects in 10-year-olds.
   - Primary pupils' fact recall improved for up to 5 weeks.
   - Vlach & Sandhofer: spacing helped 5–7-year-olds.
   
   children.md cites only the Goossens null result, and rec 17 builds on it (row 6). [background, unverified] The same Goossens group also published positive third-grade classroom results.
2. **The video deficit is age-limited, so pre-recorded content works for preschoolers.** The live-interaction studies cover only ages 9–30 months. [background, unverified] A meta-analysis of the video deficit in 0–6-year-olds exists (Strouse & Samson 2021, *Child Development*). As I recall, it found the deficit shrinks with age. Takacs 2015 shows narrated multimedia stories teach 3–6-year-olds (row 1).
3. **Dialogic reading effects are much weaker for 4–5-year-olds and at-risk children** [background, unverified; Mol et al. 2008]. Noble's null result against active controls is in the notes but is not carried into rec 21 (row 2).
4. **Praise does less for children.** In Deci et al., verbal rewards "tended to be less enhancing for children" (independent: motivation_gamification) (row 7).
5. **Gamification raised primary pupils' motivation on average:** g = 0.309, 95% CI 0.033–0.584, very high heterogeneity (Kurnaz & Koçtürk 2025; independent: motivation_gamification). This argues for re-shaping game mechanics in kids' mode, not stripping them out.
6. **Hawar does not mark Kurmanji's aspiration contrasts** (p/pʰ, t/tʰ, k/kʰ, ç/çʰ) (independent: speech_audio_tech). This qualifies "highly letter–sound regular" and the phonics scripts in rec 15 (row 5).
7. **Child speech recognition gets worse with younger age:** up to 35% WER for 4–6-year-olds, and 54% on classroom speech. Whisper has no native Kurdish support, and the best adult Kurmanji result is 10.5% WER after fine-tuning (independent: speech_audio_tech; children search) (row 11).
8. **Sleep seems to consolidate children's learning more than adults'** (independent: memory_retention; 2020 *Scientific Reports* study of 30 children aged 7–12 and 34 adults; summary only). This supports the bedtime song and story "offline missions" in rec 13, which the notes do not connect to any evidence.
9. **Young children do not choose spacing for themselves** (Vlach et al. 2019; title only, independent: memory_retention). Recycling should be scheduled by the app, not left to the child.
10. **Heritage families' app use depends on resources, technical know-how, and parents' and children's attitudes** ("Is there an app for that?", 2018; independent: heritage_minority_kurdish). A 2026 quasi-experiment with Korean heritage families found home media exposure raised vocabulary and enjoyment. Parent onboarding and child-facing media both matter, not parent coaching alone (row 12).
11. **Legal points the notes miss or misstate** (rows 8–9):
    - **COPPA mixed audience:** the status requires the service to be child-directed; children using it is not enough.
    - **Text-message consent:** allowed only when no child data is disclosed.
    - **Voice-recording policy:** the FTC's 2017 policy does not cover pronunciation recording [background].
    - **DSA streaks:** the guideline targets *communication* streaks, the guidelines are non-binding, and Art. 19's small-enterprise exemption probably applies [background].
12. **The EU Digital Fairness Act proposal is expected in Q4 2026** and may target streaks, variable rewards and virtual currencies, with minors treated as vulnerable (independent: motivation_gamification). It could turn some of the kids'-mode design choices into legal duties. The notes mention it only as "not researched".
13. **The 17.4-year "critical period" is model-dependent and contested** [background, unverified; van der Slik et al. 2022 reanalysis].

## 1. Peer interaction and collaborative learning in L2; cooperative vs competitive structures; multiplayer quiz games (Kahoot!)

### Takeaway
Interaction with feedback is one of the best-supported routes to L2 acquisition (Mackey & Goo 2007: d = 0.71 immediate, d = 1.09 delayed). Doing technology-based practice *with a peer* roughly doubled the effect of practising alone in an ASR meta-analysis (g = 0.89 vs 0.44). Cooperative goal structures beat competitive and individualistic ones for achievement and for peer relationships (Johnson & Johnson; Roseth et al. 2008). The precondition is that group rewards depend on *every member's individual learning* (Slavin). In gamification research, *team vs team* (competition combined with collaboration) is the most effective social configuration (Sailer & Homner 2020). Kahoot-style quiz games show mostly positive but heterogeneous effects on learning, classroom atmosphere and anxiety in a 93-study review (Wang & Tahir 2020). Their documented downsides are time pressure, fear of losing and novelty wear-out.

### Cited Findings

**Interaction and peer interaction (L2-specific)**
- **[MA][SIB][OLDER, still the reference] Mackey & Goo 2007**: 28 interaction studies. Interaction vs no interaction gave d = 0.71 on immediate post-tests and **d = 1.09 on delayed post-tests**. The effect was clearer at delay, i.e., it supports retention. (Snippet only, per sibling.) — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Interaction-research-in-SLA-:-A-meta-analysis-and-Mackey-Goo/2b270feebe31f2f01e7c645cc00f8e94d504fb8b); [Lancaster record](https://research.lancaster-university.uk/en/publications/interaction-research-in-sla-a-meta-analysis-and-research-synthesi/)
- **[MA][SIB] Ngo, Chen & Lai, ReCALL (2023/2024)**, ASR for pronunciation: 15 studies, 38 effect sizes. Overall g = 0.69. **Practising alone g = 0.44; with a peer g = 0.89.** Explicit corrective feedback g = 0.86 vs indirect g = 0.50. — [Cambridge/ReCALL](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/recall/article/effectiveness-of-automatic-speech-recognition-in-eslefl-pronunciation-a-metaanalysis/A915444CF252B61D14961D2FE733822D)
- **[MA][SIB] Almusharraf 2024 (JCAL)**: computer-assisted pronunciation training has a medium overall effect, which is **large when used inside classrooms**. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jcal.12974)
- **[MA][SIB] Corrective feedback**: overall d ≈ 0.61–0.64 and durable (Li 2010). Prompts (pushing self-correction) outperform recasts (Lyster & Saito 2010). See `sla_theory.md` Q4.
- **[QE][PK] Sato & Lyster 2012 (SSLA 34(4))**: Japanese university EFL learners were *trained to give each other corrective feedback* (prompts or recasts) during peer interaction. They gained more in **accuracy** than peers who interacted without feedback training, and both interacting groups gained in fluency over a control group. Conclusion: peer interaction becomes a vehicle for accuracy development when learners are taught how to correct each other. (Group sizes and exact effects not re-checked.) — [doi:10.1017/S0272263112000356](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263112000356)
- **[EXP][PK] Sato & Ballinger (eds.) 2016**, *Peer Interaction and Second Language Learning* (John Benjamins, LL&LT 45). Across the chapters, the benefit of peer interaction depends on learners' **"collaborative mindset"** and social relationship. Learners distrust peers' corrections unless the context legitimises them. — [doi:10.1075/lllt.45](https://doi.org/10.1075/lllt.45)
- **[MA][PK] Elabdali 2021 (Journal of Second Language Writing 52)**, collaborative writing: texts written collaboratively were **more accurate** than individually written texts. Evidence that collaboration transfers to later *individual* writing was weaker and less consistent. (Effect sizes not re-checked.) — [doi:10.1016/j.jslw.2021.100788](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jslw.2021.100788)
- **[MA][PK] Ziegler 2016 (SSLA 38(3))**: interaction via synchronous computer-mediated communication (text or voice chat) produced L2 development **comparable to face-to-face interaction**. Differences between the two were small and non-significant. Chat-based interaction is therefore a legitimate substitute when face-to-face is not available. — [doi:10.1017/S027226311500025X](https://doi.org/10.1017/S027226311500025X)
- **[OBS][SIB] Chatbots vs peers (System, 2025)**: learners found chatbot practice **less anxiety-provoking than peer interaction**. — [System (ScienceDirect), 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0346251X2500329X) (snippet only)
- **[MA][PK] Teimouri, Goetze & Plonsky 2019 (SSLA)**: foreign-language anxiety correlates with achievement at about **r = −.36**, based on about 100 samples and about 20k learners. Sample figures are approximate. Social speaking situations are a main anxiety trigger, so public peer settings carry a cost. — [doi:10.1017/S0272263118000311](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263118000311)

**Peer feedback, peer assessment and learning by teaching (general education; transferable)**
- **[MA][PK] Double, McGrane & Hopfenbeck 2020 (Educational Psychology Review 32)**: peer assessment improved academic performance relative to no assessment (about **g = 0.31**, not re-checked) and performed at least as well as teacher assessment. — [doi:10.1007/s10648-019-09510-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09510-3)
- **[MA][PK] Huisman, Saab, van den Broek & van Driel 2019 (Assessment & Evaluation in Higher Education 44(6))**: formative peer feedback improved academic writing more than no feedback (reported about **g = 0.91**) and more than self-assessment (about g = 0.33). It was **not significantly different from teacher feedback**. Numbers recalled, not re-checked. Higher-education writing, not L2-specific. — [doi:10.1080/02602938.2018.1545896](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2018.1545896)
- **[RCT, lab][PK] Fiorella & Mayer 2013 (Contemporary Educational Psychology 38(4))**: students who prepared to teach *and then actually taught* (explained the material on video) outperformed students who only expected to teach, on a delayed test about one week later. This is the "learning by teaching" or "protégé" effect. — [doi:10.1016/j.cedpsych.2013.06.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cedpsych.2013.06.001)
- **[MA][PK] Kobayashi 2019 (Japanese Psychological Research 61(3))**: preparing to teach gives a small learning benefit. Actually teaching after preparing adds a further benefit. (Effect sizes not re-checked.) — [doi:10.1111/jpr.12221](https://doi.org/10.1111/jpr.12221)
- **[MA][PK][OLDER] Cohen, Kulik & Kulik 1982 (AERJ 19(2))**: peer-tutoring programmes benefited both **tutees** (about ES 0.40) and **tutors** (about ES 0.33). Numbers recalled; the evidence base is old. — [doi:10.3102/00028312019002237](https://doi.org/10.3102/00028312019002237)

**Cooperative vs competitive vs individualistic goal structures**
- **[EXP review of meta-analyses][PK] Johnson & Johnson 2009, "An educational psychology success story: Social interdependence theory and cooperative learning" (Educational Researcher 38(5))**:
  - It summarises **over 1,200 studies** comparing cooperative, competitive and individualistic efforts.
  - Cooperation promoted higher achievement, more positive relationships and greater psychological health.
  - The authors' earlier synthesis (Johnson & Johnson 1989) is usually quoted as achievement effect sizes of about **0.67 (cooperative vs competitive)** and **0.64 (cooperative vs individualistic)**. **[OLDER; figures recalled, not re-checked; many primary studies are small, old and from the authors' own group.]**
  - The theory names five conditions for cooperation to work: **positive interdependence, individual accountability, promotive (face-to-face) interaction, social skills, and group processing**.

  — [doi:10.3102/0013189X09339057](https://doi.org/10.3102/0013189X09339057)
- **[MA][PK] Roseth, Johnson & Johnson 2008 (Psychological Bulletin 134(2))**: 148 studies of early adolescents (12–15), more than 17,000 participants, 11 countries. Cooperative goal structures were associated with **higher achievement and more positive peer relationships** than competitive or individualistic structures. Positive peer relationships themselves accounted for a substantial share of the variance in achievement, which supports the idea that "friends help you learn". — [doi:10.1037/0033-2909.134.2.223](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.134.2.223)
- **[MA/narrative synthesis][PK][OLDER but foundational] Slavin 1996 (Contemporary Educational Psychology 21(1))**: cooperative methods that combine **group goals with individual accountability** produced consistent achievement gains (median effect around +0.32). Methods lacking one or both produced near-zero effects (around +0.07). Numbers recalled; the qualitative conclusion is robust and widely replicated. — [doi:10.1006/ceps.1996.0004](https://doi.org/10.1006/ceps.1996.0004)
- **[MA][SIB] Sailer & Homner 2020**: gamification gives cognitive g = 0.49, motivational g = 0.36 and behavioural g = 0.25. **"Combining competition with collaboration"** (teams competing against teams) and game fiction were the significant moderators, especially for behavioural outcomes. The authors recommend letting learners "work together in teams while competing with other teams". — [d-nb.info full text](https://d-nb.info/1202307655/34); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/335189630_The_Gamification_of_Learning_a_Meta-analysis)
- **[MA exists, no figures][SIB]** A 2024 meta-analysis of gamification in *collaborative* learning (Journal of Computers in Education). — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40692-024-00349-4)
- **[QE/OBS][SIB] Leaderboards**: they help high-ranked and highly competitive learners. Low-ranked learners report lower motivation and more stress. In one longitudinal quasi-experiment, leaderboards did not raise optional practice and lowered exam scores. — [ScienceDirect 2024, LID](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1041608024001651); [C&E 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360131524002100); [MDPI 2021](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/13/12/6608)

**Kahoot! and game-based student response systems**
- **[SR][PK] Wang & Tahir 2020, "The effect of using Kahoot! for learning – A literature review" (Computers & Education 149, 103818)**: reviewed **93 studies**.
  - Kahoot! "can have a positive effect" on **learning performance, classroom dynamics, students' and teachers' attitudes, and students' anxiety**.
  - A minority of studies found little or no effect on learning.
  - Problems reported: unreliable internet connections; questions and answers hard to read on a projected screen; being unable to change an answer; **time pressure**; **fear of losing**; difficulty keeping track of players' nicknames.
  - Most included studies are short, single-classroom and self-report-heavy.

  — [doi:10.1016/j.compedu.2020.103818](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2020.103818)
- **[QE][PK] Wang 2015, "The wear out effect of a game-based student response system" (Computers & Education 82)**: Kahoot! was used repeatedly over about five months in a university course. Engagement, motivation and perceived learning declined from first use but stayed positive. The "wear-out" was real but modest. — [doi:10.1016/j.compedu.2014.11.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2014.11.004)
- **[MA][PK] Hunsu, Adesope & Bayly 2016 (Computers & Education 94)**: clicker or audience-response systems, the non-game ancestor of Kahoot!, had a **small positive effect on cognitive outcomes** (about g = 0.2; recalled) and a larger effect on non-cognitive outcomes such as engagement and self-efficacy. — [doi:10.1016/j.compedu.2015.11.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2015.11.013)
- **[QE][SIB] Novelty effect, Rodrigues et al. 2022**: gamification effects declined after about 4 weeks and recovered somewhat by weeks 6–10 (a "familiarization effect"). — [Springer, IJETHE 2022](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s41239-021-00314-6)
- **[MA][SIB] Kurnaz & Koçtürk 2025**: in K-12, gamification's motivational effect was **smallest for primary-school children (g = 0.309)** and largest for secondary students (g = 1.015). — [Wiley, Psychology in the Schools](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/pits.70056)

### Inferences
- The strongest social lever in the SLA evidence is **interaction with feedback that pushes self-correction**, not social presence as such. Multiplayer modes that only race through multiple-choice items capture engagement, not the interaction effect. Modes that make players *produce* Kurdish for each other and *negotiate meaning* (describe-and-guess, information-gap, co-writing) capture it.
- **Peer correction works only when learners are taught how to give it** (Sato & Lyster) **and trust it** (Sato & Ballinger). An app can supply both: a short "how to help a friend" micro-lesson, structured feedback buttons, and dictionary or model-answer anchors that make peer corrections verifiable.
- **Team play must carry individual accountability.** If a team score can be earned by one strong player, weaker players free-ride and learn nothing (Slavin). Team scores should aggregate each member's *own* answers or *improvement*, and every member should get turns.
- **Default to cooperative and team modes; make individual competition opt-in.** That is where the effect sizes point. It also protects children, for whom gamification has the weakest motivational effect (g = 0.31), and anxious or low-confidence adult learners (anxiety r ≈ −.36 with achievement).
- **Kahoot-style games are good for engagement and retrieval practice, not proven as teaching.** Their known failure modes (time pressure, fear of losing, wear-out) are design parameters Hevalo controls: timer length, elimination, visible ranks, and format rotation.
- **"Learning by teaching"** suggests features where learners *explain* Kurdish to someone else, for example a child teaching a parent, or a heritage speaker explaining a word in the feed. This is an inference from general-education evidence, not L2-specific data.

### Gaps
- **No Kahoot!-specific meta-analysis with a pooled effect size** could be retrieved. Several 2021–2025 meta-analyses of "game-based student response systems" or "digital quiz games" likely exist, but I could not search. Treat Wang & Tahir (2020) as the review baseline, not the latest word.
- **Peer-interaction meta-analyses specific to L2** (beyond Mackey & Goo) were not retrieved, e.g., Keck et al. 2006 on task-based interaction, or any 2020s synthesis of L2 peer feedback. Lead: Keck, Iberri-Shea, Tracy-Ventura & Wa-Mbaleka (2006), in Norris & Ortega (eds.), *Synthesizing Research on Language Learning and Teaching*, John Benjamins. URL not verified.
- **Lead [PK, URL not verified]:** Lin, H. (2014), "Establishing an empirical link between computer-mediated communication (CMC) and SLA: A meta-analysis of the research", *Language Learning & Technology* 18(3). I recall a medium overall effect of CMC interventions, but the number was not re-checked.
- No study tests **cooperative vs competitive multiplayer modes inside a language app** with learning outcomes, and none exists for Kurdish.
- 2025–2026 L2 cooperative-learning meta-analyses (e.g., in EFL contexts) could not be retrieved.

## 2. Tandem and language exchange, native-speaker interaction, conversation clubs and mentoring by fluent speakers

### Takeaway
Synchronous chat or voice interaction produces L2 development comparable to face-to-face interaction (Ziegler 2016). Peer interaction with feedback helps retention (Section 1). The specific *tandem / language-exchange* literature is older and mostly qualitative. It consistently reports gains in motivation, intercultural competence and confidence, and **little spontaneous focus on form unless the exchange is structured**. Robust proficiency evidence is scarce. No independent peer-reviewed outcome data on HelloTalk or Tandem were retrievable this session. For Kurdish, classic two-way tandem is asymmetric: few fluent and literate Kurmancî/Soranî writers, many learners. Heritage speakers have oral skills but weak literacy. A **mentoring model** (fluent speaker ↔ learner, as in Indigenous "master–apprentice" programmes) and **skill swaps** (oral speaker ↔ literate learner; Kurdish ↔ German or English) fit better than a generic exchange marketplace.

### Cited Findings
- **[MA][PK] Ziegler 2016**: synchronous computer-mediated interaction gives L2 gains comparable to face-to-face interaction (see Section 1). — [doi:10.1017/S027226311500025X](https://doi.org/10.1017/S027226311500025X)
- **[MA][SIB] Mackey & Goo 2007**: interaction effects grow at delayed post-test (d = 1.09). — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Interaction-research-in-SLA-:-A-meta-analysis-and-Mackey-Goo/2b270feebe31f2f01e7c645cc00f8e94d504fb8b)
- **[EXP][SIB] "Beginner trap" in minority-language revitalisation**: Māori and Welsh efforts produce many starters but a **low transition rate from basic learners to independent use**. The proven successes (Te Kōhanga Reo, Pūnana Leo) are **immersion with fluent speakers**, centred on children and families, not apps. — [Māori language revival (secondary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_language_revival); [Ka Wai Ola](https://kawaiola.news/hoonaauao/aha-punana-leo-the-preschool-program-that-inspired-a-movement/); [ResearchGate: Revitalizing the Māori language](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/293117325_Revitalizing_the_Maori_language_A_focus_on_educational_reform)
- **[EXP][SIB] Contingent live interaction matters for young children**: toddlers learned from live, socially contingent video chat but not from pre-recorded video (Roseberry et al. 2014; Myers et al. 2017). — [PMC3962808](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3962808); [Newswise (Myers 2017)](https://www.newswise.com/articles/new-study-of-toddlers-sheds-light-on-value-of-facetime-video-chat-as-meaningful-interaction)
- **[OBS][SIB] Zoom-mediated heritage-language maintenance** among Farsi-speaking migrants in Australia (mixed methods, 2026). Online synchronous gatherings are an established diaspora practice. — [Asian-Pacific J. of Second and Foreign Language Education 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40862-026-00441-5)
- **[OBS][PK] Heritage-language anxiety, Sevinç & Dewaele 2018 (International Journal of Bilingualism 22(2))**: among Turkish immigrants in the Netherlands, later generations felt notable anxiety when speaking Turkish. This was tied to fear of negative evaluation by family and community members, i.e., being corrected or mocked by "real" speakers. Directly relevant to Kurdish diaspora families, many of whom also came from Turkey. — [doi:10.1177/1367006916661635](https://doi.org/10.1177/1367006916661635)
- **[OBS][SIB] Chatbot practice is less anxiety-provoking than peer interaction** (System 2025). An AI partner can act as a rehearsal step before human conversation. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0346251X2500329X)
- **[MA][SIB] AI dialogue partners**: dialogue-based CALL systems for speaking give g = 0.61 (ReCALL three-level MA); chatbots g = 0.608 (Lyu 2025); generative-AI chatbots ES = 0.576 (Li 2025), with large moderation by very short (1–7 day) interventions, a novelty warning. — [ReCALL](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/recall/article/dialoguebased-computerassisted-language-learning-systems-for-second-language-speaking-development-a-threelevel-metaanalysis/31847710516602398819C5E594038E7B); [Lyu 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ijal.12668); [Li 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jcal.70060?af=R)
- **[REG][SIB] Stranger contact and minors**: Ofcom's Protection of Children Codes (enforced from 25 July 2025) say adult strangers should not be able to contact children by default. The EU DSA Article 28 guidelines (14 July 2025) require minors' accounts to be private by default, with account recommendations off. Open language-exchange matching is therefore incompatible with child users. — [Ofcom statement](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/statement-protecting-children-from-harms-online); [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors)

### Inferences
- **Real conversation with fluent speakers is the missing bridge** from app learning to independent use. That is where the "beginner trap" bites, and where interaction effects (which grow over time) live. Hevalo already has friends and DMs, so it has the plumbing. What is missing is *structure*: matching, role cards, topic prompts tied to course units, and turn-taking timers.
- **Unstructured exchange under-delivers.** My recollection of the tandem literature (see Gaps) is that partners rarely correct each other spontaneously. Conversations drift to the stronger shared language unless there is a 50/50 time split and explicit "correct me" norms. Build these into the product rather than relying on goodwill.
- **Asymmetric supply calls for a mentor model, not a marketplace.** Kurdish has a deep pool of oral speakers (elders, in-region speakers schooled in Turkish, Arabic or Persian) and a shallow pool of literate, standard-writing speakers. A "Hevalê Ziman" (language buddy) or *mamoste* (teacher) programme can pair a verified fluent volunteer with 1–3 learners for short scheduled voice sessions. It can also offer **skill swaps**:
  - a heritage speaker practises oral Kurmancî with an in-region speaker;
  - the in-region speaker gets help with German, English or Dutch, or with Hawar literacy practice.
- **Fluent speakers can also cause harm.** Heritage-language anxiety is driven by negative evaluation from "real" speakers (Sevinç & Dewaele). Mentors need a short code of conduct and feedback training: prompts not ridicule, no dialect shaming (Kurmancî vs Soranî vs regional forms), no "you're not a real Kurd".
- **AI conversation as a stepping stone, not a substitute.** Learners can rehearse a task with an AI partner, which is less anxiety-provoking, then do it with a human mentor or friend. This fits both the anxiety evidence and the interaction evidence.
- **Conversation clubs** (scheduled small-group voice rooms by level and dialect, hosted by a trained volunteer) are a lower-cost way to scale fluent-speaker contact than 1:1 matching. Direct evidence is lacking (Gaps), so measure them.

### Gaps
- **No independent, peer-reviewed outcome study of HelloTalk, Tandem or similar exchange apps** could be retrieved. I am aware only of small qualitative and self-report studies, which I cannot cite reliably without search. Safety problems on open exchange apps (unwanted romantic or sexual contact) are widely discussed anecdotally, but I have no citable source.
- **Leads from prior knowledge, details and URLs not verified:**
  - Little & Brammerts (eds.) 1996, *A guide to language learning in tandem via the Internet* (CLCS Occasional Paper 46, Trinity College Dublin). It states the two tandem principles, **reciprocity** and **learner autonomy**.
  - O'Rourke, B. 2005, "Form-focused interaction in online tandem learning", *CALICO Journal* 22(3). It reports limited spontaneous focus on form in eTandem chat.
  - O'Dowd, R. 2018, "From telecollaboration to virtual exchange", *Journal of Virtual Exchange* 1.
  - The EVALUATE Group 2019, *Evaluating the impact of virtual exchange on initial teacher education: a European policy experiment* (Research-publishing.net). This was a large Erasmus+ trial; I recall gains mainly in intercultural and digital-pedagogical competence.
  - Akiyama & Cunningham 2018, "Synthesizing the practice of SCMC-based telecollaboration: A scoping review", *CALICO Journal* 35(1). I recall it found most studies measured intercultural or affective outcomes rather than linguistic development.
- **Lead [PK, URL not verified]:** the **Master–Apprentice Language Learning Program** (Leanne Hinton; Advocates for Indigenous California Language Survival, from the 1990s). It pairs a fluent elder with a learner for many hours a week of immersion activity. Its evidence is programmatic or expert, not experimental, but it is the closest model to "mentoring by fluent speakers" for a minority language.
- **No evidence was found on conversation clubs** (online or offline) as an L2 intervention.
- **No Kurdish-specific data** on willingness of fluent speakers to volunteer, or on diaspora learners' speaking anxiety.

## 3. Family and intergenerational learning; parent–child co-learning; classroom and teacher features; Saturday and community schools

### Takeaway
For heritage children, the strongest predictor of keeping the language is **how much the parents (ideally both) use it at home** (De Houwer 2007; n = 1,899 families). Interventions that **coach the caregiver** have large effects on caregiver behaviour (d = 1.01) and moderate effects on child expressive language (d = 0.41). Those child effects shrink against active controls (Noble et al. 2019: g = 0.03 vs active controls). Young children learn from *live, contingent* interaction, including video chat with grandparents, not from passive video. Joint media engagement (co-use) adds learning. On the classroom side, Kurdish heritage schooling exists but is small:
- about 3,000 pupils in Germany's *Herkunftssprachenunterricht* (HSU);
- Swedish *modersmål* teaching;
- about 3,000 students in KRG–diaspora online schools;
- a fast-growing elective in Turkey: 59,372 pupils in 2025–26, with almost no new teachers.

Technology tends to work better when embedded in classrooms (CAPT large in-class effect; the ASR peer effect). Complementary-school research supports flexible bilingual practice ("translanguaging") over strict target-language-only rules.

### Cited Findings

**Family and intergenerational**
- **[OBS, large survey][SIB][OLDER-2007, still a landmark] De Houwer 2007 (Applied Psycholinguistics 28(3))**: 1,899 Flemish families with a child aged 6–10. Children most often spoke the minority language where **both parents used it and at most one parent used the majority language**. "One parent–one language" was neither necessary nor sufficient. — [Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/applied-psycholinguistics/article/abs/parental-language-input-patterns-and-childrens-bilingual-use/821A5852222197491F4E7ABC8AA4B099)
- **[SR/OBS][SIB] Family language policy (FLP) research 2020–2026**: children show more agency to keep the heritage language where parents strongly advocate for it and use it. Older generations prioritise the heritage language; younger ones lean to the dominant language. A 2025 paper describes a "digital shift" in parental heritage-language strategies. — [Mapping FLP 2020–2024 (T&F 2026)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/23311886.2026.2615946); [Digital shift (JMMD 2025)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01434632.2025.2491607); [FLP systematic review (JMMD)](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01434632.2020.1858302)
- **[OBS][SIB] Parents' perceived value of the heritage language predicts children's skills** (Languages 2024, 9(3), 80; title only). — [MDPI/DOI](https://doi.org/10.3390/languages9030080)
- **[MA of 19 RCTs][SIB] Dowdall et al. 2020**: caregiver book-sharing interventions, N = 2,594 children aged 1–6. Expressive language d = 0.41, receptive d = 0.26. **Caregiver book-sharing skill d = 1.01.** Child effects depended on dosage. — [Hope repository](https://hira.hope.ac.uk/id/eprint/3756/)
- **[MA][SIB] Noble et al. 2019**: shared reading overall g = 0.194, but **g = 0.028 (n.s.) against active controls**, and effects at follow-up were not significant. This qualifies the headline shared-reading claims. — [Liverpool repository](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3054189/)
- **[EXP][SIB][OLDER-2011, framework] Joint media engagement (Takeuchi & Stevens 2011)**: children learn more when adults co-use media, through shared meaning-making, links to prior knowledge, and parents' questions and word clarifications. — [The new coviewing (PDF)](https://www.thechildrensmediafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/02/Takeuchi-2011-The-new-coviewing.pdf)
- **[EXP][SIB] Contingent video chat** works for toddlers; pre-recorded video does not (Roseberry 2014; Myers 2017). — [PMC3962808](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3962808)
- **[QE][SIB] Korean heritage children (Language and Education, 2026)**: narrative media chosen around children's interests, used at home with mothers, increased enjoyment, vocabulary and autonomy. Mothers reported more confidence in supporting the language. **Children began using Korean with grandparents and tried teaching words to family members.** Effect sizes not visible in the snippet. — [T&F 2026](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09500782.2026.2638212)
- **[OBS][SIB] Grandparent–grandchild digital interaction (ILLT 2026)**: videoconferencing, digital stories and cartoons supported intergenerational transmission. In a Polish case they helped vocabulary and enthusiasm. — [T&F 2026](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17501229.2026.2614753)
- **[QUAL][SIB] Whether heritage families use apps** depends on (a) resources and know-how, (b) attitudes to the heritage language, and (c) attitudes to games and apps ('Is there an app for that?', JMMD 2018). — [T&F 2018](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01434632.2018.1502776)
- **[OBS][SIB] Heritage learners' motivation** ranks (1) understanding one's heritage, then (2) communicating with family and friends (Carreira & Kagan 2011, n ≈ 1,800, older but still the reference survey). — [Carreira & Kagan survey PDF](https://hwpi.harvard.edu/files/heritagespanish/files/carreira_kagan_survey_0.pdf)
- **[RCT lab / MA][PK] Learning by teaching** (Fiorella & Mayer 2013; Kobayashi 2019; see Section 1) supports features where the child teaches a parent, or a learner explains to a peer. — [doi:10.1016/j.cedpsych.2013.06.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cedpsych.2013.06.001); [doi:10.1111/jpr.12221](https://doi.org/10.1111/jpr.12221)

**Classroom, teacher and community-school context (Kurdish)**

Sweden's municipalities must offer mother-tongue instruction (MTI) when **at least 5 eligible pupils** and a teacher are available. Sweden had Kurdish MTI from 1975 and teacher training in Kurmanji and Sorani from 1984. — [Language on the Move](https://languageonthemove.com/kurdish-heritage-language-education/)
- **[NEWS][SIB]** A KRG Ministry of Education–Kurdistani Diaspora Confederation project has registered **30 Kurdish schools abroad**, with about **3,000 students online**. — [Kurdistan24](https://www.kurdistan24.net/en/story/893463/kurdish-now-taught-as-formal-subject-in-sweden-and-germany-under-joint-kdc-krg-initiative); [Sema TV, Feb 2026](https://sematv.net/en/2026/02/5-82)
- **[NEWS][SIB] Turkey's Kurdish electives**:
  - 2024–25: 30,862 pupils chose Kurmancî and 4,554 Zazakî.
  - 2025–26: a record **59,372** (50,809 Kurmancî + 8,563 Zazakî).
  - Supply: **only 10 of 20,000 new teaching posts went to Kurdish in 2024**.

  — [Turkish Minute, June 2025](https://www.turkishminute.com/2025/06/03/kurdish-elective-course-enrollment-hits-record-high-in-turkey-as-barriers-remain/); [Kurdistan24](https://www.kurdistan24.net/en/story/909729/kurdish-language-demand-surges-in-t%C3%BCrkiyes-schools)

ASR practice with a peer gives g = 0.89 vs 0.44 alone (Ngo et al.). — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jcal.12974); [ReCALL](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/recall/article/effectiveness-of-automatic-speech-recognition-in-eslefl-pronunciation-a-metaanalysis/A915444CF252B61D14961D2FE733822D)
- **[QUAL][PK] Creese & Blackledge 2010 (Modern Language Journal 94(1))**: ethnographic studies of UK complementary (Saturday) schools for Gujarati, Turkish, Cantonese, Mandarin and Bengali. Teachers and pupils used **"flexible bilingualism" / translanguaging**, moving between English and the heritage language, as a resource for learning and identity. Strict separation of languages did not reflect effective practice. This is the core reference for "translanguaging" in community schools. — [doi:10.1111/j.1540-4781.2009.00986.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-4781.2009.00986.x)

Start from what they know; deductive "noticing" grammar suits them. — [Frontiers in Education 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2020.00053/pdf)
- **[REG][SIB] Parental-control transparency**: the UK ICO Children's Code (Standard 11) requires that the child gets age-appropriate information and **an obvious sign when they are being monitored**. — [ICO code](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/)

### Inferences
- **The highest-leverage family feature is one that raises adult Kurdish talk at home**, not more child drill (De Houwer; FLP research). Examples:
  - weekly "family phrase" cards in Kurmancî or Soranî, with audio, for parents to use at dinner, bedtime or in the car;
  - short parent-coaching micro-lessons on *how* to read and talk with a child in Kurdish (Dowdall: caregiver skill d = 1.01);
  - prompts sized for semi-speaker parents, who are common in the diaspora.
- **Grandparents are the fluent speakers** in many families, often living in the region. A family circle where grandparents record voice answers or read a story live by video turns them into the contingent tutors that young children learn from. Many elders cannot read Hawar or Sorani script, so these features must be **audio-first, with no text entry needed**.
- **Child-teaches-parent** ("Teach Dayê/Bavo 3 words today") uses learning-by-teaching and reverses the shame dynamic for semi-speaker parents. The Korean 2026 study shows children spontaneously do this.
- **Classrooms are where safe peer interaction for children is solvable.** Children connect only with classmates via a teacher-issued class code. Classroom embedding also raises effects (CAPT in-class). Given ~59k elective pupils in Turkey and a severe teacher shortage, **teacher-light classroom tools** may be Hevalo's largest reach channel for children:
  - ready-made assignable units;
  - auto-graded homework;
  - projector-friendly live games.
- **Saturday and community schools need cheap coordination tools more than content alone**: class roster, assignment, attendance-free progress, printable worksheets, and a class-only story wall. They also need a UI that tolerates mixing languages (translanguaging) rather than forcing Kurdish-only screens on beginners.
- **Dialect and script must be per class and per family**: a Kurmancî class in Germany, a Soranî class in Sweden, a Zazakî elective group in Turkey. Teacher tools should not assume one standard.

### Gaps
- **No published effect data on parent dashboards, family circles or classroom modes inside language apps** were found (sibling also flagged this).
- **Leads [PK, URL not verified]:**
  - Maylor et al. 2010, *Impact of supplementary schools on pupils' attainment: an investigation into what factors contribute to educational improvements*, DCSF Research Report RR210 (England). Mixed evidence on attainment; positive effects on confidence and identity.
  - Li Wei's and Creese & Blackledge's wider complementary-school work.
  - Literature on Kurdish community schools in the UK, Germany and Sweden specifically. I know of no peer-reviewed outcome study.
- **"Duolingo for Schools" and similar teacher tools**: I am aware these exist (prior knowledge), but no usage or outcome data were retrievable.
- **No study on Kurdish heritage families' use of apps**, or on Kurdish grandparent–grandchild video-chat practices.

## 4. User-generated content (stories, poems, pictures) as learning material; reading with glosses; community moderation and safety at scale

### Takeaway
Reading is a well-evidenced acquisition route: extensive reading d ≈ 0.46–0.57. **Glosses nearly double incidental word learning** (45.3% vs 26.6% of target words immediately; 33.4% vs 19.8% delayed). Multiple-choice "guess-first" glosses work best, and glosses in the learner's L1 beat L2 glosses by about 4 points. So a community feed of short stories and poems *with tap-to-gloss* and level tagging can become a meaning-focused input engine. Two cautions apply:
1. For young children, interactive extras inside stories (hotspots, pop-up dictionaries) **distract**; narration and matching animation help.
2. UGC needs quality control and moderation. The community-design evidence is clear:
   - posting rules shown to newcomers raise both compliance and participation (Matias 2019: +70% newcomer participation);
   - harsh, automated rejection of newcomers' good-faith contributions drives them away (Wikipedia's decline; Halfaker et al. 2013);
   - volunteer content can build whole courses but is hard to sustain (Duolingo ended its volunteer contributor programme in 2021).

Minors raise hard legal requirements: private by default, no stranger contact.

### Cited Findings

**Reading and glossing (meta-analytic)**
- **[MA][SIB] Glossing, Yanagisawa, Webb & Uchihara 2020 (SSLA)**: 42 studies, 359 effect sizes, N = 3,802.
  - Glossed reading 45.3% / 33.4% (immediate/delayed) vs unglossed 26.6% / 19.8%.
  - **Multiple-choice glosses most effective.**
  - **L1 glosses about +4.0 points over L2 glosses.**
  - No significant difference between textual, pictorial and auditory glosses.

  — [ERIC EJ1251024](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1251024); [author preprint](https://takumiuchihara.weebly.com/uploads/1/2/3/7/123756989/yanagisawa-webb-uchihara-2019-glossing_meta-analysis.pdf)
- **[MA][SIB] Extensive reading**: Nakanishi 2015, d = 0.46 vs controls (34 studies, N = 3,942); Jeon & Day 2016, d = 0.57 (49 studies). ER is more effective for adults than for young learners. — [Wiley/TESOL Quarterly](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/tesq.157); [ERIC full text](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1117026.pdf)
- **[MA/correlational][SIB] Encounters**: number of encounters correlates with incidental vocabulary learning at r = .34. Moderators include spacing, visual support and engagement (Uchihara, Webb & Yanagisawa 2019). — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/lang.12343)
- **[EXP/correlational][SIB] Coverage**: about 95% of running words known for minimal comprehension, about 98% for comfortable unassisted reading. — see `sla_theory.md` Q2
- **[MA][SIB][OLDER-2015, still the main synthesis] Takacs, Swart & Bus 2015 (RER)**: 43 studies, 2,147 children. Multimedia features in digital storybooks (animation, music, sound) helped: story comprehension g+ = 0.17, expressive vocabulary g+ = 0.20. **Interactive features (hotspots, games, dictionaries) distracted**, most of all for children from less stimulating home environments. — [SAGE PDF](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.3102/0034654314566989)
- **[MA][SIB]** For incidental vocabulary, **narrative texts beat expository texts**, and **material written for L2 learners beats native material**. — see `sla_theory.md` Implications A1

**Peer feedback on writing (relevant to feed posts)**
- **[MA][PK] Huisman et al. 2019**: peer feedback on writing ≈ teacher feedback, and clearly better than none (see Section 1). — [doi:10.1080/02602938.2018.1545896](https://doi.org/10.1080/02602938.2018.1545896)
- **[MA][PK] Elabdali 2021**: collaborative writing improves the accuracy of jointly written texts (Section 1). — [doi:10.1016/j.jslw.2021.100788](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jslw.2021.100788)

**Volunteer and community content for minority languages**
- **[official][SIB]** Duolingo **ended its Volunteer Contributor program in March 2021**. More than 1,000 volunteers had built dozens of courses since 2013; Navajo, Hawaiian, Welsh and Irish "would not exist" without them. Duolingo moved to paid content creation and set up a $4M "Language Impact" fund for past contributors. — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/ending-honoring-our-volunteer-contributor-program-2/) **[SUPERSEDED model: the volunteer-built-course model at Duolingo]**
- **[NEWS][SIB]** Duolingo **paused Welsh course development in 2023**, when the course had more than 2M starters and 659k active learners, to prioritise high-demand languages. — [Senedd research brief](https://business.senedd.wales/documents/s150940/Research%20brief.html?CT=2)

**Community design and moderation (online communities generally)**
- **[RCT, field][PK] Matias 2019 (PNAS 116(20))**: a field experiment across 2,190 discussions in r/science. **Displaying the community rules** (as a pinned announcement) raised the probability that **newcomers complied with the rules by more than 8 percentage points** and **raised newcomer participation by 70% on average**. Clear norms both reduced harassment-type violations and grew participation. — [doi:10.1073/pnas.1813486116](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1813486116)
- **[OBS][PK][OLDER-2013, foundational] Halfaker, Geiger, Morgan & Riedl 2013 (American Behavioral Scientist 57(5))**: Wikipedia's newcomer retention fell after 2007. A key cause was quality-control machinery (fast automated and semi-automated reverts, warnings) that **rejected good-faith newcomers' contributions**, so even desirable newcomers left. This is the canonical warning that over-aggressive moderation of beginners' contributions kills participation. — [doi:10.1177/0002764212469365](https://doi.org/10.1177/0002764212469365)
- **[QE][PK] Morgan & Halfaker 2018 (OpenSym)**: inviting Wikipedia newcomers to the "Teahouse", a friendly Q&A space staffed by experienced hosts, **modestly improved short-term newcomer retention**. The effect size was small; details not re-checked. — [doi:10.1145/3233391.3233544](https://doi.org/10.1145/3233391.3233544)
- **[MA][SIB] Overjustification**: expected, engagement-contingent tangible rewards lowered free-choice intrinsic motivation by d = −0.40, worse for children. Informational positive feedback raised it (d = +0.33). This applies directly to paying in-app currency for posting creative content. — [Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999 (PDF)](https://home.ubalt.edu/tmitch/642/articles%20syllabus/Deci%20Koestner%20Ryan%20meta%20IM%20psy%20bull%2099.pdf)
- **[QUAL][SIB] Gamification misuse**: Mogavi et al. 2022 (9 years of Duolingo forum posts plus 15 interviews) found learners fixating on XP and streaks and gaming leagues. — [arXiv 2203.16175](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.16175)

**Automated moderation for Kurdish**
- **[SIB]** No rigorous, peer-reviewed benchmark of 2025–2026 frontier LLMs on Kurmanji and Sorani was found. The moderation quality of provider moderation endpoints on Kurdish text is unknown and likely weak for a low-resource language. Smaller models might work for classification (e.g., moderation) *after validation*. — see `ai_llm_tutoring.md` §3; [arXiv 2511.10664 (low-resource LLM benchmark; Kurdish not covered)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.10664)

**Safety and law for UGC with minors**
- **[REG][SIB] EU DSA Article 28 guidelines (14 July 2025)**: minors' accounts private by default; account recommendations off by default; streaks, read receipts and push notifications disabled by default; protection against manipulative design. — [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors)
- **[REG][SIB] UK Online Safety Act, Ofcom Protection of Children Codes**: enforcement from 25 July 2025; no contact from adult strangers by default. — [Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/statement-protecting-children-from-harms-online)
- **[REG][SIB] UK ICO Children's Code**: high privacy by default, profiling off, no nudges to share more. — [ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/)
- **[REG][SIB] US COPPA 2025 amendments** (compliance deadline 22 April 2026): separate parental consent for third-party disclosures; written security programme; written retention policy; expanded personal-information definition, including biometrics. Audio of a child's voice is personal information. — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-finalizes-changes-childrens-privacy-rule-limiting-companies-ability-monetize-kids-data)
- **[SIB gap]** Whether a learning platform with user-to-user content is in scope of the UK Online Safety Act "user-to-user service" duties needs legal confirmation. The sibling infers that it likely is.

### Inferences
- **The community feed can become Hevalo's graded-reading library**, which is the biggest content gap for a less-resourced language, on these conditions:
  - every post is **tap-to-gloss** from the 400k-word dictionary, shown in the reader's UI language, with an optional "guess first" multiple-choice mode;
  - posts are **auto-levelled by the reader's known-word coverage** ("readable for you": about 95% or more known);
  - glossed words flow into spaced review.
  
  This connects UGC to the strongest input evidence.
- **UGC quality varies.** Spelling in Hawar and Sorani is not consistently standardised among writers, and many fluent speakers were never schooled in Kurdish. Two layers help:
  - an **editor-curated "graded community stories" shelf**: stories accepted, lightly corrected, levelled and recorded with audio, then reusable as lesson content;
  - **writing tools** for authors: diacritic helper for ê î û ç ş; dictionary-backed spell suggestions; dialect and script tags.
- **Moderation design should follow the community-science evidence**:
  - show short, friendly rules at the first post (Matias);
  - welcome newcomers' first posts with a human or host response, not silent removal (Halfaker; Teahouse);
  - explain rejections and offer a fix-and-resubmit path;
  - keep rapid takedown for safety violations.
- **Kurdish-specific moderation risks** (inference, unsourced; see Gaps):
  - political content (party symbols, imagery or slogans that are legal in some countries and criminalised in others);
  - dialect and script conflict (Kurmancî vs Soranî vs Zazakî "real Kurdish" disputes);
  - sectarian or national tensions in Arabic-, Turkish- and Persian-language comments.

  Rules should be explicit about dialect respect and should be enforced by moderators who cover multiple dialects and the UI languages. LLM moderation of Kurdish text cannot be trusted unvalidated. Use it only for triage, with human review.
- **Do not pay Zêr per post or per like.** Use *informational, unexpected* recognition instead: "featured story", "story used in a lesson", "your poem was read 120 times", and author credit on reused content (Deci et al.; Duolingo's contributor-credit precedent).
- **Children's mode**: no public feed. Use a class- or family-only story wall instead. Kids' story reader uses narration and animation, *not* tap-hotspots (Takacs). Tap-to-gloss can be on for older children and teens reading on their own.

### Gaps
- **No study of UGC feeds as L2 reading material** inside a learning app was retrievable. The link between glossing and ER evidence and a community feed is an inference.
- **Leads [PK, URL not verified]:** Kraut & Resnick 2012, *Building Successful Online Communities: Evidence-Based Social Design* (MIT Press), the main synthesis of design claims on newcomer socialisation, norms, moderation and commitment. Also research on Lang-8 and HiNative-style peer-correction communities, whose current status I could not verify.
- **No Kurdish offensive-language or hate-speech classifier** with published accuracy was identified this session. Some Sorani datasets may exist, but this was not verified.
- **Legal status of specific political symbols** in Germany and Turkey, as relevant to Kurdish UGC moderation, was not researched this session. It needs legal review.

## 5. Social accountability (friend streaks, study buddies, shared goals): evidence on retention

### Takeaway
Social accountability reliably raises *engagement*. The best general evidence is that **progress monitoring improves goal attainment (d = 0.40) and works better when progress is reported to others or made public** (Harkin et al. 2016). Duolingo reports that **about one-third of its daily active users have a Friend Streak** (FY2025 10-K) and that matched weekly leagues raised learning time by 17% (vendor). There is **no peer-reviewed evidence that friend streaks or study buddies improve *learning* outcomes**, and Duolingo has not published a lift figure for friend features that I could retrieve. Streaks work through loss aversion and goal adoption. A broken streak demotivates unless it is repairable. Messaging streaks are named by EU regulators as an excessive-use risk for minors and should be off by default for them. Cooperative structures (Section 1) and positive peer relationships (Roseth 2008) are the theoretical basis for "learning with friends"; social isolation is a documented driver of app attrition.

### Cited Findings
- **[MA][SIB] Harkin et al. 2016 (Psychological Bulletin)**: 138 studies, N = 19,951. Prompting progress monitoring improved goal attainment, d = 0.40 (95% CI 0.32–0.48). Effects were **larger when progress was reported or made public** and when it was physically recorded. Mostly health studies. — [White Rose eprints](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/91437/)
- **[VENDOR / SEC filing][SIB] Duolingo FY2025 10-K**: as of 31 Dec 2025, about **one-third of DAUs have a Friend Streak**; about 15M DAUs had a 365+ day streak and about 43M a 7+ day streak. — [Duolingo FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1562088/000162828026012494/duol-20251231.htm)
- **[VENDOR][SIB] Duolingo leagues** (ex-CPO account): users matched on prior-week engagement. Total learning time **+17%**; highly engaged learners **tripled**; D1 and D7 retention improved. — [Lenny's Newsletter (Mazal)](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)
- **[VENDOR][SIB] Duolingo streak experiments** (600+ over about 4 years): every loosening made engagement go up — two freezes, one lesson keeps the streak (7+ day streaks up >40%), streak wager (D7 +14%). Figures via secondary summaries of Duolingo talks. — [Recall summary of Lenny's Podcast](https://www.getrecall.ai/summary/lennys-podcast/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks-or-jackson-shuttleworth-group-pm-retention-team)
- **[QE/RCT][SIB] Silverman & Barasch 2023**: showing an intact streak raises later engagement versus showing a broken one. The demotivation is amplified when the break is self-attributed and **attenuated when the streak can be repaired**. — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/On-or-Off-Track:-How-(Broken)-Streaks-Affect-Silverman-Barasch/edfc39fd411eec3313d9a70bdf87acbd88d67bfc)
- **[RCT, megastudy][SIB] Milkman et al. 2021 (Nature)**: 61,293 gym members, 54 interventions. The best intervention gave a small bonus for **returning after a missed workout** (+27% visits). Re-entry design matters. — [Penn Today](https://penntoday.upenn.edu/news/wharton-study-best-ways-boost-workout-habits)
- **[OBS][SIB] Harms**: Snapchat streaks were linked to problematic smartphone use and fear of missing out among early adolescents. Commentators describe peer pressure and "digital stress". — [ScienceDirect 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2772503023000476)
- **[REG][SIB] DSA Article 28 guidelines**: communication or messaging **streaks** are among the features to disable by default for minors. — [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors). The UK ICO Children's Code names "likes and streaks" as reward-loop nudges to avoid for children (sibling notes the quoted wording is from the 2019 draft). — [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/ico-bans-use-of-nudge-techniques-in-new-draft-guidelines/)
- **[QUAL][SIB] Attrition drivers** in language apps include **social isolation**, reduced motivation, anxiety, lack of success and lack of guidance. — [McGill thesis](https://escholarship.mcgill.ca/downloads/k643b581s?locale=en)
- **[MA][SIB] Li, Hew & Du 2024**: gamification raised perceived **autonomy and relatedness**, with minimal impact on perceived competence (g = 0.257 on intrinsic motivation). Social features mainly feed relatedness. — [Springer ETR&D](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11423-023-10337-7)
- **[OBS, survival analysis][SIB] SSLA 2024**: persistent adult app learners *paused more often* but returned faster. Design for return, not for never missing a day. — [Cambridge SSLA 2024](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/acceptance-and-engagement-patterns-of-mobileassisted-language-learning-among-nonconventional-adult-l2-learners-a-survival-analysis/C1186B329F808A11DDF2C748E77FD1EE)
- **[MA][PK] Roseth, Johnson & Johnson 2008**: positive peer relationships were associated with higher achievement in early adolescents. This is the theoretical basis for "learning with friends". — [doi:10.1037/0033-2909.134.2.223](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.134.2.223)
- **[QE][PK] Morgan & Halfaker 2018**: a welcoming peer space modestly improved newcomer retention on Wikipedia (Section 4). — [doi:10.1145/3233391.3233544](https://doi.org/10.1145/3233391.3233544)

### Inferences
- **Shared, *visible* progress with a chosen person is the best-evidenced form of social accountability** (Harkin: public or reported progress amplifies the effect). It also carries less risk than loss-aversion streaks. A weekly recap a learner can share with a parent, teacher, mentor or friend ("12 new words, finished 'Family' unit") is low-risk and evidence-aligned.
- **Friend streaks are proven for engagement at scale (vendor data) but unproven for learning.** If Hevalo adds them, they should be:
  - **opt-in, mutual and forgiving**: either friend can repair; streak freezes are earned by learning;
  - possibly **weekly** (e.g., 4 of 7 days) rather than daily (untested; see Gaps);
  - **off by default for minors** (DSA).
- **Cooperative accountability beats comparative accountability** for most learners: shared family or class goals, buddy quests where both must contribute, as opposed to public ranks.
- **Measure learning, not just retention.** Selection bias is severe: motivated users add friends. Evaluate social features by randomised rollout, with 8–12-week retention *and* delayed-recall accuracy from the spaced-repetition logs as outcomes.

### Gaps
- **No peer-reviewed study** found on friend streaks, study buddies or accountability partners in language apps with learning outcomes. Duolingo's friend-feature lift figures (e.g., for Friends Quests) were not retrievable.
- **No evidence** on weekly vs daily (or buddy vs solo) streaks for *learning* retention.
- **Lead [PK, URL not verified]:** the exercise-adherence literature on group cohesion and social support, e.g., Carron, Hausenblas & Mack 1996, *Journal of Sport & Exercise Psychology* 18(1). It is the nearest meta-analytic evidence that social support raises adherence. Numbers not recalled reliably.
- No data on small-community dynamics: Hevalo's user base is small relative to Duolingo's, so friend networks and matched cohorts will be sparse.

## Implications for Hevalo

### Takeaway
Hevalo already has the social plumbing that most learning apps lack: friends, DMs, a community feed, Kahoot-style multiplayer, leagues. The research says the gains come from **how that plumbing is structured**:
1. Turn games and the feed into **interaction with feedback** and **glossed, levelled reading**.
2. Make **cooperation (teams, family, class) the default** and individual competition optional.
3. Bring **fluent speakers (mentors, grandparents, teachers)** into the loop with structure and safety.
4. Use **visible, shared progress** rather than guilt-based streaks.
5. Moderate the community with **clear norms and a welcoming, not punitive, newcomer path**.
6. For minors, keep all social contact **within known circles (family, class)**.

### Concrete design implications

**A. Multiplayer games: from racing to interacting (Section 1)**
1. **Add team modes to every multiplayer game** (quiz, Wordle battle, rhyme match, typing race): 2–4 players per team, team vs team.
   - **Individual accountability:** the team score is the sum of each member's *own* answers, or of each member's improvement over their personal baseline. Every member must answer their own items, with no "captain answers for all".
   - Evidence: Sailer & Homner (competition + collaboration was the best social configuration) [MA]; Slavin (group goals only work with individual accountability) [synthesis, PK]; Johnson & Johnson / Roseth [MA, PK].
2. **Add genuinely cooperative modes** where players must exchange Kurdish to succeed:
   - *Describe-and-guess*: one player sees a picture or word and describes it in Kurdish by voice or text; the partner guesses.
   - *Split-clue Wordle*: each player sees different letter hints and must communicate them.
   - *Co-written story*: players alternate sentences using target words, then vote on corrections.
   - Evidence: Mackey & Goo interaction d = 0.71 → 1.09 delayed [MA]; ASR with a peer g = 0.89 vs alone 0.44 [MA]; collaborative writing improves text accuracy [MA, PK].
3. **Fix the documented Kahoot! failure modes:**
   - a **relaxed-timer** room setting, and an untimed option for accessibility;
   - a short **answer-change window**;
   - **no elimination**, so everyone plays every round;
   - end screens that show the **top 3 plus each player's personal improvement**, never the bottom ranks;
   - **large, projector-legible Hawar and Sorani text** in host mode;
   - auto-generated nicknames, to solve the "can't track nicknames" problem in class.
   - Evidence: Wang & Tahir 2020 (time pressure, fear of losing, legibility) [SR, PK]; leaderboards harm low-ranked players [QE/OBS, SIB].
4. **Every game ends with a "what we missed" recap.** Missed items go into each player's spaced-review queue, and question pools are seeded from players' due items, so games double as retrieval practice. (Aligned with `memory_retention.md` §6.)
5. **Rotate game formats and themes on a schedule** (e.g., monthly rotations, seasonal Newroz and Kurdish-poetry rounds) to counter wear-out. Evaluate new modes at 8–12 weeks, not at launch.
   - Evidence: Wang 2015 wear-out [QE, PK]; Rodrigues 2022 novelty dip at about 4 weeks [QE, SIB].
6. **Defaults by age:**
   - children: cooperative and team modes only, with no individual leaderboards;
   - teens: individual competition opt-in, showing nearby ranks only;
   - adults: choice of modes.
   - Evidence: gamification's motivational effect is smallest for primary-school children, g = 0.31 [MA, SIB]; anxiety–achievement r ≈ −.36 [MA, PK].

**B. Peer feedback that actually teaches (Sections 1, 4)**
7. **A 2-minute "How to help a heval (friend)" micro-lesson** that unlocks commenting and corrections. It teaches learners to give *prompts* ("check the ezafe here?"), not just the right answer, and to be kind about dialect.
   - Evidence: Sato & Lyster, trained peer CF improves accuracy [QE, PK]; prompts beat recasts [MA, SIB].
8. **An opt-in "Correct me" flag on feed posts and DMs.** Only flagged posts get correction affordances:
   - structured correction UI: highlight a span, propose an edit, add a short reason;
   - the author accepts or rejects;
   - accepted corrections link to the dictionary entry.

   This makes peer correction legitimate and verifiable, respecting Sato & Ballinger's trust condition [EXP, PK] and peer feedback ≈ teacher feedback [MA, PK].
9. **"Explain it to a friend" prompts.** After mastering a grammar point (e.g., ergative past, ezafe), the learner records a 30-second explanation or example for a friend or family member. This uses the learning-by-teaching effect [RCT/MA, PK].

**C. Fluent-speaker contact: mentors, exchanges and clubs (Section 2)**
10. **A "Hevalê Ziman" mentor programme (adults only):**
    - verified fluent volunteers (elders, teachers, in-region speakers) host scheduled 15–20-minute voice sessions with 1–3 learners;
    - unit-linked topic cards and a 50/50 Kurdish turn timer;
    - a mentor code of conduct and a 10-minute training: prompts not ridicule; no dialect or "real Kurd" shaming;
    - recognition for mentors, but not Zêr payment per session (see E).
    - Evidence: beginner trap / fluent-speaker immersion [EXP, SIB]; heritage-language anxiety from negative evaluation by community speakers (Sevinç & Dewaele) [OBS, PK].
11. **Skill-swap matching** instead of a generic exchange marketplace. Matching attributes:
    - dialect (Kurmancî, Soranî, Zazakî);
    - script skills (reads Hawar? reads Sorani script?);
    - oral vs literate strengths;
    - an offered skill, e.g., a German or English conversation partner for in-region users, or help with Kurdish spelling.

    Heritage speakers with oral skills and weak literacy are a natural match for in-region speakers with complementary needs. (Inference from the heritage profile [OBS, SIB] and tandem reciprocity [EXP, PK, unverified].)
12. **Weekly conversation clubs**: scheduled group voice rooms by level and dialect, hosted by a trained volunteer, with the unit's topic card. Not recorded by default, for privacy. Adults only; teens only in teacher-run class rooms.
13. **An AI rehearsal step before human conversation**: practise the unit task with an AI partner, then "do it for real" with a mentor, friend or relative. This lowers anxiety without replacing human interaction.
    - Evidence: chatbots are less anxiety-provoking [OBS, SIB]; chatbot and dialogue-CALL g ≈ 0.6 with novelty caveats [MA, SIB].

**D. Family circle and intergenerational features (Section 3)**
14. **Family circle**: a parent creates it and invites relatives (grandparents, aunts, cousins) by code or link. Family members see each other's shared progress.
    - It runs **cooperative family goals** ("our family learned 100 words this month") instead of ranks.
    - It replaces stranger-facing social features for children.
    - Evidence: De Houwer, FLP [OBS, SIB]; cooperative goal structures [MA, PK]; DSA and Ofcom stranger-contact rules [REG, SIB].
15. **Audio-first "Ask Dapîr/Bapîr" (ask grandma/grandpa) cards.** The child or learner records a question in Kurdish ("What did you eat at Newroz when you were little?"). The relative answers by voice note, with no reading or typing needed. Answers become a private family audio library with optional transcription.
    - Evidence: contingent interaction with live speakers; grandparent digital transmission [EXP/OBS, SIB].
16. **"Read together" live mode**: the same storybook page on two devices, synced, with a video or voice call alongside, for grandparent-and-child shared reading across countries. Narration and animation only, no tap-hotspots.
    - Evidence: contingent video chat [EXP, SIB]; Takacs (hotspots distract) [MA, SIB]; Dowdall shared reading [MA, SIB].
17. **Parent coaching track** for semi-speaker parents:
    - weekly "family phrase" cards with audio for routines (meals, bedtime, car);
    - 3-minute "how to read with your child in Kurdish" micro-lessons;
    - a gentle "Kurdish minutes at home" self-report.
    - Evidence: caregiver skill d = 1.01 and dosage-dependent child gains [MA, SIB]; parental input patterns [OBS, SIB]. Note the caveat that effects shrink against active controls [MA, SIB].
18. **Child-teaches-parent quests** ("Teach Dayê 3 words today; she taps to confirm"). Learning by teaching [RCT/MA, PK]; children spontaneously teach family members [QE, SIB].
19. **Transparent parent view**: the child sees that a parent can see their activity (ICO Standard 11). The dashboard shows *learning* ("can now read Hawar letters ç ş ê") and suggests an offline family activity, not screen time alone.

**E. Classroom and community-school mode (Section 3)**
20. **Verified teacher accounts with class codes:**
    - assign course-map units, alphabet lessons and community stories, with due dates;
    - an item-level progress view ("12 of 18 still confuse ê/î");
    - **private class Kahoot host mode**;
    - a class-only story wall;
    - printable worksheets and offline packs for weekend schools.
    - Classes are tagged by dialect and script (Kurmancî/Hawar, Soranî/Arabic script, Zazakî).
21. **Align ready-made class packs** to existing programmes so they work with almost no teacher preparation:
    - Turkey's Kurmancî and Zazakî electives: about 59k pupils, with almost no new teachers;
    - German HSU;
    - Swedish modersmål;
    - KRG–diaspora online schools.

    Evidence: demand and supply figures [NEWS/OBS, SIB]; classroom embedding raises CAPT effects [MA, SIB]. Note that Swedish is not among Hevalo's nine UI languages, although Sweden runs formal Kurdish teaching [SIB].
22. **Allow flexible bilingual support in class mode.** Instructions and glosses come in the pupils' school language (de, tr, sv, ar, fa…), and Kurdish-only screens are introduced progressively. Do not enforce target-language-only.
    - Evidence: Creese & Blackledge, translanguaging in complementary schools [QUAL, PK]; L1 glosses beat L2 glosses [MA, SIB].
23. **A class or school league built on cooperative class goals**, e.g., Saturday school vs Saturday school on *average improvement per pupil*, not total XP, so small and large classes compete fairly and every pupil counts (individual accountability).

**F. Community feed as graded reading, with moderation that grows participation (Section 4)**
24. **Tap-to-gloss on every feed post**, using the 400k-word dictionary:
    - glosses in the reader's UI language;
    - an optional "guess first" multiple-choice gloss;
    - one-tap "add to review".
    - Evidence: glossed 45.3% vs unglossed 26.6% word learning; multiple-choice glosses best; L1 +4 points [MA, SIB].
25. **Auto-level posts per reader** ("readable for you" at about 95% or more known-word coverage). Add a **"Graded community stories" shelf**: editor-selected posts, lightly corrected, levelled, audio-recorded (by the author or a volunteer narrator), and credited to the author. These double as lesson reading.
    - Evidence: ER d ≈ 0.46–0.57 [MA, SIB]; narrative and learner-targeted texts work best [MA, SIB]; the volunteer-to-curated content pipeline [SIB].
26. **Writing prompts tied to units** ("write 3 sentences about your family using the past tense"), plus author tools:
    - diacritic keyboard helper (ê î û ç ş);
    - dictionary-backed spelling hints;
    - dialect and script tags.
27. **Newcomer-friendly moderation:**
    - show 4–5 short community rules (in the UI language) before the first post: dialect respect, no political attacks, kindness to beginners, no personal data;
    - pre-moderate a new account's first few posts, with a **human "welcome" reply** on the first accepted post;
    - when a post is rejected, give a reason and a fix-and-resubmit option, not silent deletion;
    - instant takedown remains available for safety violations.
    - Evidence: rules displayed → newcomer compliance +8pp and participation +70% (Matias 2019) [RCT, PK]; over-aggressive rejection drives newcomers away (Halfaker 2013) [OBS, PK]; Teahouse welcome effect [QE, PK].
28. **Multi-dialect, multi-language moderator team.** Recruit trusted community moderators covering Kurmancî, Soranî and the main comment languages (tr, ar, fa, de). Use LLM or automated classification only for **triage**, with human final decisions, until it has been validated on Kurdish. Write an explicit policy on political symbols and content, reviewed by counsel against German, Turkish, Iraqi and EU law (open legal question).
    - Evidence: no Kurdish LLM moderation benchmark [SIB].
29. **Do not pay Zêr for posting, liking or messaging.** Reward creative contributions with *unexpected, informational* recognition: featured story, "your poem was used in a lesson", read counts, author credit.
    - Evidence: engagement-contingent rewards d = −0.40 [MA, SIB]; gamification misuse [QUAL, SIB].

**G. Social accountability without guilt (Section 5)**
30. **A shareable weekly progress recap** (can-do statements, words learned, minutes of listening), sent to a chosen accountability person: parent, teacher, mentor or buddy.
    - Evidence: progress monitoring d = 0.40, larger when reported or public [MA, SIB].
31. **Optional "Study buddy" pairs** with a shared *weekly* goal (e.g., 4 days out of 7 each). Both can see the other's progress. Either can send one supportive nudge per day from preset kind messages, with no guilt templates. The goal can be repaired by doing extra review.
    - Evidence: forgiving and repairable streaks [QE/VENDOR, SIB]; comeback bonuses +27% [RCT, SIB]. Weekly vs daily is untested, so A/B test it.
32. **Friend streaks: opt-in for adults, off by default for under-18s, never for children's profiles.** Include freezes earned through learning, a repair window, and a "welcome back" bonus after a break.
    - Evidence: DSA and ICO on streaks [REG, SIB]; Snapchat streak harms [OBS, SIB].
33. **Evaluate every social feature by randomised rollout.** Primary outcomes: 8–12-week retention *and* delayed-recall accuracy from spaced-repetition logs. Selection bias (motivated learners add friends) makes simple before/after or friend vs no-friend comparisons misleading.

**H. Safety architecture for minors (cross-cutting; detailed in `children.md`)**
34. **Age router at sign-up** (neutral age screen):
    - under-13s: parent-managed child profile with **social limited to the family circle and teacher classes**; auto-generated Kurdish nicknames; no free-text chat in games (emote buttons only); no public feed; no DMs;
    - teens: private by default; no friend suggestions; mentor and exchange programmes closed; public multiplayer chat off by default;
    - mentors and conversation-club hosts: **adults only**; no 1:1 sessions between unrelated adults and minors.
    - Evidence: Ofcom, DSA Article 28, ICO and COPPA 2025 [REG, SIB].

### Gaps (for the implications)
- None of these features has been tested for **Kurdish learners**. All transfer is by analogy from other languages and contexts. The strongest transfers rest on SLA interaction and glossing meta-analyses, the cooperative-learning meta-analyses and the online-community field experiments. The weakest rest on vendor data (friend streaks, leagues) and tandem research, which is mostly qualitative.
- Numbers tagged [PK] above (Johnson & Johnson 0.67/0.64, Slavin +0.32/+0.07, Huisman g = 0.91, Double g = 0.31, Hunsu g ≈ 0.2, Cohen-Kulik 0.40/0.33, Teimouri r = −.36) **must be verified against the original papers before publication**. This session could not retrieve them.

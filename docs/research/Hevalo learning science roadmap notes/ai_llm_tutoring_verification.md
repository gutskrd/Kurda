# Fact-check: ai_llm_tutoring.md (AI and LLM tutoring for language learning, 2023–2026)

**How this check was done. Read this first.**
- **I could not run any web searches.** All four searches I sent (Kestin, Bastani, the Nigeria trial, Tutor CoPilot) came back "Web search was not performed: this turn's web search budget is used up (limit: 200 WebSearch calls per turn, shared by every agent in it)".
- **WebFetch is blocked.** A test fetch of arxiv.org returned `EGRESS_BLOCKED`.
- I did not try to get around either limit (no curl, no reader or proxy services).
- I used four other routes instead:
  1. **Raw search output saved earlier in this session.** The file `scratchpad/searches.txt` holds 227 query blocks from all research agents; it lives in this session's scratchpad and is not in the repo. I used it in two ways:
     - to check that the notes copied their snippets faithfully;
     - to find details or conflicts in *other* agents' searches.
     A snippet from the same chain the notes rely on does **not** count as corroboration. That includes children.md's searches for the legal claims, which this note copies.
  2. **Independent sibling retrievals**, mainly speech_audio_tech.md and two separate child-ASR searches. These count only when they cite a *different* source.
  3. **The Hevalo repository**, for product facts: `shared/src/locales.ts`, `docs/admin/dictionary-import.md`, `shared/src/ferheng.ts`, and the `hevalo_current_state_*.md` notes, which cite code paths.
  4. **My background knowledge.** It is labelled "[background, unverified]" every time and **never** upgrades a claim to "confirmed". I made up no URLs and opened none.
- **Verdict rules:**
  - **confirmed**: a retrieval outside the notes' own source chain, or the repository, matches the claim.
  - **overstated**: the core may hold, but a number, label or design inference goes beyond the evidence, or other evidence narrows it.
  - **refuted**: independent evidence (here, the repository) contradicts the claim.
  - **unverified**: no corroboration was available. Following the task rules, I gave no benefit of the doubt.
- **Many weaknesses below come from the notes' sources, not from suspected errors.** Almost every study figure in the notes rests on one search summary of blogs or press pages. No one in this project read a full paper.

## Omitted counter-evidence and caveats

1. **AI help can raise the product without raising the learner.** [background, unverified]
   - Fan et al. 2024 (*BJET*, "metacognitive laziness"): ChatGPT support improved essay scores but not knowledge gain or transfer.
   - Lehmann, Cornelius & Sting (2024–25): LLM access harmed learning when it *replaced* practice and helped only when it *complemented* it. Learners overestimated how much they had learned.
   - Darvishi et al. 2024 (*Computers & Education*): students leaned on AI prompts when writing peer feedback, and their performance fell once the prompts were removed.
   - These generalise Bastani's finding to writing and feedback tasks. They bear directly on rec 8 and rec 12 (AI suggestions for co-writing and community posts): the AI should prompt revision, not rewrite.
2. **Effects shrink with time and better controls.** [background, unverified] Wu & Yu 2024 (*BJET*, chatbot meta-analysis) found larger effects in short interventions than in long ones. Together with Li 2025's 1–7-day moderator and Lyu's comparison-group moderator (row 5), pooled g ≈ 0.6 is best treated as an **upper bound** for a long-running app. Many generative-AI meta-analyses pool small, short, unblinded quasi-experiments with researcher-made tests.
3. **No durable, AI-free outcome in any cited trial.**
   - Kestin and Tutor CoPilot measured immediate outcomes; Nigeria reported none at delay.
   - Only Bastani tested performance without the AI, and there the guarded tutor merely broke even (row 3).
   - The notes rightly ask Hevalo to use delayed, AI-free checks (rec 5). They should also say plainly that **no cited trial shows a durable gain from LLM tutoring**.
4. **Self-report outcomes are unreliable.**
   - L2 chatbot studies lean on self-reported anxiety, willingness to communicate (WTC) and self-efficacy.
   - Bastani found that students overestimate their learning with AI [background, unverified].
   - Hevalo's A/B tests should not treat "learners say they feel more confident" as a primary metric.
5. **LLM feedback on learner errors over-corrects.** [background, unverified] Studies of GPT-family grammatical error correction (GEC) report fluency rewrites and low precision on minimal-edit benchmarks. Rec 6's "feedback on accuracy, complexity and one better phrasing" therefore risks flagging correct heritage or dialect forms as errors. No Kurdish GEC benchmark exists. Add an "error-diagnosis precision" task to HevalBench, rated by native Kurmanji and Sorani speakers, before learners see any AI corrections.
6. **Heritage learners are not EFL undergraduates.**
   - Heritage-speaker anxiety is tied to family and community judgement and to dialect legitimacy.
   - An AI that "corrects" a grandparent's dialect form could make that anxiety worse.
   - The evidence that chatbots reduce anxiety comes entirely from adult EFL learners, so transfer to heritage learners is untested.
7. **Children:** no AI-tutor study of language learning in under-13s was found, as the notes say. [background, unverified] Children anthropomorphise and over-trust conversational agents, which supports the notes' "no open chat for children" rule. This is an additional reason for it, not a new constraint.
8. **Provider terms could decide model choice.** [background, unverified] The Gemini API Additional Terms have barred use in services "directed towards or likely to be accessed by individuals under the age of 18". Vertex AI on Google Cloud runs under separate terms. Since Gemini is the only positive Kurdish signal (row 8), check both paths, plus Anthropic's and OpenAI's minor-use conditions, **before** HevalBench picks a vendor.
9. **EU AI Act beyond Article 50.** [background, unverified] Article 5 (in force since February 2025) bans AI that exploits age-related vulnerabilities to distort behaviour. This matters if AI-driven nudges are combined with gamification for children. Article 50 AI-disclosure duties apply from 2 August 2026. The Digital Omnibus timing for Annex III remains unverified.
10. **Machine-translation baselines were not considered.** [background, unverified]
    - Google Translate supports both Kurmanji and Sorani.
    - Meta NLLB-200 covers kmr_Latn and ckb_Arab, but its weights are CC-BY-NC.
    - Both are cheap baselines for HevalBench translation items. The heritage agent's search on them returned nothing.
11. **Kurdish AI competitors already exist.** kurdishtts.com offers voice chat, and the "Kurdistan AI" app claims Kurmanji and Sorani voice; both are vendor claims (row 9). They weaken the notes' framing of the Kurdish field as only fragmented course apps, and they make good HevalBench comparison points.
12. **The cost evidence is not comparable.**
    - Nigeria's $48 per student covers a facilitated programme.
    - Tutor CoPilot's $20 is per *human tutor*.
    - Neither price covers realtime voice, which the notes never priced.
    - Cost claims in rec 11 should wait for current API pricing.

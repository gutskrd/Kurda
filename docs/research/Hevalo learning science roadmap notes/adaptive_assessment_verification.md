## Omitted counter-evidence and caveats

1. **Almost the whole evidence base for sections 1–3 is unverified model memory.**
   - Every study behind the learner model (rec 3–6), the difficulty targets (rec 7), the placement (rec 14) and the effect-size expectations is tagged [MK]. That covers Lindsey, VanLehn, the Kulik studies, Slavin, Pane, Roschelle, Nickow, Lortie-Forgues, Kraft, Wilson, Jansen, Linacre, Weiss & Kingsbury, LexTALE, the DET, Gervet and pyKT.
   - None was retrieved in this run, by the notes or by this check.
   - The recommendations are mostly cautious ("start simple", "A/B-test the target"), so the design risk is moderate. **No number from these sources should go into a report, deck or marketing claim until it has been checked.**
2. **The placement CAT cannot deliver the precision it promises** (row 9, computed here).
   - This undermines the per-skill CEFR profile in rec 16 and the "θ growth between re-placements" metric in rec 18.
   - The SE of a difference between two 20-item placements is √2 × SE, about 0.64–1.05 logits. So an individual learner's growth smaller than roughly 1.3–2.1 logits cannot be told apart from noise. Use cohort averages, or longer checkpoints, for growth.
3. **The probe hold-out (rec 19) measures something different from what the notes claim.**
   - Holding 5% of items out of review gives the **forgetting curve without review**. It does not give the retention the live scheduler achieves.
   - To estimate "words still known at 30 days" under the real system, sample probe items from *all* learned items, including scheduled ones, at times the scheduler does not choose.
   - Each probe is itself a retrieval-practice event (a testing effect), so probes change later retention and must be logged as reviews.
   - Withholding review from held-out items costs those learners a little learning. Disclose this in the "how we improve Hevalo" notice.
4. **Small subgroups make the equity checks underpowered.**
   - [background, unverified] Mantel–Haenszel DIF detection usually needs a few hundred responses per group per item.
   - With few children, or small UI-language groups, rec 6 and the UI-language DIF check in rec 24 will be underpowered for a long time.
   - Pool items by feature (for example, "items containing ڕ") to check DIF at the feature level.
5. **The "A/B illusion" is contested.**
   - [background, unverified] Mislavsky, Dietvorst & Simonsohn (2020, *Marketing Science*, "Critical condition") reported that people do not dislike an experiment more than they dislike its worst condition. Their explanation is that objections come from a disliked arm, not from experimenting itself.
   - Heck, Chabris and colleagues (2020, *PNAS*) replicated Meyer et al.'s effect in some scenarios.
   - The practical advice still holds: test only arms that are acceptable on their own.
6. **Within-learner, item-level randomisation (rec 22) has limits.**
   - It suits manipulations that act on single items, such as scheduling, a mnemonic hint, or audio-first presentation of one word.
   - It is contaminated when a feature changes overall behaviour: a learner given mnemonics on some words may start using them on all words. Carryover between conditions has the same effect.
   - Leagues, streaks, XP rules and session length must be randomised at the user level, which brings back the power problem in row 3.
7. **Elo and FSRS can double-count.**
   - Rec 3's Elo θ per (variety × skill) and the FSRS memory state per word both update after the same answer.
   - Without a clear split (θ for format and difficulty selection, FSRS for timing), a lapse is penalised twice, once as lower ability and once as forgetting. The learner can then get stuck on items that are too easy.
   - Neither the notes nor memory_retention.md says how the two are combined.
8. **Self-assessment in children.**
   - [background, unverified] The finding that younger children self-assess less accurately than older ones is usually credited to **Butler & Lee (2006)**, which compared 4th and 6th graders.
   - **Butler & Lee (2010)** tested a self-assessment *intervention* with 6th graders and found modest gains.
   - The notes' citation may merge the two. Either way, "child can-do self-ratings need performance checks" holds.
9. **Wheel-spinning's "10 opportunities" is a definition, not a law.**
   - [background, unverified] Beck & Gong defined wheel-spinning operationally as no mastery (3 correct in a row) within 10 opportunities in maths tutors, and prevalence differed between systems.
   - Repeated vocabulary attempts spread over spaced sessions are a different unit.
   - The 8–10 trigger in rec 9 is a starting heuristic to tune from Hevalo's own learning curves.
10. **Response-time scoring with children** (rec 12, Maris & van der Maas).
    - Math Garden uses it with children. But speed pressure may disadvantage children on touchscreens, younger readers, and anyone typing Kurdish diacritics or Sorani letters on a non-Kurdish keyboard.
    - Rec 12 already limits speed scoring to timed games. Also exclude children's timed scores from θ until the keyboard confound is measured.
11. **Duolingo's own evidence is engagement-only or vendor-authored.**
    - HLR, leagues (+17% learning time, from an ex-employee's newsletter retrospective, per motivation_gamification_verification.md row 10) and notifications all report engagement.
    - The ACTFL-scored studies have Duolingo-affiliated authors and no controls.
    - No public study links a learner-model *change* to a delayed proficiency outcome. The notes' Gaps say so, but the §4 Takeaway's "vendors validate apps with external tests" reads more strongly than the evidence.
12. **Corpus resources are much thinner for Kurmanji.**
    - Sorani has corpora in the hundreds of millions of words (AsoSoft about 188M). Kurmanji has single-digit millions in Pewan (snippet).
    - Frequency-based difficulty priors (rec 4) and "RLD-lite" frequency bands (rec 15) will be far less reliable for Kurmanji. Weight community and teacher judgements more there.
13. **Already in the notes' Gaps and still open:**
    - Birdbrain's architecture and target.
    - LLM-based KT results in 2025–26.
    - Any human test of the 85% rule.
    - Any language-learning RCT that varies the target success rate with delayed outcomes.

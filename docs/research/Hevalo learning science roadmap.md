# Ship the Loop, Then Make Kurdish Stick

The change that would most improve learning on Hevalo is not a new feature: it is letting people learn at all. Today the web app has no lesson player, the mobile app that has one is not in the stores, and the course holds **18 lessons, 40 exercises and about 43 Kurmanji words, with no audio and no listening or speaking items**, so every research-backed idea in this report multiplies something close to zero until a real learning loop ships with real content. The two best-supported levers in learning science, retrieval practice and spaced review, are exactly where Hevalo's engine is weakest: it schedules exercises instead of words, counts same-day replays as spaced reviews, puts every multiple-choice answer in first position, tells every speaker their recording is correct, and accepts *e* for *ê*. Two defects the earlier draft roadmap missed come before everything else: a content import that would wipe every learner's review history on each deploy, and real-money Gems that can be farmed by replaying easy lessons. After those fixes the binding constraint is content and people, not code: a Kurmanji A1–A2 course with native audio, a curated bilingual glossary (the 400k-word dictionary defines Kurdish in Kurdish and cannot gloss for learners), graded reading and listening, and a Sorani course with its own grading. Rewards should pay for learning rather than attendance, minors need protections now and a separate parent-managed mode later, and Kurdish speech and language AI is ready only for narrow, adult, human-checked uses. Plan on any single feature moving delayed, independent outcomes by roughly 0.05–0.2 standard deviations and judge each one by delayed recall rather than XP; no Kurdish learning app has published outcome data, so Hevalo can be the first. One caveat runs through everything: the research team worked from search summaries rather than full papers, and adversarial fact-checks downgraded many headline numbers, so the figures below are planning aids, each marked with how far it was verified.

## Nobody can take a lesson yet, and parts of the engine teach the wrong thing

A member of the public cannot take a single lesson on Hevalo today. The web `/app/learn` page shows course titles that cannot be clicked beside a static picture of a lesson (`web/src/pages/Learn.tsx`; `web/src/landing/mocks.tsx:325`), and the only client with a working lesson player, the Expo app, is not in the stores (issue #279). Behind that sits a capable server engine (graded sessions, SM-2 review, practice, placement, a course map, goals, leagues, analytics and A/B bucketing) with very little to teach: **one Kurmanji course of 15 lessons plus a 3-lesson Newroz course, 40 exercises, about 43 distinct Kurmanji targets, and zero listening or speaking items**, loaded only when someone runs the import by hand (`api/content/kurmanji-seed.json`; `api/start.sh`). The research on app learning is observational but consistent that use predicts gains: hours of Babbel use predicted gains on every test in one commissioned study ([MSU Today](https://msutoday.msu.edu/news/2020/04/how-effective-are-language-learning-apps)), and Duolingo learners' gains tracked session completion ([Smith, Jiang & Peters 2024](https://www.lltjournal.org/item-detail/1152/)). Both are confounded by motivation, but retrieval and spacing cannot help learners who never reach a lesson. The enabling work is to move the pure session reducer (`mobile/src/lesson/player.ts`) into `shared/`, build the web player, course map and practice screens against endpoints that already exist, release the mobile app, send new sign-ups to a first lesson rather than the community wall (`web/src/pages/VerifyEmail.tsx:62,74,93`), and let learners save and resume mid-lesson so stopping costs nothing. Ship this together with the first rebuilt course units; a 15-lesson course is finished in a day.

Several defects teach less or teach something false, and most cost under an engineer-week. **All 15 seeded multiple-choice items have the correct answer in first position and options appear in authored order** (`api/src/content/exercises.ts:156-161`), so a learner who notices stops retrieving; placement uses the first exercise of each level (`api/src/placement/service.ts:76-82`), so it is gameable too. A per-session seeded shuffle like the one `match_pairs` already uses fixes both, but `checkAnswer` takes no seed, so the seed must be threaded through all three call sites (`api/src/content/sessions.ts:130,205`; `api/src/practice/service.ts:115,183`; `api/src/placement/service.ts:91,162`); once shuffling exists, re-keying stored items is unnecessary, though an import lint on answer positions is still worth adding. `ReviewService.record` treats every answer as a scheduled review, so a same-day replay or a padded, not-yet-due practice item stretches its interval as if spacing had happened; advance the schedule only when an item is due or at least half its interval has passed, still record a wrong answer as a lapse, and align due dates to the learner's local day. The speaking scorer **marks every recording correct** (`api/src/content/speaking-scorer.ts:27`); replace it with record-then-compare against the native clip and a low-weight "close / not yet" self-rating, and add a settings switch so a denied microphone no longer disables speaking for the whole course (`mobile/src/lesson/LessonPlayerScreen.tsx:128-145`). On phones, lesson and dictionary audio and recording depend on browser APIs and silently fail (`mobile/src/lesson/useAudio.ts`, `useRecorder.ts`); rebuilding them on `expo-audio`, which already works in the library's voice notes, unblocks every listening and speaking idea in this report. The grader accepts answers that match only after folding ê→e or ş→s as correct "typos" (`api/src/content/exercises.ts:221-233`) although these letters distinguish words; section 2 turns this into a strict spelling mode. Practice will probably return a server error for anyone who has saved a dictionary word, because `dict:` ids reach a `uuid[]` cast (`api/src/practice/service.ts:88-93,122`). Daily-Wordle XP is keyed by day across all users, so only the first finisher each day is credited while everyone is told they earned it (`api/src/game/wordle-service.ts:245`), and the six defined achievements are never awarded.

Two defects the draft roadmap missed outrank most of these. First, **the content import creates a new version, with new exercise UUIDs, for every lesson on every run** (`api/src/content/import.ts:9,166-170`), while review state is keyed on exercise UUID (`api/src/review/service.ts:79-89`). Running the import automatically on deploy, the obvious fix for "content is not loaded", would therefore **reset every learner's review schedule on every deploy**. Hash each lesson's content and version only when the hash changes, add a test that a no-change re-import creates zero versions, and ideally land stable word-level item ids (section 2) first. Second, the economy discussion overlooked **Gems, the currency Hevalo already sells for real money** through in-app purchase (`api/src/iap/service.ts:50-92`). Gems are also granted for a perfect lesson (5, capped at 50 a day), league promotion (25), tournament wins (50) and achievements (15) (`api/migrations/1751000040000_gem-rules.js:25-28`), and the perfect-lesson grant fires on replays (`api/src/content/routes.ts:77`). With every multiple-choice answer in first position, **a learner can farm the 50-Gem daily cap by replaying easy lessons**. Pay perfect-lesson Gems on first completion only or drop them, switch off league-promotion Gems for minors, and apply the EU consumer authorities' virtual-currency principles (real-money prices shown, no pressure on children) to Gems rather than to the earn-only Zêr ([Lewis Silkin on the CPC principles](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2025/03/26/eu-bodies-act-against-gaming-companies-to-protect-children-from-in-game-purchase-102k658)).

Measurement is the other precondition, and its plumbing matters more than the draft assumed. No client emits analytics events and the seeded experiment is never read (`mobile/src/analytics/tracker.ts` has no callers). `analytics_consent` defaults to false (`api/migrations/1751000010000_consent.js:16`) and is not checked at ingest. Routing the answer-level learning log through that flag would leave non-consenting learners with no review history and bias every experiment toward opted-in users, so keep three streams separate: the review log and scheduler state are service data the product needs to work; analytics events are consent-gated; experiment assignment is logged server-side. Experiment readouts then describe only the consenting sample, and should say so. The GDPR export contains the profile, refresh tokens and OAuth links (`api/src/gdpr/service.ts:113-136`) and should also cover learning data and user content: posts, DMs, voice notes and images.

Some built features are unreachable, and some copy is untrue. Mobile shows only `courses[0]`, which hides the Newroz course (`mobile/src/screens/LearnScreen.tsx:60`); grammar notes open only from inside a mobile lesson and never on web; due-review counts are never shown; the word-of-the-day pool cannot be filled because `addToWotdPool` has no production caller; and web Wordle ends with "The word was {word}" and no meaning, audio or save, although mobile Wordle already offers save (`web/src/pages/Wordle.tsx:161`; `mobile/src/wordle/WordleScreen.tsx:148-219`). Closing the loop after every game (meaning, audio, missed answers, save to review) turns each round into feedback-rich retrieval. The landing page promises slowable lesson recordings and six exercise types including listening and speaking, the Learn page says "Pick a course to begin" over unclickable cards, guests are told solo games need no account although the API requires one, and onboarding promises "a Kurdish story every day" with no job behind it (`web/src/i18n/en.ts:803,1124,1134,1240`; `mobile/src/i18n/translations.ts:1001-1002`). For a mission-driven minority-language product, trust is worth the afternoon these fixes take.

## Spaced retrieval is the strongest lever and Hevalo's weakest system

### What the memory evidence supports

Practice testing beats restudying by about half a standard deviation across meta-analyses, more when feedback follows ([Rowland 2014, summarised in Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1727423/full); [Yang et al. 2021](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2021-yang.pdf)), and in real school classrooms **57% of retrieval-practice effects were medium or large** ([Agarwal, Nunes & Blunt 2021](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/350049741_Retrieval_Practice_Consistently_Benefits_Student_Learning_a_Systematic_Review_of_Applied_Research_in_Schools_and_Classrooms)). Spacing beats massing in second-language learning with a medium-to-large effect that is larger on delayed tests ([Kim & Webb 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/lang.12479)); two search summaries of that meta-analysis disagree on the values (0.58/0.80 versus 0.76/1.15), so plan on the direction, not the decimals. Equal and expanding schedules performed alike ([Latimier, Peyre & Ramus 2021](http://www.lscp.net/persons/ramus/docs/EPR20.pdf)): modern schedulers expand intervals because memory stabilises, not because expansion is magic. Two results shape the design. **Delayed form recall (25.1%) sits well below delayed meaning recall (39.4%) after deliberate study**, and delayed gains range from about 24% to 73% depending on the activity ([Webb, Yanagisawa & Uchihara 2020](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/modl.12671); delayed figures confirmed at snippet level), so producing and spelling a Kurdish word is the skill that most needs spaced practice. Practising production also appears to build recognition, while practising recognition builds little production ([Nakata 2020](https://howtoeigo.net/research/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/Nakata-2020-The-Routledge-Handbook-of-Vocabulary-Studies_Part2.pdf); one research programme, unverified), which makes multiple choice a scaffold rather than a destination. For children the picture is less settled: retrieval effects were robust in 10-year-olds ([Karpicke et al. 2016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4786565/)), but a multi-classroom study of real vocabulary lessons in grades 2–6 found no benefit ([Goossens et al.](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/67956)), and young children do not choose spacing for themselves ([Vlach et al. 2019](https://vlachlab.education.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/372/2026/03/VlachEtAl2019.pdf)). The app has to schedule, and child effects are a hypothesis to test.

### Rebuild memory around words, not exercises

Hevalo's review table keys every item on an exercise UUID, so **the same word in two exercises is two unrelated items, a new lesson version restarts its history, and placement and games never feed the schedule**; nothing records latency, the raw answer or the state before a review (`api/src/review/`; migration `1751000022000_review-items.js`). The replacement is a word-level knowledge model. Tag every exercise with what it tests (lexemes with lemma, variety and script; chunks; grammar points such as `kmr.ezafe.feminine`, `kmr.past.ergative` or `ckb.past.clitic-agent`; graphemes) and with the skill exercised (meaning recall, form recall, listening, spelling or reading). Keep memory state per user × item × skill, and write every graded retrieval from any feature to an append-only review log with source feature, millisecond timestamp, grade, latency, format and number of options, hint use, attempt number, days since the last review, and scheduler state before and after. Passive exposures, such as a word seen in a story or a dictionary look-up, go in a separate table, because no published method credits them as reviews. Duolingo trained its word model on ordinary lesson traces this way ([Settles & Meeder 2016](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/settles.acl16.pdf)), and it is the log a modern scheduler needs ([fsrs-optimizer](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/fsrs-optimizer)). Stable item ids also make lesson versioning safe, which is why they should land before the content import is automated. The admin Users page shows no learning data; a per-learner item view belongs in the same work.

### Choose the scheduler on Hevalo's own logs

The one primary source the research team read in full was the open spaced-repetition benchmark, and it overturns several common beliefs ([srs-benchmark](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/srs-benchmark)). It runs on **10,000 Anki users and about 727 million reviews** ([anki-revlogs-10k](https://huggingface.co/datasets/open-spaced-repetition/anki-revlogs-10k)), not the 20,000-user figure often quoted. FSRS-6 scores a log loss of 0.3460, against 0.4694 for a three-parameter half-life regression and 0.6258 for an SM-2-like Anki default. **FSRS-7, listed in September 2026, scores 0.3401 and is the only version that models same-day reviews realistically; on same-day reviews FSRS-6 does worse than a constant predictor** (0.3842 against 0.3816). A zero-parameter moving average of the learner's recent success (0.3369) also beats FSRS-6 overall, so calibration must be checked against trivial baselines. Fitting per learner matters far more than grade detail: FSRS-6 scores 0.3664 on default parameters and 0.3460 when fitted, but 0.3509 with pass/fail grades only, and the benchmark's own size analysis recommends pretraining from 8 reviews and full optimisation from 64 rather than waiting for 1,000 (see also [Anki issue 3094](https://github.com/ankitects/anki/issues/3094)). Hevalo will generate many same-day retrievals through in-lesson recycling and games, so benchmark FSRS-6 against FSRS-7 on its own logs (checking whether the TypeScript library ships FSRS-7), run the winner in shadow mode, and start an A/B test only after it beats the moving-average baseline over a minimum number of reviews. Do not use "beats SM-2" as the gate: SM-2 has no native probability, and the benchmark scores it by assuming 90% recall at the scheduled interval. Use pass/fail grades, target about 0.90 desired retention for adults, since workload rises steeply above it ([Anki manual](https://docs.ankiweb.net/deck-options.html?highlight=learning)), and for children test 0.85 against 0.90 rather than assuming a lower target is gentler, because it doubles the share of failed reviews. When review debt grows, cap new items, never reviews. Keep expectations modest: benchmark wins are prediction, Duolingo's scheduler A/B test measured engagement ([Settles & Meeder 2016](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/settles.acl16.pdf)), and the one delayed classroom result, personalised review about 10% better than generic spacing a month after an 8th-grade Spanish course ([Lindsey et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797613504302)), is unverified and may be a relative figure. Fold the unwired `adaptive.ts` into this work or retire it.

### Teach, recall, produce, recycle

The lesson player tests without teaching: there is no introduction phase, wrong answers are never re-asked, and the results screen lists misses without their answers (`mobile/src/lesson/player.ts:81-92`; `components/LessonResults.tsx:40-47`). The research supports a five-step structure for new units. Introduce 3–5 new items with native audio, written Kurdish, a gloss in the learner's interface language and a picture for concrete words; check recognition with feedback; move to production, first with a tap-to-place word bank (which also gives drag exercises the tap alternative accessibility rules expect) and then typed recall with the Kurdish key bar; recycle each new item about 3–5 times across the session with other items in between; and close on the learner's own errors, with the first review the next day. The cap matters. Retrieving each word 5 or 7 times in a session beat 1 or 3 times ([Nakata 2017](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/abs/does-repeated-practice-make-perfect-the-effects-of-withinsession-repeated-retrieval-on-second-language-vocabulary-learning/F14BA8A576CD2563D14CEA46E35D842E)), but whether 5 and 7 differ was not verified, larger sets help by lengthening the gaps between retrievals ([Nakata & Webb 2016](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/271207356_Does_studying_vocabulary_in_smaller_sets_increase_learning_The_effects_of_part_and_whole_learning_on_second_language_vocabulary_acquisition)), and 3–5 items × 5–7 retrievals is 15–35 retrievals, not the 12–20 one note claimed. "Guess first" belongs only where a guess is possible (cognates, pictures, known morphology): the pretesting evidence comes from English word pairs ([pretesting study, 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12292081/)), and most Kurdish word forms are arbitrary to a newcomer. Interleave only confusable material such as e/ê, i/î and u/û, the ezafe endings, or present versus past agreement: interleaving helped overall (g = 0.42), but blocking beat it for word materials in a meta-analysis of category learning ([Brunmair & Richter 2019](https://www.psychologie.uni-wuerzburg.de/fileadmin/06020400/2019/Brunmair_Richter_in_press__2019_META-ANALYSIS_OF_INTERLEAVED_LEARNING.pdf)), the grammar support is one experiment ([Nakata & Suzuki 2019](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:7096acb5-f759-4fe9-a06a-d6a0e20f75d0/files/ssf268575c)), and a recent study found the benefit varies by grammatical pattern ([Xiong](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/modl.70082)). Explicit instruction produces larger immediate effects than implicit exposure across several meta-analyses, with thin and conflicting evidence on which lasts longer ([review of five meta-analyses](https://journal.kci.go.kr/meeso/archive/articlePdf?artiId=ART002651180)), so keep grammar notes short (at most three cards, each followed by practice) and pair every rule with both comprehension items ("who saw whom?" for the ergative past) and production. These desirable difficulties lower accuracy inside the lesson, so never tune difficulty on in-lesson accuracy ([Soderstrom & Bjork 2015](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691615569000); not re-checked). Because content is the long pole, author new and rebuilt units to this structure rather than spending the first quarter retrofitting the engine. The cheapest piece, re-asking a missed item about three positions later with its correct form and audio, already exists in the alphabet (`COMEBACK = 3`, `web/src/alphabet/Practice.tsx:25`) and should move to lessons now; it is also an ideal item-level experiment, re-queueing a random half of misses and comparing their recall at the next review.

### Feedback that names the error, and spelling that counts

Feedback today is a verdict plus the first accepted answer. Corrective feedback has a medium, durable effect overall ([Li 2010](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9922.2010.00561.x); not re-checked this run), and its value grows when it tells the learner what went wrong. Add `hint`, `explanation`, `image` and audio fields to the exercise schema; diagnose typed errors by type (missing diacritic, wrong letter, wrong ending, word order, Sorani dot or ە/ه confusions); and show the learner's answer directly above the model answer with the differing letters highlighted. A hint ladder (a clue, then a partial form, then the answer with an explanation) suits typed and spoken items only, since on multiple choice it degenerates into elimination, and using a hint should lower the scheduler grade rather than cost XP. The popular claim that prompts beat recasts comes from classroom oral feedback and is contested ([Lyster & Saito 2010](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/234580927_Oral_Feedback_in_Classroom_SLA_A_Meta-Analysis)), so test prompt-first against answer-first rather than adopting it. Spelling needs its own fix: because the grader accepts ê→e and ş→s folds as correct, a literacy course would count its target errors as successes. Make leniency a property of each item and level. Beginners get "almost" with partial credit and the letter shown; dictation, spelling and literacy items reject the near-miss and name the letter; once the word model exists, such an answer fails the spelling skill but passes the meaning skill; and editors list attested regional variants to accept with a "standard versus common spoken" note. The strict flag can ship before the word model.

### Games, the map and the missing strands

The games draw on a 33-question quiz bank and a 79-word pool unrelated to the course and never touch scheduling (`api/src/game/question-bank.ts`; migration `1751000094000_seed-game-dictionary.js`). Seeding solo Wordle, race and quiz from each learner's due and nearly-due items, logging every game answer weighted by format (a four-option quiz answer is weaker evidence than a typed Wordle guess), and adding a solo quiz bot would turn game time into review time. Rate this Moderate rather than Strong: whether game-format retrievals deserve flashcard-equivalent scheduling is unknown, and timed play mixes language with typing skill, so use speed only against a learner's own baseline and keep children's timed scores out of ability estimates. The course map shows "decayed" nodes, but strength is 0.6 × ease + 0.4 × repetitions and only falls after wrong answers (`api/src/placement/skill-strength.ts`), so a skill left alone for months stays gold; base strength on predicted recall and send a tap on a "ready to refresh" skill to a short review. A difficulty model (Elo or Rasch with a guessing floor, choosing format and difficulty while the scheduler chooses timing, without penalising one lapse twice) can wait for data; simple models predict about as well as deep ones at small scale ([Pelánek 2016](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compedu.2016.03.017); not re-checked), and target success rates such as "85%" come from theory about classifier training, not language learning ([Wilson et al. 2019](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-12552-4)). Finally, Nation's widely used framework recommends spending no more than about a quarter of learning time on deliberate item study, with the rest split across meaning-focused input, meaning-focused output and fluency ([Nation, Cambridge Papers in ELT](https://www.cambridge.org/us/files/8415/7488/4554/CambridgePapersinELT_TimeForSpeaking_2017_ONLINE.pdf)). That is expert opinion, not a tested ratio, but almost everything Hevalo offers is deliberate study. Count a completed graded-reader or listening session, with its comprehension check, toward XP, goals and the streak, and add a gentle weekly strand-balance nudge; otherwise the reward system will push learners away from the reading and listening that section 3 builds.

## Content, a real glossary and Kurdish-specific teaching are the long pole

### Build the Kurmanji course and the tools editors need

Every engine improvement multiplies whatever content exists, and today that is a 43-word beginner course. Sequence the core by frequency where corpus data exist, weighted by editors' judgement because Kurmanji corpora are thin (**about 4 million words in Pewan against roughly 188 million for Sorani in AsoSoft**: [Pewan statistics](https://www.researchgate.net/figure/The-Pewan-Corpuss-Basic-Statistics_tbl1_270877570); [AsoSoft corpus](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/333827857_Toward_Kurdish_language_processing_Experiments_in_collecting_and_processing_the_AsoSoft_text_corpus_Digital_Scholarship_in_the_Humanities)), and by learners' real needs: family, home, food, greetings, introductions, Newroz. The draft roadmap assumed roughly 600–800 lemmas and chunks for A1 and about 1,500 by the end of A2; treat that as a planning assumption for editors to set. Teach greetings, fixed phrases and light-verb constructions as whole chunks, since studying collocations as units beat word-by-word study ([Boers and colleagues](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1174070.pdf)). Give the ezafe, oblique case, gender (taught through ezafe or demonstrative forms, because bare nouns hide it) and the past-tense ergative their own units, accepting attested variation, since Kurmanji children acquire ergativity from variable caretaker input that reflects change in progress ([UT Austin dissertation](https://tdl-ir.tdl.org/handle/2152/ETD-UT-2009-12-448)). Put listening and speaking items in every unit, keep some contrastive translation items for collocations and light verbs, and end each unit with a can-do statement and a small meaningful task such as leaving a voice message for a relative. Corpus frequency bands built from Pewan, OSCAR and community text, with editor weighting, should be an explicit work item, because sequencing, difficulty priors and level bands all depend on them. None of this can be authored at volume in the current admin, where lessons are edited as raw JSON opened by pasted UUID, new lessons can be created only under a known skill UUID, and only the JSON import creates courses, units and skills (`admin/src/pages/Content.tsx:195,214`). Per-type forms, a course/unit/skill browser, a grammar-note editor, the unused live preview (`admin/src/preview.ts`), audio and image upload reusing the alphabet studio's record-trim-normalise pipeline, lint checks (answer-position spread, diacritic variants, Hawar and Sorani script validators, missing audio, gloss or tags) and per-item statistics are what let Kurdish editors who are not engineers produce content.

### The 400k-word dictionary cannot gloss; a bilingual glossary must come first

Three separate fact-checks refuted the idea that Hevalo's dictionary can supply glosses, ground AI explanations or level texts. The roughly 447,000 Wîkîferheng entries **define Kurdish words in Kurdish, ship with zero entries seeded, and are mostly inflected forms and proper nouns** (`docs/admin/dictionary-import.md`; `shared/src/ferheng.ts:9`); English glosses exist only for **about 8,200 Kurmanji and 1,700 Sorani senses** from a separate English-Wiktionary import, and a competitor already advertises the same Wîkîferheng source. Both sources are CC BY-SA, so derived data must be shared alike. A curated learner glossary is therefore a prerequisite for saved-word review cards, graded reading, feed glosses, AI explanations and a child-safe word list. Each entry needs lemma, part of speech, gender, ezafe forms and key inflections; glosses in English, Turkish, German and Arabic first; an example sentence with audio; frequency rank; variety and script tags; a "cousin word" link to the other variety, seeded from the Sorani–Kurmanji–English [KurdishBLARK corpus](https://github.com/KurdishBLARK/InterdialectCorpus); an editor-reviewed flag and a child-safe flag. [KLPT](https://sinaahmadi.github.io/docs/articles/ahmadi2020klpt.pdf) can seed a lemmatiser that maps inflected forms to entries. The case for glosses is strong in direction: glossed reading taught 45.3% of target words immediately against 26.6% unglossed, multiple-choice glosses worked best, and first-language glosses beat target-language ones by about 4 points ([Yanagisawa, Webb & Uchihara 2020](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1251024)). One fact-check confirmed these figures from a logged summary and another could not, so treat them as snippet-level. The same meta-regression ranked hyperlinked, tap-to-reveal glosses only third ([meta-regression record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/336889327_How_do_Different_Forms_of_Glossing_Contribute_to_L2_Vocabulary_Learning_from_Reading_A_Meta-Regression_Analysis)), and learners often skip optional look-ups, so pre-teach key words and gloss them inline ("guess first" or in the margin), keeping tap-to-gloss as the fallback.

### Graded reading and listening are the bridge out of the beginner trap

Minority-language apps produce many starters and few independent users: Duolingo's Welsh course had more than 2 million starters when development paused in 2023 ([Senedd research brief](https://business.senedd.wales/documents/s150940/Research%20brief.html?CT=2)), and Māori revitalisation reports a low transition from basic learners to independent use ([Māori language revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_language_revival)). Graded input is the bridge. Extensive reading improves language against controls, though at d = 0.46–0.57 the effect is small by second-language benchmarks ([Nakanishi 2015](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/tesq.157); [Jeon & Day 2016](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1117026.pdf)). Narrative texts and texts written for learners beat expository and native texts for incidental vocabulary, and listening taught vocabulary at roughly the rate reading did (15% versus 17% of target words immediately, compared across studies, with small absolute gains) ([incidental-vocabulary meta-analysis](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/language-teaching/article/how-effective-is-second-language-incidental-vocabulary-learning-a-metaanalysis/E38E3468FD2090B1FA3051051DE8E70C)). Captions help, modestly: g = 0.56 equals **only about 3–4.5 percentage points more target words** ([Kurokawa et al. 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/lang.12697)). Build a curated, levelled shelf inside the existing library: short narratives (folk tales, family life, Newroz, diaspora life), each editor-corrected and narrated by a native speaker, with 2–4 comprehension questions, pre-taught key words, one-tap save with the sentence kept as context, captions on by default for beginners that fade to none, and uncaptioned listening checks. Karaoke-style, audio-paced highlighting is plausible but unproven: the only meta-analytic figure found for reading while listening could not be traced to its paper and measured comprehension rather than vocabulary, so A/B-test it against self-paced reading. A "readable for you" label can start at about 95% known words for glossed reading and 98% for fluency reading, but those thresholds come from English unassisted reading, comprehension rises gradually with coverage rather than at a cliff ([lexical coverage research](https://scholarspace.manoa.hawaii.edu/bitstreams/be187723-ba8b-433c-9472-b3b4ac847b86/download)), and Kurdish needs a lemmatiser before any coverage figure means anything. Supply is the risk. Licensed material can come from *Hano*, a Sorani and Kurmanji children's magazine launched in Sweden ([Rudaw](https://www.rudaw.net/english/culture/02072025)), bilingual picture books and Zarok TV's own productions; its dubbed cartoons are probably not licensable, and Turkey once closed the channel by decree ([VOA](https://voanews.com/a/turkey-shutters-popular-kurdish-tv-channel-for-children/3568433.html)), which is political exposure for a partner. Editor-selected community posts, lightly corrected and credited to their authors, can join the shelf; unedited learner posts should not enter levelling or review. The library already stores a language field that composers never set (`api/migrations/1751000072000_library-posts.js:18`), so exposing it and adding `ckb` and script values is the first step.

### Heritage literacy is a strong hypothesis, not yet a finding

The draft called reading and spelling the bottleneck of Hevalo's "largest audience". Neither half is established. The profile of strong listening and weak literacy comes from a US survey of about 1,800 college heritage learners of other languages ([Carreira & Kagan](https://hwpi.harvard.edu/files/heritagespanish/files/carreira_kagan_survey_0.pdf); unverified), heritage proficiency is a continuum that includes receptive-only "overhearers" ([Frontiers in Education review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2020.00053/pdf)), decades of bans "stunted linguistic development in each new generation" of Kurds ([CASLT](https://www.caslt.org/en/blog-discovering-languages-kurdish/)), and Hevalo has no data on who its learners are. The design survives as a hypothesis to size. Ask in onboarding which variety the learner wants, who speaks Kurdish around them and which variety, how well they understand, speak, read and write, and why they are learning; store languages rather than ethnicity, and keep the questions optional, since family-heritage answers can reveal ethnic origin for users in Turkey, Iran and Syria (counsel should confirm the GDPR treatment). Then size a "Read and write what you already speak" track on those answers and on placement data: sound–spelling mapping (ê/î/û against e/i/u, ç, ş, x, q; for Sorani the vowel letters, ڕ and ڵ), dictation in strict mode, read-aloud alongside native audio, short texts on familiar topics, and contrastive tips per interface language (ç and ş are familiar to Turkish readers, ê, î and û are not, and Turkish ı is not Kurmanji i). Heritage speakers score lower than classroom learners on explicit, metalinguistic tasks ([Montrul 2012, citing Bowles 2011](http://www.eurosla.org/Docs/Yearbook2012/Montrul.pdf)), so grammar for them should start from sentences they already say, and placement and checkpoints must not be built from rule questions. Identity and family motivate effort, but the "ideal self" construct predicted intended effort at r = .61 and achievement at only r = .20, about .10 after publication-bias correction ([Al-Hoorie 2018](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1202469.pdf); fact-checks disagree on whether this was independently retrieved), so identity framing needs plans and progress tracking behind it. Placement v2 should wait until there are tracks to route into. With at most 20 items, a Rasch adaptive test cannot reach a standard error of 0.4 logits even on one skill (each item contributes at most 0.25 information), and split across three skills each estimate stays 0.76–1.26 logits wide, so report one overall level with a visibly wide band, let per-skill estimates accumulate from practice, and draw several random items per level instead of today's single fixed one (`api/src/placement/service.ts:70-82`).

### Extend the alphabet, then give Sorani its own course

The web alphabet is Hevalo's best teaching module, and letters and phonics are where children's apps show their largest effects ([Kim et al. 2021](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1323865.pdf); first-language literacy apps). Extend it: store "Check yourself" results as review items with spaced rounds across days, port the module to mobile, replace the eSpeak clips with native recordings through the existing studio, add minimal pairs beyond the four that exist plus Sorani ڕ/ر and ڵ/ل, and unlock decodable mini-stories with audio after about 6–10 letters. Hawar is close to phonemic but **does not write Kurmanji's aspirated versus unaspirated stops (p/pʰ, t/tʰ, k/kʰ, ç/çʰ), a contrast absent or fading in some dialects** ([Kurdish phonology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_phonology); a reference-level source), so every letter and word needs native audio, and aspiration drills should wait until Kurdish linguists confirm and rank the contrasts, and must accept speakers whose dialect lacks them. An optional Sorani handwriting stage is a reasonable experiment, but the touchscreen version is untested. Sorani has zero courses today. Before building one, move the tested folding function into the shared text library and apply it in grading, keeping its deliberate ه→ھ mapping and adding zero-width non-joiner handling; published Arabic-script normalisers exist to learn from ([arXiv 2301.11406](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.11406)). Then build Sorani A1 with native audio and units on pronoun clitics, definiteness and the past-transitive clitic agent, Sorani's own ergative pattern, which needs units just as Kurmanji's does, keeping separate item banks and ability scales per variety. Offer Latin-script transliteration only as an opt-in aid after measuring its error rate: rule-based tools exist ([Ahmadi 2019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.10278)), but no accuracy figure was found. Sequence Sorani after the Kurmanji pipeline works, so a small editorial team is not split too early. Zazakî needs an explicit decision as well: 8,563 Turkish pupils chose the Zazakî elective for 2025–26 ([Kurdistan24](https://www.kurdistan24.net/en/story/909729/kurdish-language-demand-surges-in-t%C3%BCrkiyes-schools)), and the dictionary import already supports `zza`; the defensible call is variety tags now and no course until Kurmanji and Sorani are staffed. Finally, content must speak the learner's language. Course, unit and skill titles are `title_en` everywhere, and explanations in a learner's first language beat target-language-only explanations for vocabulary, especially for young learners ([Lee & Macaro 2013](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1540-4781.2013.12044.x); unverified). Translate prompts, hints, notes and titles into Turkish, German and Arabic first, letting families choose, since a diaspora child's strongest language may be the school language. A Persian interface would serve Iranian Kurds literate in Perso-Arabic script, but app-store and payment sanctions complicate distribution there; Swedish matters mainly for children and parents in Sweden.

## Kurdish speech and AI are ready for drills and drafting, not conversation

Kurdish speech and language technology moved quickly in 2025–26, but almost all of it is measured on native adult speech, and none has been evaluated on Kurdish learners. The table sets out what is realistic in October 2026.

| Capability | Kurmanji | Sorani | Verdict for Hevalo |
|---|---|---|---|
| Native recorded audio | Human recording | Human recording | Do now; it is the bar competitors already advertise |
| Perception drills ("which did you hear?") | Recordings only | Recordings only | Do now |
| Record-and-compare and shadowing | Recordings plus a microphone | Same | Do now, after the phone-audio fix |
| Recognising a known target word, adults | Fine-tuned Whisper about 10.5% WER in-domain and 28% on FLEURS-Kobani; Omnilingual ASR lists `kmr_Latn` but did worse on Badini | XLS-R about 10–12% WER; Google Chirp 3 `ckb-IQ` in preview; ElevenLabs speech-to-text (vendor claim) | Pilot only, after an in-house test of false accepts and false rejects |
| Sound-by-sound pronunciation scoring | No labelled learner data or published method | Same | Not feasible yet |
| Children's speech | No Kurdish child data; English child error rates several times adult | Same | No automatic scoring for children |
| Text-to-speech | Only confirmed MMS model is in Arabic script; licence probably non-commercial | Research F5 models report MOS 3.9–4.7 (single teams); ElevenLabs lists Sorani (vendor claim) | Listening volume only, labelled, after native raters approve |
| LLM-written Kurdish | No benchmark; Arabic and Turkish word leakage observed | One informal 5-prompt test | Offline drafting with human review |
| Real-time AI voice conversation | Not supported by mainstream APIs | Unresolved | Not now |

Native audio comes first. Kurdish competitors market native recordings for every word ([Dialect](https://www.mydialect.io/en/guides/best-app-to-learn-kurdish), vendor claim) and record-and-compare pronunciation ([uTalk, via a review](https://learnlanguagesfromhome.com/duolingo-kurdish/)). Record every course word, chunk and sentence; use several voices for core items across regional varieties (Serhed, Botan and Badînî for Kurmanji; Silêmanî and Hewlêr for Sorani), justified by dialect coverage and natural input rather than by phonetic-training research, which found no difference between 2 and 30 talkers ([Uchihara et al. 2025](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/high-variability-phonetic-training-hvpt-a-metaanalysis-of-l2-perceptual-training-studies/6ABB8C1F32D88D53EA8D05A4565E76F6)); store speaker, variety, consent and licence per clip; record "slow, careful" native takes rather than time-stretching; and keep open licences or model-training use behind a separate adult opt-in, never licensing children's voices openly.

Listening and perception drills are the best value in this area and should join the first wave: the same meta-analysis found large perception gains from perception training (g = 1.02, 0.71 after publication-bias adjustment; unverified), with small, unreliable transfer to production ([Uchihara et al. 2024](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/applied-psycholinguistics/article/does-perceptual-high-variability-phonetic-training-improve-l2-speech-production-a-metaanalysis-of-perceptionproduction-connection/E38D8F5CE65DC708137B0E95F97C6BC7)), and dictation improved sound recognition but not overall listening in one study ([Tsukuba](https://tsukuba.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/2007991/files/DA010616.pdf)), so pair it with meaning questions. The listening exercise type exists with a 0.75× option but has zero items; add listening items and audio-first steps to every unit, "which did you hear?" rounds and "Wordle by ear", and hold some voices back to test generalisation. Speaking practice without scoring is next: pronunciation teaching works best on specific sounds in controlled production, with unclear effects on spontaneous speech ([Saito & Plonsky 2019](https://experts.nau.edu/en/publications/effects-of-second-language-pronunciation-teaching-revisited-a-pro/)), and shadowing tentatively helps comprehensibility and fluency ([2025 systematic review](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/29984475.2025.2546827)). Record-and-compare, shadowing of sentences and dialogues, "say it to a family member" for heritage learners, and a unit-end voice message to a relative need only recordings and a working microphone.

Automatic pronunciation feedback is attractive but not ready for Kurdish. ASR-based training averaged g = 0.69 in English-learning studies, with explicit feedback ahead of indirect (0.86 versus 0.50) ([Ngo, Chen & Lai 2024](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/recall/article/effectiveness-of-automatic-speech-recognition-in-eslefl-pronunciation-a-metaanalysis/A915444CF252B61D14961D2FE733822D); core effect confirmed, confidence interval and moderator splits single-snippet). Whisper has no Kurdish out of the box; a Kurmanji fine-tune on about 68 hours reached 10.5% WER in-domain ([arXiv 2410.16330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16330)) but 28.11% on the 2026 FLEURS-Kobani benchmark ([arXiv 2603.29892](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.29892)); Sorani reached 10.0–11.8% ([arXiv 2406.02561](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.02561)); Meta's Apache-licensed Omnilingual ASR lists both varieties ([arXiv 2511.09690](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.09690)), but a FLEURS-Badini evaluation reported higher error on Badini ([ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.iwslt-1.14/)); and Google's Chirp 3 offers Sorani only in preview ([Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/speech-to-text/docs/models/chirp-3)). No Kurdish mispronunciation-detection paper or labelled learner dataset exists, and word error rate on native speech is the wrong metric anyway. Build a 1–2 hour evaluation set per variety with speaker-disjoint splits, consenting adult native speakers, learners and heritage speakers tagged by dialect, and deliberately mispronounced tokens; normalise scripts, since script mismatch alone produced error rates above 100% in one study ([arXiv 2608.16379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16379)); and measure false accepts and false rejects. Only if those are acceptable should Hevalo implement narrow "did you say the target word" checks: lenient, adults only, never withholding XP, streaks or progress, on the server first and on-device later ([react-native-sherpa-onnx](https://github.com/XDcobra/react-native-sherpa-onnx)). Zero-shot LLM audio models correlated 0.45 with human ratings at sentence level against 0.81 for a specialised model in English ([arXiv 2503.11229](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.11229)), so they should not score anything until they beat a baseline on Hevalo's own set. **Children's speech rules out automatic scoring for kids**: Whisper's error rate is about 25% on child speech against about 3% on similar adult speech ([Learning Agency](https://the-learning-agency.com/the-cutting-ed/article/how-speech-recognition-systems-struggle-with-childrens-voices/)), and 54% on classroom speech even after adaptation ([NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10520979)), all in English. Text-to-speech is fit only for listening volume (long-tail dictionary entries, community-post read-aloud, extra sentences), labelled "computer voice" as the alphabet already does, and only after about ten native raters per variety score it blind. Kurmanji options are thin, with the only confirmed MMS model in Arabic script ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-tts-kmr-script_arabic)); Sorani research models report high naturalness from single teams ([JSALT 2025 models](https://huggingface.co/aranemini/central-kurdish-tts)), and an audit found one public Sorani release had test recordings leaked into training ([arXiv 2609.11246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11246)). The worry that TTS cannot produce Kurmanji's unwritten aspiration is overstated, since end-to-end models learn word-specific pronunciations, but the risk is real for rare words; native audio stays the model for teaching items.

Large language models deserve the same caution. The only Kurdish comparison found was informal: three models on the same five Sorani prompts, where Gemini Flash rated "Excellent" and open-weight GPT-OSS looped and injected Arabic and Turkish words where Kurdish ones exist ([KurdEval](https://github.com/Lavanmawlood/KurdEval)). That is a signal, not a ranking, and says nothing about Kurmanji. The controlled AI-tutoring trials are weaker than their headlines. Unguarded GPT-4 raised high-school maths practice scores 48% but lowered exam scores 17% once access was removed, and a guarded tutor removed that harm ([Bastani et al., PNAS 2025](https://chibe.upenn.edu/publications/generative-ai-without-guardrails-can-harm-learning-evidence-from-high-school-mathematics)); the report that the guarded arm added no learning beyond the control is unverified. The Harvard physics tutor's "about twice the gains" was an immediate post-test whose size a re-analysis puts at 9–24% ([Kestin et al. 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/); [Clay Ford re-analysis](https://www.clayford.net/posts/ai_tutoring_nature/)); the Nigerian +0.31 SD was a whole facilitated after-school programme against no extra instruction ([World Bank](https://blogs.worldbank.org/en/developmenttalk/addressing-the-learning-crisis-with-generative-ai--lessons-from-)); and Tutor CoPilot assisted human tutors ([arXiv 2410.03017](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.03017v1)). Chatbot meta-analyses in language learning report g ≈ 0.58–0.61, small to medium by second-language benchmarks and inflated by 1–7-day studies ([Lyu 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/ijal.12668); [Li 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jcal.70060?af=R)). **No cited trial shows a durable gain from AI tutoring.**

The order of work follows from that. First build HevalBench: 200–400 items per variety covering grammar explanations, the precision of error diagnosis on real learner answers (including heritage and dialect forms that are correct), sentence generation within a word list, translation against machine-translation baselines, dialect and lexical purity, and script fidelity, scored by separate native raters per variety and re-run on every model release. Second, use AI offline to draft example sentences, distractors, rhyme sets and stories within known-word lists, with script and lexicon validators, native-editor approval, provenance labels and item statistics tracked by origin. Third, "why was this wrong?" explanations for adults and teens: the exercise engine decides correctness; the model explains in the learner's interface language from the item, the learner's answer, the unit's vetted grammar note and curated glossary entries; output is cached per item and normalised wrong answer, reviewed before display at launch, and reportable. For Kurdish-language interfaces (`ku`, `ckb`) that means LLM-written Kurdish prose, the highest-risk case, so restrict explanations to non-Kurdish interface languages until HevalBench passes for Kurdish output, and never send a child's free text or voice to a model. Text role-play bounded to a unit's scenario comes last, for adults, if HevalBench passes and counsel decides whether a named character falls under California's companion-chatbot law ([Jones Walker on SB 243](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/ai-regulatory-update-californias-sb-243-mandates-companion-ai-safety-and-accoun.html)). Check each provider's terms on minors before choosing a model; Gemini's API terms reportedly restrict services likely to be used by under-18s (unverified). Keep costs bounded by caching, per-user daily AI budgets, and small models only for classification tasks after validation.

## Rewards, community and children must serve learning, not attendance

### Pay for learning, forgive lapses, and stop the guilt

Gamification helps learning a little: across meta-analyses, cognitive g = 0.49, motivational 0.36 and behavioural 0.25, with only the cognitive effect holding in rigorous studies ([Sailer & Homner 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w); snippet-level). Its failure mode is documented: Duolingo learners fixate on streaks and XP and farm them ([Mogavi et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.16175)). Hevalo's economy rewards attendance and grinding. Zêr is paid for opening the app on a seven-day escalating cycle that resets after a missed day (`api/src/rewards/daily-cycle.ts`); a daily Wordle win alone keeps the streak; game XP counts toward goals and leagues; mistakes cost hearts that can fail a lesson; reminders are English-only and loss-framed ("Don't lose your streak!"). Expected rewards for merely doing a task lowered later free-choice motivation (engagement-contingent d = −0.40), while informational positive feedback raised it (+0.33) ([Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999](https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf)). That literature is contested ([Eisenberger, Pierce & Cameron rebuttal](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10589298)), and its safe reading is that paying for participation is risky while recognising mastery is not. So require one completed lesson or review for the daily Zêr claim and drop the reset; never pay Zêr for posts, likes or messages, or let it buy streak protection; give Zêr sinks a learning or cultural purpose (bonus readings, recorded poems, dialect audio, avatar items with Kurdish meaning); and enable goal rewards only for learning-based goals. XP should favour evidence of learning: diminishing returns on items already known well (lesson replays already pay 25%), a bonus for correct recall on due reviews, more for typed or spoken production than multiple choice, a daily cap on game XP toward goals and leagues, and credit for completed reading and listening. Award the dormant achievements for mastery milestones (every Hawar letter mastered, 100 words still known at a seven-day check), presented as informational surprises. Remove lesson-ending hearts: errors followed by feedback support learning ([Metcalfe 2017](https://www.columbia.edu/cu/psychology/metcalfe/PDFs/Learning%20from%20errorsAnnual%20ReviewMetcalfe2016.pdf)), and error-based limits push out first the learners who make the most mistakes.

Streaks should forgive. A broken streak lowers later engagement less when it can be repaired ([Silverman & Barasch 2023, CU Boulder summary](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions); unverified), habits took a median of about 66 days to reach peak automaticity with wide variation ([Lally et al. 2010](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/32898894_How_are_habits_formed_Modeling_habit_formation_in_the_real_world)), and persistent app learners paused more often but returned faster ([SSLA 2024 survival analysis](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/acceptance-and-engagement-patterns-of-mobileassisted-language-learning-among-nonconventional-adult-l2-learners-a-survival-analysis/C1186B329F808A11DDF2C748E77FD1EE)), so design for return rather than for never missing. Duolingo reports that loosening its streak raised engagement, including 40% more learners on seven-day streaks when one lesson sufficed ([summary of a Duolingo talk](https://www.getrecall.ai/summary/lennys-podcast/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks-or-jackson-shuttleworth-group-pm-retention-team)), but these are vendor engagement figures. Display total days learned and the longest streak beside the current one (both already computed), earn freezes through learning (one per five review sessions, say), add a 48-hour repair window by extra review and an optional weekly mode of four days in seven, rewrite the copy, and A/B-test a small comeback bonus as a cheap experiment rather than a proven win: the famous +27% was the best of 54 arms in a gym megastudy, with cash-convertible points, measured during the programme ([Milkman et al. 2021](https://penntoday.upenn.edu/news/wharton-study-best-ways-boost-workout-habits)). Leagues should get a one-tap opt-out now, then nearby ranks, a personal-best line, hidden relegation in lower tiers, and a personal-best mode when too few learners are active. Leaderboards lowered exam scores in one longitudinal quasi-experiment ([Learning and Individual Differences 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1041608024001651)), trait competitiveness changes who benefits ([Computers & Education 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360131524002100)), and Duolingo's +17% learning time from matched leagues is an ex-executive's engagement figure ([Mazal](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth)) whose often-repeated "about 30" cohort size has no source. Notifications are a small lever: Duolingo's reminder bandit added 0.5% DAU through better wording, not by sending reminders ([Yancey & Settles 2020](https://research.duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf)), and one tailored prompt made engagement about 3.9% more likely, unit unclear ([Bidargaddi et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.2196/10123)). Push delivery is a stub (`api/src/push/provider.ts:52`; `mobile/src/push/tokenSource.ts:20-22`). Implement it; add a learning category driven by due reviews with deep links to lessons and practice; ask permission after the first completed lesson; send at most one reminder a day at the learner's planned time, in all nine interface languages, with rotated templates; back off when ignored; and test what happens as reminders fade. An onboarding if-then plan ("After ___ I will do one Hevalo session") is a cheap prompt, but the often-quoted d = 0.65 comes from a 2006 meta-analysis via a secondary summary ([summary](https://goalsandprogress.com/implementation-intentions-gollwitzer-how-to/)), and newer estimates are probably smaller.

### Structure the community around cooperation and safety

Hevalo's user base is small and split by variety, level, time zone and age, so asynchronous formats come before anything that needs many people online at once. The server supports quiz 2v2, private rooms and tournaments that no client exposes; exposing them with relaxed timers, no elimination and end screens that show the top three and each player's improvement is cheap. Expect engagement more than learning: combining competition with collaboration was a significant moderator only for behavioural outcomes ([Sailer & Homner 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)). Groups currently sum weekly XP; replace that with cooperative learning goals counted from probe-checked items and ranked by average improvement per member, so small and large groups compete fairly. A weekly recap a learner can share with one chosen person (parent, teacher, mentor or buddy) fits the strongest accountability evidence: monitoring progress improved goal attainment (d = 0.40), more when progress was recorded or reported ([Harkin et al. 2016](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/91437/); mostly health studies). Share completed progress, not plans. The feed needs moderation before it needs features, because the automated classifier scores toxicity, hate and sexual content as zero (`docs/security/ai-moderation.md`). Pre-moderate new accounts' first posts; show short rules before a first post (in one large field experiment this raised newcomer compliance by more than 8 points and participation by 70%: [Matias 2019](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1813486116), not re-checked); answer first accepted posts with a human welcome; explain rejections with a fix-and-resubmit path, since aggressive rejection drove good-faith newcomers from Wikipedia ([Halfaker et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1177/0002764212469365), not re-checked); recruit moderators for Kurmancî, Soranî and the main comment languages; have counsel review a political-symbols policy; and protect users in Turkey, Iran and Syria by default with no real names, no location and easy deletion. Turning the feed into a writing space rates Weak and comes later: unit-linked writing prompts, the key bar in composers, and an opt-in "correct my Kurdish" flag with structured corrections anchored to the curated glossary. Trained peers raised accuracy in classroom studies ([Sato & Lyster 2012](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0272263112000356); not re-checked), but heritage speakers fear correction by "real" speakers ([Sevinç & Dewaele 2018](https://doi.org/10.1177/1367006916661635)), so dialect respect must be a rule. Learning by teaching ("explain it to a friend", a child teaching a parent three words) is a near-free experiment ([Fiorella & Mayer 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cedpsych.2013.06.001); not re-checked). Fluent-speaker rooms and mentors are the eventual bridge to real conversation but need volunteers, vetting and safeguarding, so pilot them late and for adults only.

### Protect minors now; build a separate kids' mode later

No client collects an age, so Hevalo's exposure to minors is unknown. Some safety facts are better than the draft assumed: DMs and gifts are already friends-only, matchmaking has no free-text chat, and uploaded images are stripped of metadata (`api/src/chat/service.ts:87-88`; `api/src/shop/routes.ts:129-132`; `api/src/media/imageProcess.ts:63,77`). The real exposure is elsewhere: friend requests and suggestions from strangers, open groups with group chat (`api/src/groups/service.ts:12,57`), profiles public by default (`api/migrations/1751000045000_user-search.js:11`), the public feed and comments, and minors' stored voice notes and voice comments. `restricted_mode` is set only for under-16s, only at email registration, is never recomputed as users age and is never read, and Google and Apple sign-up skip age entirely (`api/src/gdpr/consent.ts:9`; `api/src/auth/service.ts:357-362`; `api/src/auth/oauth.ts:160`). The interim protections are mostly default flips, small to medium effort. Put a neutral age screen on every sign-up path (birth month and year, no default, no hint that older users get more), store an age band, and derive minor status at request time. Route under-13s, or users below their country's consent age where consent is the legal basis (16 in Germany, the Netherlands and Ireland, 15 in France, 14 in Spain and Austria, 13 in Sweden according to one aggregator: [Didomi](https://www.didomi.io/blog/privacy-laws-underage-consumers)), to a polite stop page and a parent waitlist until parent-managed profiles exist. For under-18s, make profiles private, block stranger friend requests and suggestions and open-group chat, pre-moderate or disable public posting, turn streaks and push off unless opted in, drop loss-framed reminders and time-limited shop offers, put a parental gate before any Gems purchase, scrub minors' request data in error reporting (Sentry, `api/package.json:30`), decide what happens to stored child voice notes, and add break nudges. Use 18 for these engagement defaults and 13–16 only for consent.

The legal basis is real but should not be overstated. The amended US COPPA rule has applied since 22 April 2026 ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-finalizes-changes-childrens-privacy-rule-limiting-companies-ability-monetize-kids-data)); a service becomes "mixed audience" only if it is directed to children, which launching a kids' mode would do ([Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/coppas-coming-of-age-key-compliance-changes-in-ftcs-final-rule)), while a general-audience app is covered once it knows a user is under 13; text-message parental consent is allowed only when no child data goes to third parties ([PRIVO](https://www.privo.com/blog/understanding-new-coppa-amendments)); and stored recordings of a child's voice need parental consent. These facts are consistent across one research agent's law-firm sources; counsel should confirm them. The EU's July 2025 guidelines call for private-by-default accounts and for messaging streaks and push notifications to be off by default for minors ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors)), but they are non-binding, name messaging rather than learning streaks, and may not bind a company of Hevalo's size. The UK Children's Code's final nudge standard concerns privacy and data while encouraging nudges toward breaks ([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/advice-and-services/audits/data-protection-audit-framework/toolkits/age-appropriate-design/nudge-techniques/)), and Ofcom's codes say adult strangers should not be able to contact children by default ([Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/statement-protecting-children-from-harms-online)). Treat all of this as good practice now and ask counsel to settle scope.

A kids' mode should not be the adult app with a cartoon skin, and it should wait for a full compliance build: parent-owned family accounts with nickname-and-avatar child profiles, verifiable parental consent, social contact limited to family circles and teacher classes with auto-generated Kurdish nicknames and preset phrases, no public feed, a child-filtered dictionary, a parental gate before the shop, Gems and external links, no third-party SDKs in child sessions, no language model ever receiving a child's free text or voice, a written security programme and retention schedule, a data-protection impact assessment, and app-store age signals where Texas- and Utah-style laws require them ([FPF comparison](https://fpf.org/blog/comparing-enacted-app-store-accountability-acts/)). Keep the main app out of Apple's Kids Category, as `DEPLOY.md:172-175` already does, but build kids' mode to that category's rules, including a parental gate before purchases and links ([guideline summary](https://www.goodbarber.com/help/apple-rejection-r100/apple-rejection-apps-for-kids-a365/)). Pedagogy follows age bands. For ages 3–5 everything is spoken: picture choices, songs and *lorî*, action games where a character says *Destên xwe bilind bike!* and a parent confirms, and narrated stories with matching animation but no hotspots, games or dictionary pop-ups, which distracted children in storybook studies, especially less-advantaged ones ([Takacs, Swart & Bus 2015](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.3102/0034654314566989)). For ages 6–8 the alphabet and phonics are the core, with composited narration in the style of Duolingo ABC ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/a-good-read-building-duolingo-abc-for-android/)) and decodable stories; for 9–12, the existing games in private rooms and very short explicit grammar tips, supported by a study of 8–9-year-olds that found the type of instruction, not age alone, shapes how children learn ([Lichtman 2016](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/457C069A5339D656788E7E8D217B2A3A/S0305000915000598a.pdf/age-and-learning-environment-are-children-implicit-second-language-learners.pdf)). Each session ends on a "done for today" screen with an offline mission ("Ask Dayê what this is called"). Children's apps average **+0.31 SD in rigorous studies, more for letters and phonics**, but those are first-language literacy and maths apps ([Kim et al. 2021](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1323865.pdf)). Reshape motivation rather than removing it: gamification still raised primary pupils' motivation modestly (g = 0.31; [Kurnaz & Koçtürk 2025](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/pits.70056); unverified), but tangible rewards undermined children's motivation more than adults' and praise lifted it less ([Deci et al. 1999](https://depts.washington.edu/techdocs/papers/deciExtrinsicRewardsAndIntrinsicMotivation99.pdf)), and **80% of apps used by 3–5-year-olds contained manipulative design**, with stickers, trophies and accumulating daily rewards coded as lures ([Radesky et al. 2022](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2793493)). So: no streak-loss messages, leagues, daily-login rewards, countdowns, pleading characters, loot boxes or hearts; cosmetics earned through learning milestones and shown as surprises; cooperative family or class goals. Pre-readers get words recycled across songs, stories and games on a schedule the app controls, and children from about age 7 get short, low-stakes spaced-review games. Score every activity on the Four Pillars (active, engaged, meaningful, socially interactive) before release ([Hirsh-Pasek et al. 2015](https://kathyhirshpasek.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2015/09/Psychological-Science-in-the-Public-Interest-2015-Hirsh-Pasek-3-34.pdf); [Meyer et al. 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35282402/)), measure letter–sound accuracy and picture naming a week later, and never optimise children's time in the app.

Families and classes are where the evidence is thinnest and the potential largest. Parent co-learning ("Kurdish to say to your child", shared-reading prompts such as *Ev çi ye?*) is well motivated, but caregiver book-sharing programmes produced small child effects (expressive d = 0.41, receptive 0.26) after in-person training, with the large effect (d = 1.01) on the caregivers' own behaviour ([Dowdall et al. 2020](https://hira.hope.ac.uk/id/eprint/3756/)); shared reading showed no effect against active controls ([Noble et al. 2019](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3054189/)); and the link between parents' language use and children's minority-language use is correlational ([De Houwer 2007](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/applied-psycholinguistics/article/abs/parental-language-input-patterns-and-childrens-bilingual-use/821A5852222197491F4E7ABC8AA4B099)). Expect small, dose-dependent effects, and A/B-test prompts against an unprompted read-along. A family circle with audio-first "Ask Dapîr" voice cards serves identity and family ties, but learning from live video chat has been shown only for toddlers in small lab studies ([Roseberry et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3962808)), so treat it as a bonding and motivation feature, with each recorded adult's consent. A parent digest should show what was learned rather than minutes, and the child should see a clear sign that a parent can see progress ([ICO Children's Code](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/)). Teacher and class mode is the safe route to peer play for children and a plausible distribution channel: Turkey's elective drew **50,809 Kurmancî course selections for 2025–26, while only 10 of 20,000 new teaching posts in 2024 went to Kurdish** ([Turkish Minute](https://www.turkishminute.com/2025/06/03/kurdish-elective-course-enrollment-hits-record-high-in-turkey-as-barriers-remain/); [Kurdistan24](https://www.kurdistan24.net/en/story/909729/kurdish-language-demand-surges-in-t%C3%BCrkiyes-schools)). But those are selections rather than enrolments, the pupils are minors in state schools, the elective has an official curriculum ([IMPACT-se](https://www.impact-se.org/wp-content/uploads/Two-Languages-One-Country-Turkeys-Elective-Kurdish-Curriculum.pdf)), and classroom use would likely need ministry approval. Pilot with two or three diaspora weekend schools first.

## Measure delayed recall, fit the plan to capacity, and price the people

Hevalo's north star should be **delayed, unassisted recall of sampled items at 7 and 30 days**, with no hints or audio replay and production wherever possible, reported per cohort, segment and feature. Supporting metrics are items still known per hour studied, can-do checkpoints passed on delayed checks, letter–sound accuracy and picture naming for children, native-rated intelligibility of sampled recordings for speaking, scheduler calibration against the moving-average baseline, and content quality from learning curves per knowledge component, item difficulty, distractor choice rates and answer-position bias. Engagement (DAU, retention, minutes, streaks) and wellbeing (late-night sessions by minors, share of XP from farmable repeats, league and notification opt-outs, a short streak-stress pulse) are guardrails: a mechanic that lifts engagement but worsens them does not ship. Probe design matters. Draw probes at random from all learned items, scheduled or not, at times the scheduler did not choose; withholding items from review measures only forgetting without review. Log each probe as a review, because testing changes memory; withhold at most about 5% of items to estimate forgetting curves, and disclose it; blend probes unmarked into reviews and keep them out of XP and leagues. Re-placement growth is meaningful only as a cohort average, since an individual's difference between two 20-item placements is noise unless it exceeds roughly 1.3–2.1 logits.

Experiments must respect Hevalo's scale. A two-arm user-level test needs **about 393 learners per arm to detect d = 0.2 and about 1,570 for d = 0.1** (arithmetic confirmed), and realistic effects are small: large education trials average about 0.06 SD ([Lortie-Forgues & Inglis 2019](https://doi.org/10.3102/0013189X19832850); not re-checked). Run item-level, within-learner randomisation first wherever a feature acts on single items (re-queueing misses, feedback type, audio-first introduction, gloss type), run user-level tests of streaks, XP, leagues and notifications one at a time or label them as monitoring, use pre-period covariates to cut variance ([Deng et al. 2013](https://doi.org/10.1145/2433396.2433413); not re-checked), pre-register one primary learning metric, and run motivation mechanics for 8–12 weeks, a sensible minimum rather than an evidence-based window. Test only variants that are acceptable teaching on their own, never manipulate mood, never optimise children's time in the app, tell parents about learning experiments, publish a plain-language "how we improve Hevalo" notice, and complete a data-protection impact assessment. Before claiming levels, validate externally: 50–100 consenting adults rated by Kurdish teachers, at least two raters with reported agreement, correlated with in-app placement, as Duolingo did against standardised tests, though its studies were vendor-authored and tested completers only ([Jiang et al. 2021](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/flan.12600)). Write per-unit can-do statements adapted from the CEFR's descriptors, including its young-learner collections ([Council of Europe](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages)), say "aligned to CEFR descriptors" rather than "certified", acknowledge Turkey's official Kurmancî curriculum, and call the result the first openly published Kurdish can-do framework "that we know of", since the search for existing ones failed rather than came back empty.

Capacity forces choices. The draft's first quarter held roughly 45–70 engineer-weeks of engine work against 36–60 available, assuming 3–5 engineers, while also claiming content was the long pole. Defer placement v2 until there are tracks to place into, keeping only the onboarding questions, goal and age screen; author new units to the lesson-v2 structure instead of retrofitting the engine; limit leagues to a one-tap opt-out for now; promote listening and perception drills, which need no speech recognition, into the first wave; and protect the capacity of the course, authoring tools and recording drive.

Cost is the gap the research never filled: no note priced anything. Before the month-3 gate, price voice talent (3–5 voices across the A1–A2 core plus sentences implies thousands of clips), a paid core of Kurmancî and Soranî editors, legal review, native raters for HevalBench, text-to-speech checks (about ten per variety) and the speech evaluation set (1–2 hours per variety), moderators by variety, model calls (cached per item and wrong answer) and speech-model hosting. Volunteer-only pipelines are fragile: Duolingo ended its volunteer course-contributor programme in 2021 ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/ending-honoring-our-volunteer-contributor-program-2/)) and paused Welsh in 2023, so pair paid editors with moderated, credited community contributions (sentences, variants, recordings validated by two votes), with explicit consent and a licence per contribution and adults only.

Revenue plumbing already exists (Gems in-app purchase, a `users.premium_until` field, premium-only and time-limited shop items: `api/migrations/1751000089000_cosmetics.js:13,31`; `api/src/shop/routes.ts:14-26`). Charge for convenience (offline packs, ad-free use), for features that cost money to serve (AI explanations beyond a free quota, human corrections, live classes), for school and diaspora-organisation licences and family plans, and consider diaspora patronage. Never sell hearts or energy, never limit review or alphabet practice, and if a free tier ever needs a limit, cap new units per day. The market leader's own 2026 strategy has turned toward "teaching better" and a stronger free experience ([Duolingo Q2 2026 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001562088/000162828026053299/q2fy26duolingo6-30x26share.htm)), which raises the bar for any limiter on the core loop.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "what should Hevalo add?" to "in what order, and how will we know?" Most of the menu (spaced retrieval, feedback, graded input, forgiving streaks, kids' modes, AI explanations) is familiar from larger apps, and the evidence behind each item is modest once the fact-checks are applied. What is distinctive is the sequence the evidence implies for a small, minority-language platform: make the loop reachable and honest, move memory onto words, then let content, native audio and a real glossary carry the gains, with every feature judged by whether Kurdish is still remembered a month later. The binding constraint is human rather than technical: editors, voices, raters, moderators and counsel. In 2026, AI can amplify those people by drafting, triaging and explaining in high-resource interface languages, but it cannot replace them for Kurdish, and speech recognition is a research project for Kurmanji and for children.

Two strategic openings follow. First, no Kurdish app publishes outcome data, and the market leader's published wins are mostly engagement figures, so a Hevalo that measures delayed recall, validates levels with teachers and publishes the results would own something competitors cannot copy quickly; it is also the only way to turn this report's many hypotheses into evidence. Second, the biggest unknown is the audience itself: whether Hevalo's learners are mostly heritage speakers who need literacy, receptive-only learners who need oral input, or newcomers starting from zero. The onboarding questions and placement data planned for the first quarter are the cheapest research Hevalo can do, and they should decide how much of the heritage, family and Sorani roadmap gets built and when.
